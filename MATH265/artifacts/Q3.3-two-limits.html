MATH 265Final Exam 2Question 18 points

Limits at the Edges

Two limits that look unrelated are the same question asked twice. One asks what the graph settles down to far away to the right. The other asks what it does as it runs into a wall. Answer both by naming the edge.

The question, verbatim

Evaluate the following limits, no credit will be given for unjustified answers. If a limit does not exist explain why.

a. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty}\left(\frac{3x^2 - 4}{4 + 2x + 2x^4}\right)\sin(x)\)
b. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 5\pi/2}\tan x\)

Part (a) · the answer

\(= 0\)  FINITE

The oscillating factor is trapped between ±1, and the fraction it multiplies goes to zero. Squeeze Theorem. Type 0.

Part (b) · the answer

does not exist  DNE

Left side +∞, right side −∞. They disagree, so you may not write “\(=\infty\)”. The reason is the answer.

Section 00

The thirty-second version

If you remember nothing else, remember these two chains. Neither needs a calculator and neither needs L’Hôpital.

(a)  lim (3x^2-4)/(2x^4+2x+4) * sin(x)      as x -> inf

  -> sin(x) has NO limit, so the product law is ILLEGAL here
  -> but |sin(x)| <= 1 always, so sin(x) is BOUNDED

  -> bounded x vanishing = SQUEEZE
     -R(x)  <=  R(x)sin(x)  <=  R(x),   and both ends -> 0
  ANSWER   0
(b)  lim tan(x)                             as x -> 5pi/2

  ANSWER   does not exist   (+inf from the left, -inf from the right)
The mechanism

What each part looks like

Two pictures worth more than the algebra. The left one is a squeeze: the wavy line cannot escape the two dashed envelopes, and the envelopes are closing on zero, so the wave has nowhere to go but zero. The right one is a wall: the curve runs up one side of it and comes from below on the other, which is exactly why there is no single answer.

Section 01

What this question is really testing

This is question 1 of Final Exam 2 and it carries 8 points for two limits, so 4 points each. That is the highest per-limit value anywhere on the four sample papers: Q3.1 spreads its allocation over five limits and Q3.2 over six. The examiner has picked the two limits that cannot be done by substituting and factoring, then written no credit will be given for unjustified answers across the top.

The single insight that turns two problems into one: each part is an asymptote question.

That is why the same paper also asks Q4.1, “find the vertical and horizontal asymptotes”. Q4.1 is this question with the wrapper taken off. If you can do Q3.3 you can do Q4.1 in one line, and the reverse holds too.

Section 02

The five things you must have cold

None of these can be derived under time pressure. They have to be recall.

2.1  The degree rule for a rational function as \(x \to \pm\infty\)

Compare only the leading terms. Everything else is noise.

CaseLimitExample
top degree < bottom degree0 \(\dfrac{3x^2-4}{2x^4+2x+4} \to 0\)
top degree = bottom degreeratio of leading coefficients \(\dfrac{3x^2+1}{2x^2-5} \to \dfrac32\)
top degree > bottom degree\(\pm\infty\), sign from the leading coefficients \(\dfrac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2} \to -\infty\)

The mechanism, if you ever have to show it, is to divide top and bottom by the highest power in the bottom:

$$\frac{3x^2-4}{2x^4+2x+4} = \frac{\dfrac{3}{x^2}-\dfrac{4}{x^4}}{2+\dfrac{2}{x^3}+\dfrac{4}{x^4}} \longrightarrow \frac{0-0}{2+0+0} = 0$$

The rate is worth knowing as well: this one shrinks like \(\tfrac32 x^{-2}\). Verified, \(x^2R(x) \to 3/2\).

2.2  The Squeeze Theorem, in the only shape this course asks for

If \(g \le f \le h\) near the point of interest and \(g\) and \(h\) both tend to the same \(L\), then \(f\) tends to \(L\) too. The corollary is what you actually use:

$$\textbf{bounded} \ \times \ \textbf{something tending to } 0 \ = \ 0$$

If \(\lvert u(x)\rvert \le M\) for all \(x\) and \(v(x) \to 0\), then \(-M\lvert v\rvert \le uv \le M\lvert v\rvert\), and both outer expressions go to zero.

Bounded is load-bearing

It is not enough for the other factor to merely “not blow up fast”. Verified contrast: \(R(x)\cdot x^3 \to \infty\) even though \(R(x) \to 0\), because \(x^3\) is not bounded.

2.4  “Does not exist” versus “\(=\infty\)”, which are different answers

Strictly, \(\lim = +\infty\) already means the limit does not exist as a real number. But it says more than “does not exist”: it says the function grows without bound in one definite direction. Give the most informative true statement.

2.5  The two facts about \(\sin\) that this question turns on

$$\lvert \sin x\rvert \le 1 \ \text{ for every real } x \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lim_{x\to\infty}\sin x \ \text{ does not exist.}$$

Both are used in part (a) and they pull in opposite directions. The second forbids the product law. The first rescues the problem anyway. Verified: sampling \(\sin\) across \(x\in[1000,1007]\) fills \([-1,1]\), so no limit exists.

The most common error on this whole topic

Treating \(\tfrac{k}{0}\) as if it were \(\tfrac00\) and hunting for a factor to cancel. There is nothing to cancel. \(\tfrac{k}{0}\) with \(k \ne 0\) is always an infinite one-sided behaviour, and the only remaining question is the sign on each side.

Section 04

Q3.3 worked several ways

Part (a), Route A — squeeze, the intended route

Name the fraction so you can talk about it:

$$R(x) = \frac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}, \qquad \text{so the question is } \lim_{x\to\infty} R(x)\sin x.$$
  1. \(R(x) \to 0\) by the degree rule: top degree 2, bottom degree 4.
  2. For \(x > 2/\sqrt3 \approx 1.1547\) the numerator is positive and the denominator is positive, so \(R(x) > 0\). Since \(x\to\infty\), we may assume \(x\) is past that point.
  3. Because \(-1 \le \sin x \le 1\) and \(R(x) > 0\), multiplying through keeps the inequalities: \(-R(x) \le R(x)\sin x \le R(x)\).
  4. Both outer functions tend to \(0\), so by the Squeeze Theorem the middle one does too.
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\frac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}\right)\sin x = 0$$
Verified three ways

The inequality \(\lvert R\sin\rvert \le R\) holds on 4000 samples across \([10,1490]\) with zero violations; the supremum of \(\lvert R\sin\rvert\) over a full period shrinks like \(1.5/x^2\); and the same conclusion holds as \(x \to -\infty\).

Part (a), Route B — the product law, which is illegal here

Worth writing out once, because it is the route most students take and it is not an arithmetic slip, it is an invalid step.

$$\lim (R(x)\sin x) \overset{?}{=} \left(\lim R(x)\right)\cdot\left(\lim \sin x\right) = 0 \cdot \left(\lim_{x\to\infty}\sin x\right)$$

The product law requires both limits to exist. \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\sin x\) does not exist, so the law does not apply and that line is not mathematics.

Why this one is dangerous

It reaches the right answer, which is exactly the problem. It will be marked wrong on a paper that says “no credit for unjustified answers”, and the identical reasoning gives the wrong answer on the Family 3 variation, where the fraction tends to \(3/2\) instead of \(0\). You may write “\(0 \times \text{bounded} = 0\)” as shorthand for the squeeze. You may not write “\(0 \times \lim \sin x\)”.

Part (a), Route C — L’Hôpital, which does not apply

Substituting gives \(0 \cdot (\text{no limit})\), which is neither \(\tfrac00\) nor \(\tfrac\infty\infty\), so there is no indeterminate quotient to work on. Forcing it into \(\dfrac{\sin x}{1/R(x)}\) does not help: the top still has no limit, and differentiating gives \(\dfrac{\cos x}{(1/R)'}\), which oscillates forever.

Part (a), Route D — bound first, then compare

Since \(\lvert\sin x\rvert \le 1\),

$$0 \le \left\lvert R(x)\sin x \right\rvert \le \lvert R(x)\rvert = R(x) \longrightarrow 0,$$

so \(\lvert R\sin\rvert \to 0\), and a quantity whose absolute value tends to zero tends to zero. Same squeeze with the modulus taken first, one line shorter.

Part (b), Route A — rewrite as \(\sin/\cos\) and test the sides

  1. Is \(5\pi/2\) actually an asymptote? \(\tan\) blows up where \(\cos = 0\), at \(x = \tfrac\pi2 + k\pi\). Setting \(\tfrac\pi2+k\pi = \tfrac{5\pi}{2}\) gives \(k=2\). Yes.
  2. Substitute to see the shape. \(\tan\tfrac{5\pi}{2} = \dfrac{\sin(5\pi/2)}{\cos(5\pi/2)} = \dfrac{1}{0}\). That is \(k/0\) with \(k = 1 \ne 0\), a pole, not an indeterminate form. Nothing to factor.
  3. Test each side by asking only about the sign of \(\cos\). Just to the left, the angle is coterminal with just under \(\pi/2\), where \(\cos\) is small and positive, so \(\tan \to +\infty\). Just to the right, \(\cos\) is small and negative, so \(\tan \to -\infty\).
  4. Conclude. The one-sided limits differ in sign, so the two-sided limit does not exist and cannot be written as \(\pm\infty\).
$$\lim_{x\to 5\pi/2^{-}}\tan x = +\infty,\qquad \lim_{x\to 5\pi/2^{+}}\tan x = -\infty, \qquad \lim_{x\to 5\pi/2}\tan x \ \text{DNE}$$
Verified

At \(h = 10^{-3}, 10^{-5}, 10^{-7}\), and the size checked too: \(\tan(5\pi/2 - h) \approx +1/h\) and \(\tan(5\pi/2+h)\approx -1/h\).

Part (b), Route B — reduce to a coterminal angle first

$$\frac{5\pi}{2} = 450^\circ = 360^\circ + 90^\circ, \quad\text{so } \frac{5\pi}{2} \text{ is coterminal with } \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

\(\tan\) has period \(\pi\), so the behaviour near \(5\pi/2\) is an exact copy of the behaviour near \(\pi/2\), and you already know that picture: it climbs to \(+\infty\) on the left and comes up from \(-\infty\) on the right. Done, in two lines.

Part (b), Route C — the monotonicity argument

\(\tan' = \sec^2 x > 0\) wherever it is defined, so \(\tan\) increases on each branch. An increasing unbounded function on \(\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{2}, \tfrac{5\pi}{2}\right)\) must run to \(+\infty\) at the right end of that interval. Correct, but slower, and easy to fumble which branch you are on. Use it only as a check.

Section 05

The exam-ready write-up

These are the sentences that earn the method marks. Learn the shape, not the words.

Part (a), four sentences

The factor \(\dfrac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}\) has numerator of degree 2 and denominator of degree 4, so it tends to \(0\) as \(x\to\infty\). The factor \(\sin x\) has no limit as \(x\to\infty\), so the product rule for limits cannot be applied. However \(\lvert\sin x\rvert\le 1\) for all \(x\), so \(\left\lvert\dfrac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}\sin x\right\rvert \le \dfrac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}\) for \(x > 2/\sqrt3\), and the right-hand side tends to \(0\). By the Squeeze Theorem the limit is \(0\).

Sentence two is the one worth marks and the one everybody omits. It is the sentence that shows you knew the easy route was illegal.

Part (b), four sentences

\(\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\) is undefined where \(\cos x = 0\), that is at \(x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}+k\pi\), and \(\dfrac{5\pi}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}+2\pi\) is such a point. At \(x = \dfrac{5\pi}{2}\) we have \(\sin x = 1\) and \(\cos x = 0\), so this is of the form \(1/0\) and not an indeterminate form. As \(x \to \dfrac{5\pi}{2}^{-}\), \(\cos x \to 0^{+}\), so \(\tan x \to +\infty\); as \(x \to \dfrac{5\pi}{2}^{+}\), \(\cos x \to 0^{-}\), so \(\tan x \to -\infty\). The one-sided limits are different, so the limit does not exist; \(x = \dfrac{5\pi}{2}\) is a vertical asymptote.

Sentence four is the “explain why” the question explicitly asked for. Note that it does not say \(=\infty\), which would be a false claim about both sides.

Section 06

Verify before you commit

Eight seconds each, and between them they catch nearly every error available here.

Part (a) · the magnitude check

At \(x = 100\), \(R(x) \approx 1.5/100^2 = 1.5\times10^{-4}\), and \(\sin\) can only scale that by at most \(1\). So the expression is already under \(0.0002\) at \(x=100\) and keeps shrinking like \(1/x^2\). An answer of \(0\) is plausible; \(3/2\) or \(\infty\) is not.

Part (a) · two cheap invariants

  1. A bounded factor can never change the limit of a vanishing factor. If your answer is non-zero you have treated the oscillating factor as if it grew. It cannot: it lives in \([-1,1]\).
  2. Degrees. \(2 < 4\), so the fraction heads to \(0\), not to a ratio. If you wrote \(3/2\) you compared the wrong pair of terms, which is the Family 3 answer.

Part (b) · the sign check, done independently

Do not re-derive it. Picture the graph of \(\tan\) near any asymptote: up on the left, up from below on the right. Now check that your algebra agrees. Two independent routes to the same sign is what makes it a check rather than a restatement.

Part (b) · is it even an asymptote?

Before any sign work, confirm \(\cos\) is genuinely \(0\) there. Verified trap: at \(x = 2\pi\) and \(x = 3\pi\), \(\tan\) is perfectly well behaved and the answer is simply \(0\). A number containing \(\pi\) is not automatically an asymptote.

Worth memorising outright

\(\dfrac{5\pi}{2} = 450^\circ \equiv 90^\circ\). If you can say “450 degrees is 90 degrees” without hesitating, part (b) takes twenty seconds.

\(\csc x\) at \(2\pi\)−∞ +∞ DNE flipped back

Read as structure, and count the distinct behaviours: there are only two. Either it is \(+\infty\) then \(-\infty\), or the reverse. The whole table is generated by two rules: \(\tan\) and \(\cot\) never change (period \(\pi\)), while \(\sec\) and \(\csc\) alternate at consecutive asymptotes (period \(2\pi\)).

Section 09

The trap list

Ranked by how often each one actually costs marks on this question.

  1. Using the product law on part (a). \(\lim \sin x\) does not exist, so the law does not apply. Right number, no marks, and the identical step gives the wrong answer on Family 3.
  2. Answering \(+\infty\) for part (b). The two sides disagree in sign, so no single infinite value is correct.
  3. Not testing both sides at all. Once you reach \(k/0\), the sign work is the question.
  4. Treating \(k/0\) as \(0/0\) and hunting for a factor. There is nothing to cancel and the time is gone.
  5. Assuming a squared denominator forces \(+\infty\). Q3.1b squares \((x+2)\) and the answer is still DNE.
  6. Assuming any \(\pi\)-flavoured number is an asymptote. \(\tan\) at \(2\pi\) is \(0\); \(\cot\) at \(5\pi/2\) is \(0\).
  7. Applying \(\tan\)’s period to \(\sec\). Period \(\pi\) versus \(2\pi\) is the whole difference.
  8. Forgetting \(\sqrt{x^2}=\lvert x\rvert\) at \(-\infty\), which silently flips every answer in Family 6.
  9. Comparing the wrong terms in the degree rule. For part (a) that is \(3x^2\) against \(2x^4\), not \((3-4)\) against \((2+2+4)\).
  10. Reaching for L’Hôpital. Part (a) is \(0\times\text{bounded}\) and part (b) is \(1/0\). The rule applies to neither.
  11. Writing “DNE” with no reason on a paper that says in bold that unjustified answers score zero.

Section 11

The one-page decision tree

Evaluate a limit.
|
+-- Where is x going?
|
+== TO INFINITY (or minus infinity) =========================================
|   |
|   +-- Is there an OSCILLATING factor (sin, cos of anything)?
|   |     YES -> what does the OTHER factor do?
|   |            -> 0        : SQUEEZE. answer 0.           [F1]
|   |            -> L != 0   : DNE, oscillates in [-L, L].  [F3]
|   |            -> infinity : DNE, unbounded oscillation.  [F3]
|   |     NO  -> continue
|   |
|   +-- Any SQUARE ROOT?
|   |     YES -> difference? multiply by the CONJUGATE.     [F6]
|   |            quotient?   divide by |x|, and remember
|   |                        sqrt(x^2) = |x| = -x when x<0.  [F6]
|   |
|   +-- Otherwise it is RATIONAL: compare DEGREES.          [F0]
|         top < bottom -> 0
|         top = bottom -> ratio of LEADING coefficients
|         top > bottom -> +-infinity, sign from leading coefficients
|         then, if x -> -infinity, re-check every ODD power
|         for a sign flip.                                  [F7]
|
+== TO A FINITE POINT a =====================================================
    |
    +-- SUBSTITUTE first. What came out?
    |
    +-- a number          -> that is the answer. Done.
    |
    +-- 0/0               -> factor, conjugate, common denominator,
    |                        trig identity. Then substitute again.
    |                        If it all cancels it is a HOLE,
    |                        not an asymptote.               [F5]
    |
    +-- k/0 with k != 0   -> a POLE. Do NOT factor.
    |                        Cancel first, then count the MULTIPLICITY
    |                        of the surviving denominator factor:
    |                          EVEN -> same sign both sides -> "= +-infinity"
    |                          ODD  -> signs flip           -> "DNE"   [F5]
    |
    +-- a trig function undefined at a -> rewrite as sin/cos. [F4]
    |                        tan, cot : period pi,  EVERY asymptote identical
    |                        sec, csc : period 2pi, asymptotes ALTERNATE
    |                        First confirm cos (or sin) is really 0 at a.
    |                        tan at 2pi is 0. cot at 5pi/2 is 0. Do not guess.
    |
    +-- oscillation at a (sin(1/x)) -> multiplied by something -> 0?
                             YES -> SQUEEZE, answer 0.       [F2]
                             NO  -> DNE, oscillates.         [F3]

FINALLY, before you commit:
  1. Magnitude plausible?
  2. Sign derived a SECOND, independent way?
  3. Is it "= +-infinity" or "DNE"? Those are DIFFERENT answers.
  4. Written paper: write the justification sentence.

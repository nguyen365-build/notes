00

The 30-second version

If you only remember one thing, remember this chain. It is three lines of reading and no algebra at all.

f(x) = -3|x-2|                      parent: y = |x|,  a V with vertex (0,0)

  -> read INSIDE the bars:  (x-2)   SHIFT RIGHT 2         vertex (0,0) -> (2,0)
  -> read OUTSIDE, the 3:   x3      STRETCH VERTICALLY 3  arms get 3x steeper
  -> read OUTSIDE, the -:   x(-1)   REFLECT IN THE X-AXIS the V becomes a ^
  -> label                          (2,0) and (0,-6) and (4,-6)
01

What this question is really testing

It looks like the easiest question on the paper, and it is the one most likely to be marked zero on a fully correct graph. That is not a paradox.

Read the grading clause again: "no credit will be given if other method is used." The examiner is not buying a graph. The examiner is buying evidence that you can decompose a formula into named, ordered operations, and the graph is only the receipt. A perfect V drawn by plotting points scores nothing, because plotting points is the other method.

The quiet reason this question matters

Absolute value is the cheapest way to manufacture a corner, and a corner is the cheapest way to satisfy "continuous but not differentiable". Midterm 1 item 5 and Midterm 2 item 5 both ask you to sketch a function that is continuous but not differentiable at a point. If you own \(-3\lvert x-2\rvert\), you own that question too, and you can write it down in four seconds.

A note on the wording

The paper says "the basic graph of \(\lvert x \rvert\) in the interval in order to obtain". That is an incomplete sentence in the original, and no interval is ever named anywhere in the paper.

Do not spend exam time on it. Graph \(f\) on all of \(\mathbb{R}\), pick a window that contains the vertex with a couple of units either side, and label your axes. If your copy does name an interval, the only extra work is pushing the two endpoints through the same chain, which is Family 8 below.

The five things you must have cold

None of this is derivable under time pressure. All of it is recall.

2.1 · The parent graph and its anchor points

\(y=\lvert x\rvert\) is a V with vertex at the origin, made of two straight rays of slope \(-1\) and \(+1\).

\(x\)−2−1012
\(\lvert x\rvert\)21012

Carry these five points and nothing else. The vertex says where the graph is; the pairs either side say how steep it is and confirm the symmetry. One point is not enough, because a single point cannot show a stretch.

2.2 · The master template

Every transformation question in this course, for every parent function, is one expression with four dials:

$$y = a \cdot f\big(b(x-h)\big) + k$$

DialWhere it sitsWhat it does
\(h\)inside, subtracted from \(x\)horizontal shift, right if \(h\gt0\)
\(b\)inside, multiplying \(x\)horizontal scale by \(1/b\); reflects in the \(y\)-axis if \(b\lt0\)
\(a\)outside, multiplying \(f\)vertical scale by \(\lvert a\rvert\); reflects in the \(x\)-axis if \(a\lt0\)
\(k\)outside, added to \(f\)vertical shift, up if \(k\gt0\)
The structural fact, worth more than the table

Two of the four dials lie to you and two tell the truth. The inside dials \((h, b)\) do the opposite of what they look like: subtracting moves right, multiplying by a big number compresses. The outside dials \((a, k)\) do exactly what they look like.

So you never need four rules. You need one rule, inside is backwards, and then you read the formula.

2.3 · The canonical order

This is note-sheet block A6, and it is the order to write on the exam.

#StepOnly if
1horizontal shift by \(h\)\(h \ne 0\)
2horizontal scale by \(1/b\), reflecting in the \(y\)-axis if \(b\lt0\)\(b \ne 1\)
3vertical scale by \(\lvert a\rvert\)\(\lvert a\rvert \ne 1\)
4reflect in the \(x\)-axis\(a\lt0\)
5vertical shift by \(k\)\(k \ne 0\)

Inside first, outside second. Within each half: scale before shift on the way out, shift before scale on the way in.

2.4 · The atoms, as point maps

This is the version that never lies to you, and it is what I used to verify every claim on this page. A transformation is a rule that takes a point and returns a point.

TransformationPoint map
shift right \(h\)\((x,y) \mapsto (x+h,\;y)\)
shift up \(k\)\((x,y) \mapsto (x,\;y+k)\)
vertical stretch by \(a\)\((x,y) \mapsto (x,\;a\,y)\)
reflect in the \(x\)-axis\((x,y) \mapsto (x,\;-y)\)
horizontal scale for \(f(bx)\)\((x,y) \mapsto (x/b,\;y)\)
reflect in the \(y\)-axis\((x,y) \mapsto (-x,\;y)\)

Unsure whether a claimed order is right? Take one anchor point, push it through, and check the result satisfies the target equation. Five seconds, and it settles every argument.

2.5 · What the bars do to a number

\(\lvert u\rvert\) is \(u\) when \(u\ge0\) and \(-u\) when \(u\lt0\). So \(\lvert x-2\rvert\) is \(x-2\) to the right of 2 and \(2-x\) to the left. The switch happens where the inside is zero, and the switch point is the vertex.

Fastest vertex-finder there is

Set the inside of the bars to zero and solve.

03

The universal procedure

Six steps, every transformation question
  1. Name the parent. Strip every number. What is left is \(\lvert x\rvert\), \(x^2\), \(\sqrt x\), \(1/x\), \(x^3\), \(\sin x\). Say it out loud and draw it small in the corner with its anchor points.
  2. Force the formula into the template. Make it look exactly like \(a\,f(b(x-h))+k\). Factor the inside if it is not factored. Expose any hidden sign. This step is where marks are lost, not the sketching.
  3. Read the four dials. Write \(a, b, h, k\) as four numbers before saying anything about the graph.
  4. List the transformations in canonical order, skipping every dial at its default. Name each one in words, with direction and amount.
  5. Push three anchor points through, one stage at a time. Keep the table. Vertex plus one point on each arm is enough.
  6. Draw and label. Axes with scales, the curve, vertex coordinates, two more labeled points. Then state the vertex and range in words.

04

Q2.2 worked five ways

Forced into the template, \(f(x)=-3\lvert x-2\rvert\) gives \(a=-3\), \(b=1\), \(h=2\), \(k=0\). Two dials are at their default, so only three transformations survive.

Route A The canonical order - use this one

Step 1. Shift right 2. \(y=\lvert x\rvert \to y=\lvert x-2\rvert\). The V keeps its shape and slides right; vertex \((0,0)\to(2,0)\).

Step 2. Stretch vertically by 3. \(y=\lvert x-2\rvert \to y=3\lvert x-2\rvert\). Every height triples. The vertex sits at height 0 so it does not move; the arms go from slope \(\mp1\) to \(\mp3\).

Step 3. Reflect in the \(x\)-axis. \(y=3\lvert x-2\rvert \to y=-3\lvert x-2\rvert\). Every height changes sign, the V becomes a \(\wedge\), and the vertex is now the maximum.

Route B Fuse the two vertical steps

\(a=-3\) is one number, so you can call it one transformation: "scale the \(y\)-values by \(-3\)". Two steps instead of three, and verified correct.

The risk is that the question says "indicate the transformations in the order they are applied", which invites a list. A two-item list against a three-operation formula can read as an omission. Defensible, no upside, do not do it.

Route C Some other order - and why all six work here

I pushed the parent graph through all \(3!=6\) orderings of {shift right 2, stretch by 3, reflect in \(x\)-axis}. All six reproduce \(f\) exactly.

The most useful idea on this page

On this question the order genuinely does not matter, and yet the question is explicitly about order. The order is free only because two things happen to be true at once: there is no vertical shift \((k=0)\) and no horizontal scaling \((b=1)\).

  • A vertical stretch and a vertical shift do not commute.
  • A horizontal scale and a horizontal shift do not commute.
  • But a vertical operation and a horizontal operation always commute, because one touches only \(y\) and the other only \(x\).

Here the only horizontal operation is a shift, and both vertical operations are just multiplication of \(y\). Nothing can collide. Write the canonical order anyway - it costs nothing, and it is the only order still right when the examiner adds a \(+k\) or a \(2x\).

Route D Horizontal compression instead of vertical stretch

A genuine curiosity of this parent: because \(\lvert 3x\rvert = 3\lvert x\rvert\) identically, a vertical stretch by 3 and a horizontal compression by \(\tfrac13\) draw the same graph. I verified both halves, and verified that [compress by 1/3, shift right 2, reflect] reproduces \(f\).

Two reasons not to write it. First it is fragile: the compression must come before the shift. I verified that [shift right 2, compress by 1/3, reflect] does not give \(f\); it gives \(y=-\lvert x-4\rvert\). It only survives after the shift if the compression is taken about the line \(x=2\), which I also verified, and which no marker wants to read.

Second, the formula shows a 3 on the outside. Calling an outside number a horizontal transformation invites the marker to think you cannot tell inside from outside, which is exactly the skill being examined.

Correct, and still a bad answer

Write the vertical stretch.

Route E The piecewise form - do not submit this

\(f\) splits at the vertex:

$$f(x)=\begin{cases} 3x-6, & x\lt2 \\ -3x+6, & x\ge2 \end{cases}$$

Verified to agree with \(f\) everywhere, with slope \(+3\) on the left and \(-3\) on the right. Genuinely useful, and genuinely worth zero marks here, because graphing two lines is the other method.

Use it for the three things it is good for: checking your sketch in eight seconds, one-sided limits at the corner, and the derivative \(f'(x)=3\) for \(x\lt2\), \(-3\) for \(x\gt2\), undefined at 2. All three verified. That last one is the ready-made "continuous but not differentiable" answer.

05

The exam-ready write-up

Copy this shape. Six lines, and it earns every available mark.

Write exactly this

The basic function is \(y=\lvert x\rvert\), a V with vertex at the origin.

Writing \(f(x)=-3\lvert x-2\rvert\) in the form \(a\lvert x-h\rvert+k\) gives \(a=-3\), \(h=2\), \(k=0\). The transformations, in the order they are applied, are:

  1. Shift right 2 units: \(y=\lvert x-2\rvert\). The vertex moves from \((0,0)\) to \((2,0)\).
  2. Vertical stretch by a factor of 3: \(y=3\lvert x-2\rvert\). Each \(y\)-value is tripled, so the arms become three times steeper. The vertex is unchanged because its height is 0.
  3. Reflection in the \(x\)-axis: \(y=-3\lvert x-2\rvert\). Each \(y\)-value changes sign, so the V opens downward.

Tracking \((0,0)\), \((\pm1,1)\) and \((\pm2,2)\) through the three steps gives \((2,0)\), \((1,-3)\), \((3,-3)\), \((0,-6)\) and \((4,-6)\). The graph is a \(\wedge\) with maximum at the vertex \((2,0)\), arms of slope \(+3\) and \(-3\), and \(y\)-intercept \(-6\). The range is \(y\le0\).

Three things in that write-up do real work, and none of them is the graph.

  • The line "in the form \(a\lvert x-h\rvert+k\) gives \(a=-3, h=2, k=0\)" proves you decomposed rather than guessed.
  • Each step shows the intermediate equation, so the marker sees a transformation applied to a function, not to a picture.
  • The sentence about the vertex not moving under the stretch pre-empts the obvious follow-up doubt.

Do not skip the last two lines. Naming the vertex and the range in words costs four seconds and is often a mark of its own.

06

Verify before you commit

Four checks, about eight seconds total. Do all four every time.

  1. The vertex, from the inside. Set \(x-2=0\), so \(x=2\), and \(f(2)=-3\lvert0\rvert=0\). Vertex \((2,0)\). If your corner is elsewhere, the sketch is wrong, not the algebra.
  2. Direction of opening, from the sign of \(a\). \(a=-3\lt0\), so it opens downward and the vertex is a maximum. There is no third case.
  3. One easy point. \(f(0)=-3\lvert-2\rvert=-3(2)=-6\). Both sign traps live inside that single evaluation: the bars turn \(-2\) into \(+2\), and then the \(-3\) makes it negative. If you got \(+6\) you applied the bars after the multiplication.
  4. Magnitude sanity. Move 1 sideways from the vertex, drop 3. Move 2, drop 6. Dropping 1 for 1 means you forgot the stretch; rising means you forgot the reflection.

The single most expensive mistake

It is not a typo. It is drawing a correct graph without naming the transformations. "No credit will be given if other method is used" makes the named ordered list the answer, and the graph supporting evidence. Write the list first, then draw.

The most expensive typo

The missing bracket. abs(x)-2 and abs(x-2) are different functions: one has vertex \((0,-2)\), the other \((2,0)\).

On the bars specifically: the vertical bar character is not an operator in either parser, so an answer typed with vertical bars will not parse and the preview box comes back empty. Always abs(...).

08

The variation catalog

The families are organised by the tool the variation forces, not by how the formula looks. Read them as structure, not as sixty facts.

How to read every table below

Every question in this topic is the same four numbers. You read \(a\), \(b\), \(h\), \(k\) off the formula and the answer writes itself. Only three things vary: which dials are non-default, which parent sits underneath, and which direction you are asked to go (formula to graph, or graph to formula). That is the whole space.

Where the signs come from. There are exactly two sign-flipping mechanisms and they sit on opposite sides of the function: \(a\lt0\) flips vertically, \(b\lt0\) flips horizontally. Every other sign is a direction \((h, k)\), not a reflection. Name which mechanism is producing a minus sign and you cannot get a reflection question wrong.

Family 2 A vertical shift joins in, and now order matters

Trigger
A constant added outside the bars: \(a\lvert x-h\rvert+k\), \(k\ne0\).
Tool
Vertical stretch before vertical shift, always.
Why
The first family where a wrong order draws a genuinely different graph, and the likeliest way the examiner upgrades Q2.2.

Take \(g(x)=-3\lvert x-2\rvert+4\).

  • Correct: right 2, stretch 3, reflect, then up 4. Verified: reproduces \(g\).
  • Wrong: right 2, up 4, then stretch and reflect. Verified: does not reproduce \(g\).
  • That wrong order actually draws \(-3(\lvert x-2\rvert+4)\), whose vertex is \((2,-12)\) instead of \((2,4)\). Both vertices verified.

The \(x\)-intercepts, every time

Family 3 A coefficient inside the bars

Trigger
\(x\) multiplied by something inside: \(\lvert 2x-6\rvert\), \(\lvert\tfrac x2-1\rvert\), \(\lvert 3x+6\rvert\).
Tool
Factor the inside first: \(\lvert bx+c\rvert=\lvert b\rvert\cdot\lvert x+c/b\rvert\).
Trap
Reading the shift off the unfactored constant.

Verified: \(\lvert 2x-6\rvert = 2\lvert x-3\rvert\) identically. Here is the precise version of the "shift before stretch" rule, which is subtler than the one-liner. There are two consistent readings, and both are correct:

The rule to actually carry

Both correct readings are literally the same map, since \(\tfrac{x+6}{2}=\tfrac x2+3\). So the rule is not "shift first" or "scale first". It is: the shift amount must match the reading you chose. Factor and use \(h\), or do not factor and use \(c\), but never take \(h\) from one and the position from the other.

The safe habit: always factor. Then \(h\) is unambiguous.

A special mercy for this parent

Because \(\lvert bx\rvert=\lvert b\rvert\lvert x\rvert\), a horizontal compression by \(\tfrac1b\) and a vertical stretch by \(b\) give the same graph for \(\lvert x\rvert\). Verified. So \(\lvert 2x-6\rvert\) can also be described as "right 3, then stretch vertically by 2".

But do not carry the habit to other parents, where it is false: \(\sqrt{2x}=\sqrt2\sqrt x\) and \((2x)^2=4x^2\) work, yet \(\sin(2x)\ne2\sin x\).

Family 4 The reflection that is invisible

Trigger
A minus sign on \(x\) inside the bars: \(\lvert -x\rvert\), \(\lvert 2-x\rvert\), \(\lvert -x+5\rvert\).
Tool
Recognise the parent is even, so the \(y\)-axis reflection changes nothing, then rewrite in standard form.

Verified identities: \(\lvert -x\rvert=\lvert x\rvert\) and \(\lvert 2-x\rvert=\lvert x-2\rvert\) for every \(x\). So \(\lvert 2-x\rvert\) is not "reflect in the \(y\)-axis then shift"; it is just "shift right 2". I verified that inserting a \(y\)-axis reflection at the start of the Q2.2 chain leaves the answer unchanged.

The trap

The reflection is invisible only because the parent is symmetric, and only when it acts on the parent. Once the graph has been shifted, a \(y\)-axis reflection is very much visible: I verified that [right 2, stretch 3, reflect in y-axis] does not give \(f\), because it produces \(3\lvert x+4\rvert\).

Family 6 Absolute value as a transformation, not as the parent

Trigger
Bars around something that is not bare \(x\): \(\lvert x^2-4\rvert\), or bars around the input, \(f(\lvert x\rvert)\).
Tool
A different pair of operations entirely. The family most likely to catch you out, because it looks like the same topic and is not.

Outer bars, \(y=\lvert f(x)\rvert\): fold up. Keep everything at or above the \(x\)-axis; reflect everything below it upward.

Inner bars, \(y=f(\lvert x\rvert)\): mirror the right half. Discard the left half and replace it with a mirror image of the right. The result is always even.

\(\lvert x^2-4\rvert\) and \(\lvert x\rvert^2-4\) are different functions. I verified they disagree.

That fourth row. Applying \(f(\lvert x\rvert)\) to \(-3\lvert x-2\rvert\) turns one \(\wedge\) into two, joined at \((0,-6)\), and the point that was near a maximum becomes a local minimum. It is the most interesting graph in the topic and a legitimate exam variation.

Family 7 The reverse direction, graph to formula

Trigger
A graph or description is given; produce the equation or name the transformations.
Tool
The vertex gives \(h\) and \(k\) immediately. One more point gives \(a\).

Read the vertex: that is \((h,k)\). Read the opening direction: down means \(a\lt0\). Take any other point \((x_1,y_1)\) and solve \(a=\dfrac{y_1-k}{\lvert x_1-h\rvert}\). Sanity check with the mirror point.

Worked: vertex \((5,-2)\), through \((7,-8)\). Then \(a=\dfrac{-8-(-2)}{\lvert7-5\rvert}=\dfrac{-6}{2}=-3\), so \(y=-3\lvert x-5\rvert-2\). Verified at \(x=5\), \(x=7\), and \(x=3\) by symmetry.

The one trap here

Use \(\lvert x_1-h\rvert\) in the denominator, not \(x_1-h\). Pick a point to the left of the vertex and forget the bars, and you get the sign of \(a\) backwards.

The payload

Midterm 1 item 5 and Midterm 2 item 5 both ask you to sketch a function that is continuous but not differentiable at a point. The answer is a corner, and the fastest corner in existence is \(\lvert x-a\rvert\). So if the paper asks for continuous but not differentiable at \(x=0\), write \(y=\lvert x\rvert\), or \(y=-3\lvert x\rvert\) if you want to look like you meant it, and move on.

The related distinction: a corner has two different finite one-sided slopes, a cusp has one-sided slopes going to \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\), and a vertical tangent has both going to the same infinity. All three are non-differentiable; only the first two are cheap to draw.

09

The trap list

Ranked by how often each one actually costs marks on this question.

  1. Drawing a correct graph without naming the transformations. Not a maths error, and the number one way to score zero here. The clause makes the ordered list the answer. Write the list before you draw.
  2. Shifting the wrong way. \(\lvert x-2\rvert\) moves right. Fix it mechanically: set the inside to zero, \(x-2=0\Rightarrow x=2\), so the shift was rightward.
  3. Reflecting in the wrong axis. \(a\lt0\) is an \(x\)-axis reflection. A \(y\)-axis reflection comes from \(b\lt0\) and is invisible on this parent anyway. Writing "reflect in the \(y\)-axis" for the \(-3\) shows you cannot tell inside from outside.
  4. Applying the bars after the multiplication. \(f(0)=-3\lvert0-2\rvert=-3(2)=-6\), not \(-3(-2)=+6\). The bars run first.
  5. Reading the shift off an unfactored inside coefficient. \(\lvert 2x-6\rvert\) has its vertex at 3, not 6. Factor first, every time.
  6. Wrong order once a vertical shift is present. \(-3\lvert x-2\rvert+4\) has vertex \((2,4)\); doing the \(+4\) first draws \((2,-12)\). Stretch before shift on the vertical side.
  7. Forgetting the stretch entirely. The commonest sketch error. One across from the vertex should be three down, not one down.
  8. Leaving the graph unlabeled. Axes with scales, vertex with coordinates, two more labeled points. Unlabeled graphs bleed marks even when the shape is perfect.
  9. Mistaking \(\lvert f(x)\rvert\) for \(f(\lvert x\rvert)\). Outer bars fold up; inner bars mirror the right half. Family 6.
  10. Treating the missing interval as a task. It is a typo in the paper. Graph on \(\mathbb{R}\), label properly, move on.

How to score yourself

Mark sign and magnitude separately, because they are different diseases with different cures.

  • Sign errors (shifted the wrong way, reflected the wrong axis, opened the wrong direction) mean you have not internalised inside versus outside. Cure: re-read 2.2 and redo Families 0, 1 and 4. Do not do more drills yet.
  • Magnitude errors (forgot a stretch, used the unfactored shift, wrong intercept) mean the reading is fine and the arithmetic is rushed. Cure: the four checks in section 6, on every single drill.
  • Order errors only appear in Families 2 and 3. If those are your only misses you are in good shape; write the canonical order mechanically.
  • Naming errors (correct graph, no list) are the most dangerous because they feel like success. Cure: on every drill, write the numbered list before touching the graph.

Given a formula to graph by transformations
|
+-- 1. STRIP the numbers. What parent is left?
|      |x| / x^2 / x^3 / sqrt(x) / 1/x / sin x
|      Draw it small with its anchor points.
|
+-- 2. Are the bars around bare x, or around something else?
|      |  around bare x (or the parent's own variable)  -> go to 3
|      |  around a whole function, y = |f(x)|           -> FOLD UP.  Family 6. done
|      +- around |x| as the input, y = f(|x|)           -> MIRROR RIGHT HALF. Family 6. done
|
+-- 3. Is x multiplied by anything INSIDE?
|      |  no  -> b = 1, skip horizontal scaling
|      +- yes -> FACTOR IT OUT first:  |bx+c| = |b| * |x + c/b|
|                then either fold |b| into the outside coefficient
|                (legal for |x| only), or keep it and use h = c/b
|
+-- 4. Is the coefficient of x inside NEGATIVE?
|      -> rewrite so it is positive.  |2-x| = |x-2|.  For |x| this is free.
|         (for x^3, sqrt, 1/x, sin it is a real y-axis reflection: keep it)
|
+-- 5. READ THE FOUR DIALS off  a*f(b(x-h)) + k
|      a = ?   b = ?   h = ?   k = ?      <- write these down as numbers
|
+-- 6. LIST, in this order, skipping every default:
|      1  shift right h            (left if h<0)
|      2  horizontal scale 1/b     (+ reflect in y-axis if b<0)
|      3  vertical scale |a|
|      4  reflect in x-axis        (only if a<0)
|      5  shift up k               (down if k<0)
|
+-- 7. PUSH 3 POINTS through the list, one stage per column.
|      vertex + one point on each side.  Keep the table.
|
+-- 8. DRAW: axes with scales, curve, vertex labeled, 2 more points labeled.
|      State the vertex and the range in words.
|
+-- 9. CHECK (8 seconds):
|      inside = 0 gives the vertex x
|      sign of a gives the opening direction
|      f(0) gives the y-intercept
|      1 across from the vertex = |a| down/up
|
+-- 10. PREVIEW any typed box.  abs(...) not bars,  explicit *,  bracket the argument.

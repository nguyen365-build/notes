Nothing Checks the Name

Four integrals, and a second sentence that asks you to say which technique you used. Every mistake you can make in the algebra has a check. The one you can make in the name does not, and this page is about that asymmetry.

This course's integration-technique vocabulary is one name wide. The only technique it ever names is substitution.

That is good news under pressure, because the naming half of this question has a very small answer space. For any part, the honest name is either "substitution, with $u$ equal to something" or "no technique, just algebra and the power rule".

03What the only check you have can see

Q16.1 established the one instrument available on any integration question: differentiate your answer and compare it to the integrand. It is fast and it is decisive. This run extended that census with a family Q16.1 did not have, and the result is the figure below.

Twenty-six wrong answers were constructed deliberately, one cell each, and run through the check. Teal is caught: twelve of them. Terracotta and amber are not, and amber is this question's own subject - seven naming errors, the largest single blind family of the four. Counts measured by harness section q162c.

Differentiating back detects every error that changes the function, and is structurally blind to every error that does not. A wrong name does not change the function.

What it catches, and by how much

Nothing in the caught set is marginal. The smallest disagreement anywhere among the twelve is a relative gap of 0.293 - roughly thirty percent of scale, and symbolically a whole missing factor. You do not need a calculator to see it.

The naming result is stronger than a threshold comparison. For every one of the seven, the derivative values the checker computed were bit-identical to the ones it computes for the correct answer. The check never sees the name, so its input is literally unchanged, and no tolerance anywhere could alter the verdict.

And the blind arm needs a control, or it proves nothing. "The label is undetected" and "my checker is broken" produce the same output. Two controls were run: a non-constant perturbation of the same size as the seeded $+7$, which the same checker does catch at a gap of 0.00493; and an answer that is both misnamed and mathematically wrong, caught at 2.000 by the mathematics rather than by the name.

04The technique list describes your work

This is the idea the question exists for, and it is easy to state and easy to doubt, so it was measured. Four pairs of routes were compared as functions across seven sample points each.

$\tan x\sec^2 x$  (b) $u=\tan x$, giving $\tan^2x/2$ $u=\sec x$, giving $\sec^2x/2$ differ by exactly $1/2$
$\dfrac{\sqrt{5x}}{x^2}$  (inside c) divide through: NO technique substitution, $u=5x$ identical, difference 0

The last row is the one to study. Part (c) needs no technique. But a student who splits the fraction and then substitutes $u=5x$ on the middle term gets exactly the same answer and owes a name list that includes substitution. Neither list is wrong. The one that matches the work is the one that is right.

The first row is the cleanest demonstration. The two routes do not merely differ by a constant - they produce the same function, from two genuinely different technique lists. So "same answer" carries no information at all about "same technique".

Write the name while you take the step, not when you look back at the finished answer. The finished answer cannot tell you which route produced it, and this section is the proof.

05The four parts, and the name each one owes

(a)  RUNG 3 · SUBSTITUTION

Try $u=\sqrt{2x}$, so $du = dx/\sqrt{2x}$. Then ask the only question that matters: is that $du$ present? The integrand carries $dx/\sqrt x$, which is not quite it, but the two differ by a constant:

$$\frac{dx}{\sqrt x} \;=\; \frac{\sqrt2\,dx}{\sqrt2\sqrt x} \;=\; \frac{\sqrt2\,dx}{\sqrt{2x}} \;=\; \sqrt2\,du.$$

That single line is the whole of part (a).

$$\int \frac{\cos\sqrt{2x}}{\sqrt x}\,dx \;=\; \sqrt2\int\cos u\,du \;=\; \sqrt2\,\sin\left(\sqrt{2x}\right)+C, \qquad x>0.$$

The $\sqrt x$ downstairs needs $x>0$, not $x\ge0$. The answer is perfectly well defined at $x=0$, which is exactly why the restriction is easy to forget.

(b)  RUNG 3 · SUBSTITUTION · DEFINITE

Take $u=\tan x$, so $du=\sec^2x\,dx$, which is sitting right there. Either change the limits with the variable, or back-substitute and use the originals - but never mix the two.

$$\int_0^{\pi/3}\tan x\sec^2x\,dx \;=\; \int_0^{\sqrt3} u\,du \;=\; \left[\frac{u^2}{2}\right]_0^{\sqrt3} \;=\; \frac32.$$

No $+C$ here. A definite integral is a number. And $u=\sec x$ is equally correct, giving $\sec^2x/2$, which exceeds $\tan^2x/2$ by exactly $1/2$ and lands on the same $3/2$.

(c)  RUNG 1 · NO TECHNIQUE

This is the part the naming instruction exists for. Split the fraction over its single-term denominator and rewrite the root as a power:

$$\frac{x^3+\sqrt{5x}-4}{x^2} \;=\; x + \sqrt5\,x^{-3/2} - 4x^{-2}.$$

The exponent step is where marks go: $x^{1/2}/x^2 = x^{-3/2}$, and the arithmetic $\tfrac12-2=-\tfrac32$ is done wrong more often than anything else on this question. Then the power rule three times:

$$\int \frac{x^3+\sqrt{5x}-4}{x^2}\,dx \;=\; \frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{2\sqrt5}{\sqrt x}+\frac4x+C, \qquad x>0.$$

Say that there was no technique - do not leave the line blank. A blank technique line and a skipped instruction look identical on the page. "No technique required: algebraic simplification, then the power rule term by term" takes four seconds.

(d)  RUNG 3 · HINTED SUBSTITUTION

The exam prints the rewrite, so take it. The hint says $\sec^3x\tan x = \sec^2x\,\sec x\tan x$, and it helps because $\sec x\tan x$ is exactly the derivative of $\sec x$.

$$\int \sec^3x\tan x\,dx \;=\; \int u^2\,du \;=\; \frac{\sec^3 x}{3}+C, \qquad \cos x\ne0.$$

The hint is not a shortcut around the technique. It is the technique's setup step, printed because the examiner wants the substitution rather than the sine-and-cosine detour. Name both halves.

Naming a technique you did not use is worse than naming nothing, because it is a positive claim about your method that a marker can see is false. And since no check will catch it, it has to be prevented rather than detected.

The only instrument that exists: a two-way audit

Read the answer and the technique line against each other in both directions. It takes ten seconds and it is not automatic.

  • Name to work. For the technique I named, where in my working is it visible? This catches the invented name, which is the expensive one.
  • Work to name. For every step my working took, is it named on the page? This catches omissions, which are the common ones.
  1. Not naming the technique at all. The instruction is the second of two sentences and it is the easiest thing on the paper to read past.
  2. Leaving part (c)'s line blank because there was no technique. Write "none required".
  3. Getting $\tfrac12-2$ wrong on part (c). Caught by differentiating back, at a relative gap of 0.497 - but only if you check.
  4. Dropping the $\sqrt2$ on part (a). The answer looks entirely reasonable and is wrong by a factor of 1.414.
  5. Missing the $+C$ on (a), (c) and (d). Three of four parts are indefinite and nothing you can check will remind you.
  6. Putting a $+C$ on part (b). It is a definite integral, so the answer is the number $3/2$.
  7. Swapping the limits on part (b). The antiderivative behind the wrong answer measures 2.981e-16 - flawless. Only a sign check finds it.
  8. Writing x^-3/2 or sec^3(x) into Mobius. Neither raises an error and both parse as something else.

The two questions share their trap and invert their emphasis. On Q16.1 the cost of missing that a part needs no technique is doing extra work. On Q16.2 the cost is naming a technique you did not use, which is a positive false claim.

One procedure, both questions

1  CLASSIFY   limits present -> a number, no +C.  absent -> a function, +C.
2  ALGEBRA    divide through, expand, roots to powers.  A sum of powers is solved.
3  u AND du   pick u, compute du, check du is present up to a constant factor.
4  IDENTITY   rewrite, then re-enter at step 2.  (Or take the printed hint.)
5  FINISH     +C, or F(b) - F(a) upper first.  State the domain if there is a root.
6  CHECK      differentiate back.  Catches every wrong FUNCTION.  No wrong NAME, ever.
7  NAME       say what you did - and audit it both ways, because step 6 cannot.

Steps 1 to 6 are Q16.1's. Step 7 is Q16.2's, and it is the only step in the whole category with no automatic check.

Q8.4 on this same paper asks the identical favour for derivatives. Between them the two naming items are 10 of Final Exam 2's 81 points, so the habit is worth practising once, properly.

Each antiderivative was checked three ways. Complex-step differentiation at twelve points per part across the stated domain, with integer powers by repeated multiplication rather than through a logarithm, which would place a branch cut on the negative real axis. A bounded Richardson ladder whose observed order was measured rather than assumed - all four parts reached 2.000 at the finest rung, against a curved control that also reached 2.000. And tanh-sinh quadrature of the integrand over an interval inside a single branch, an arm which never sees the closed form.

A digit lock inherits the precision of the method that produced it, so that precision was measured. The first quadrature agreed with the exact value to only 4.974e-08 on the one integral with a singular endpoint. My first diagnosis blamed the abscissa alone - computing a node as centre-plus-half-width destroys by cancellation exactly the digits that double exponential clustering creates - and a control refuted it.

Defects found in the course files

  • The review file misquotes this very question. It writes part (a) as $\int\cos\sqrt x/\sqrt x\,dx$; the exam prints $\sqrt{2x}$ under the cosine. The dropped 2 changes the answer. Trust the paper.
  • It contradicts itself on the Fundamental Theorem's part numbers, calling the same statement Part 1 in one place and Part 2 in another. The mathematics is right in both; the labelling is not consistent.
  • Its integration strategy is ambiguous where it matters. "Inside function present, use substitution" and "Polynomial, expand first" are given with no ordering, and $\left(x^2-4\right)^2$ satisfies both. Substitution fails there. Algebra goes first, always.
  • The questions file is not an independent source. Its final 31,306 characters are byte-identical to the tail of the papers file, which is 99.9 percent of it. A term in both files has been counted once, not corroborated twice.
  • The categorized file is cp1252, not utf-8, and a utf-8 read of it fails at byte 318. Asserted as a live control rather than taken on trust. It contains no integration vocabulary at all.

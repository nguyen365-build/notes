The Floor Costs Nothing

Item 7 of Final Exam 2 pairs two questions on the same non-negative, cusped function. Part (a) already found its largest and smallest values. Part (b) is this question, and because the function can actually reach zero, the floor of the bound is free - all the real work, and all the real risk, sits in the ceiling and in one multiplication everyone is tempted to skip.

01The question and the answer

the comparison property, unchanged since Q17.1

If \(m \le f(x) \le M\) for every \(x\) in \([a,b]\), then \(m\,(b-a) \le \displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \le M\,(b-a)\).

A bound is not an estimate. An estimate rewards the single closest number. A bound only has to be TRUE - it can be arbitrarily loose and looseness is not an error. What IS an error is an invalid \(m\) or \(M\): a value that does not actually bound \(f\), even if the resulting interval happens to still contain the answer. Section 09 below measures exactly how often each kind of mistake shows.

04The shape, the cusp, and the two bounds

The mistake this whole category punishes is writing \(m \le I \le M\) instead of \(m(b-a) \le I \le M(b-a)\). Here that mistake is CAUGHT by the student's own answer - unlike Q17.1, where it is invisible. The top panel below is the actual curve, sampled from the real function; the bottom two are the bound comparison, on a shared scale.

Top: \(f(x)=(x^2+2x)^{2/3}\) on \([-2,3]\), showing the local max at \(x=-1\), the cusp at \(x=0\), and the two attained extremes \(m=0\), \(M=15^{2/3}\). Middle: the correct one-piece bound (amber) against the mutilated bound obtained by dropping \((b-a)\) (rust) - the true value marker sits inside the correct bar and outside the mutilated one. Bottom: splitting at the three critical points keeps the same floor but cuts the ceiling by a third.

The general rule, derived from Q17.2's average-value form: the slip hides exactly when the interval's width is at most \(M/f_{\text{avg}}\). Here \(f_{\text{avg}} = I/(b-a) = 2.1347260073\), so the threshold is \(15^{2/3}/2.1347260073 = 2.8491722005\). The exam's width is \(5\), well past it - caught with room. Q17.1's threshold for its own integrand was \(4/\pi=1.2732395447\); Q17.2's was also \(4/\pi\); this is a genuinely different number because the integrand is different.

Quadrature warning specific to this integrand. Left un-split, tanh-sinh over \([-2,3]\) gives \(10.6733805848\) at level 8 - wrong by \(2.5\times10^{-4}\) - because double-exponential clustering only helps at the ends of a piece, not at an interior singularity. Split at \(x=0\) first. Composite Simpson on the raw integrand measures order \(1.6666\), confirmed on a bounded ladder from \(n=64\) to \(4096\); substituting away the cusp restores order \(4.0000\) on the same routine. A digit-lock here needs the substitution, not just two ladders agreeing - Q15.5 already found two resolutions of this same cusp shape agreeing to \(3\times10^{-9}\) while both being \(4.3\times10^{-9}\) wrong.

08The variation catalog, six families

Family 0 the interval moves

Trigger. Same integrand, a different interval.

Re-run the closed-interval method for the new interval; multiply by its own width. Verified across eight intervals from \([-2,-1]\) (width 1, true/ceiling 73.92%) to the full \([-2,3]\) (35.10%). The two humps \([-2,-1]\) and \([-1,0]\) are the SAME integral (\(0.7391741598\) each), because \(f(-1+t)=f(-1-t)\).

Family 1 splitting to tighten

Trigger. "Improve your bound" / a finer partition.

For \(k\) EQUAL pieces the width ratio converges to \(1.3288\), not \(1\) - because \(f\) has THREE monotone branches, and the ratio is \(\sum(\text{branch spans})/(M-m)\), not the branch count. Splitting AT the branch points (Route C) gets the full \(33.4\%\) ceiling cut in one step instead.

Family 2 factor-wise sandwich

Trigger. The integrand factors into two bounded pieces.

On \([0,3]\), \((x^2+2x)^{2/3}=x^{2/3}(x+2)^{2/3}\) with \(2^{2/3}\le(x+2)^{2/3}\le5^{2/3}\), giving \([5.9434690480,\ 10.9479635920]\) - a \(72.6\%\) narrower gap than the crude one-piece bound on that subinterval, checked pointwise over 200001 samples with zero violations. Needs a factorable integrand; genuinely new to this question.

Family 4 average value & MVT

Trigger. Find \(c\) with \(f(c)=f_{\text{avg}}\).

\(f_{\text{avg}}=2.1347260073\), \(c=1.0295281386\), unique on \([-2,3]\) because \(f\) never exceeds \(f_{\text{avg}}\) on \([-2,0]\) (its local max there is only \(1\)). A wrong \(m'\) hides iff \(m'\le f_{\text{avg}}\); a wrong \(M'\) hides iff \(M'\ge f_{\text{avg}}\).

Family 5 estimates go bad when m=0

Trigger. A follow-up asks for one number.

Q17.2 ranked "midpoint of the bounds" first. Here it is nearly the WORST of four estimates tested (error \(4.53\) vs the 3-piece midpoint's \(0.55\)), because with \(m=0\) it is just half the ceiling - zero information from the floor. Refine the bound before estimating from it.

Family 7 cusp omission, tested not assumed

Trigger. Solving only \(f'=0\), missing where \(f'\) is undefined.

On the EXAM's own interval this is ABSORBED - \(x=-2\) independently forces \(m=0\). Tested on six other intervals: it propagates into a genuinely wrong bound on four of them, two still hiding and two caught. The absorption is a property of THIS interval, not of the mistake in general.

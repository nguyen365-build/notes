MATH 265 · Midterm 1, item 6 · 4 points

The Perpendicular Tangent

A four-point question where the calculus is one line and every lost mark is somewhere else. Here is the pipeline, every variation the examiner can build from it, and the two places the marks actually go.

Find the values of \(x\) such that tangent line to the curve \(f(x) = 3x^2 + 4x - 3\) is perpendicular to the line \(y = 6x + 2\).

The answer
\(x = -\dfrac{25}{36} \approx -0.694444\)

Exactly one value. The curve is a quadratic, so its slope function is linear, so it hits any required slope exactly once.

Type into Möbius
-25/36

Exact fraction. -0.694 fails at a \(10^{-4}\) tolerance, and -1/6x parses as \(-\frac{1}{6x}\).

01 · The device

It is a pipeline, not a derivative

Four stations run from the printed line to the reported answer. Only the third one is calculus, and it is the cheapest of the four. Stations one and four are where four-point items go to zero.

01 · Read

\(m_{\text{given}} = 6\)

Off the printed line. If it arrives as \(Ax+By=C\), solve for \(y\) first; the slope is \(-A/B\), never \(A\).

02 · Flip

\(m_{\text{req}} = -\dfrac16\)

Perpendicular means \(m_1m_2=-1\). Reciprocate and negate. Half a flip is the most common wrong number on the page.

03 · Solve

\(6x + 4 = -\dfrac16\)

The only calculus: \(f' = 6x+4\) by the power rule, then a linear equation. Then count the roots.

04 · Report

\(x = -\dfrac{25}{36}\)

The stem asked for values of \(x\). A point or a line equation would be the wrong object, and a machine-marked box rejects it.

Where the four points actually go

The differentiation here is a first-week power rule and carries almost no risk. The examiner is testing that the word perpendicular reaches you as the number \(-\frac16\), and not as \(6\), \(-6\), or \(+\frac16\).

Note the stem says “the values of \(x\)”, plural. Here there is exactly one. The plural is a habit of phrasing, not a hint, so do not invent a partner answer to satisfy the grammar. On a cubic curve, though, the plural is literal and the second root is the classic lost mark.

02 · The picture

What perpendicular looks like

Drawn with equal scales on both axes, so the right angle is a real right angle. On a stretched plot it would not look like one, which is why the check is arithmetic and never visual.

03 · The count

How many answers should there be?

This is the one question in the family worth asking out loud, because the stem never answers it. The number of tangent points is the number of times the slope function \(f'\) crosses the horizontal level \(m_{\text{req}}\). Sketch \(f'\), draw the level, count crossings. That one picture covers every curve in the catalog.

04 · Three routes

Worked three ways, and which one to use

Route A - slope first, then solve

The given line is already in \(y=mx+b\) form, so \(m_{\text{given}}=6\) and \(m_{\text{req}}=-\frac16\). By the power rule \(f'(x)=6x+4\), so

$$6x + 4 = -\frac16 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 6x = -\frac{1}{6}-\frac{24}{6} = -\frac{25}{6} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = -\frac{25}{36}.$$

\(f'\) is linear with nonzero slope, so this is the only root.

Route B - the quadratic slope formula

For \(f=ax^2+bx+c\), solving \(2ax+b=m\) once gives a formula good for every quadratic in the family:

$$x = \frac{m-b}{2a} = \frac{-\frac16 - 4}{6} = -\frac{25}{36}.$$

Route C - from the vertex, by symmetry

The vertex is where the slope is zero, at \(x_v = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac23\). Moving \(\Delta x\) right of the vertex changes the slope by \(2a\,\Delta x\), so slope \(m\) occurs at

$$x = x_v + \frac{m}{2a} = -\frac{24}{36} - \frac{1}{36} = -\frac{25}{36}.$$

Under exam pressure, use Route A, and carry Route C's “which side of the vertex” as the check. Route B is a real accelerator on a quadratic but does not generalise, and this family's harder variants are all cubics and rationals.

The unasked extras, in case the variant demands them

The tangent point is \(\left(-\frac{25}{36},\,-\frac{1871}{432}\right)\approx(-0.694444,\,-4.331019)\), and the tangent line is

$$y = -\frac16\left(x + \frac{25}{36}\right) - \frac{1871}{432} \;=\; -\frac{x}{6} - \frac{1921}{432}.$$

Both were confirmed by evaluating the curve and the line at the tangent point and getting the same number to \(10^{-14}\), and by showing \(f - T\) has a double root there. A double root is what tangency means; a single root would be a mere crossing.

05 · The catalog

Every variation, by the part it moves

There are only three moving parts in this whole family, and each variation moves exactly one. Families 0 to 4 change the phrase that sets \(m_{\text{req}}\). Families 5 to 7 change the shape of \(f\), and therefore only the root count. Families 8 to 12 change what you report at the end.

Family 0 · the phrase

Identify \(m_{\text{req}}\)

Trigger: the relationship word in the stem.

perpendicular to a line \(\to -1/m\). parallel to \(\to m\). horizontal \(\to 0\). perpendicular to the \(y\)-axis \(\to 0\). perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis \(\to\) vertical tangent, impossible for a polynomial.

Family 1 · quadratic

Perpendicular, quadratic curve

Trigger: “perpendicular”, and \(f\) is quadratic. This is Q9.1 itself.

\(f'\) is linear, so exactly one answer, always. Every answer is the vertex shifted by \(m_{\text{req}}/(2a)\), so the sign of \(m_{\text{req}}\) alone tells you which side to land on.

Family 2 · parallel

Parallel instead of perpendicular

Trigger: “parallel to”, or “the same slope as”.

Identical procedure, no reciprocal. This is the easier variation, which is exactly why it is dangerous: a reflex trained on perpendicular flips a slope that should not be flipped.

Family 3 · horizontal

Horizontal tangent

Trigger: “horizontal”, “parallel to the \(x\)-axis”, or in disguise: the vertex, the maximum, the critical point.

Same question with \(m_{\text{req}}=0\), and the bridge to the whole of the extrema category. On a quadratic the answer always equals \(-b/(2a)\), a free cross-check.

Family 4 · degenerate line

The given line is \(x=c\) or \(y=c\)

Trigger: a line with no \(y\), or no \(x\).

\(-1/m\) is undefined or infinite, so go to the geometry. Perpendicular to a vertical line means a horizontal tangent, \(f'=0\). Perpendicular to a horizontal line needs a vertical tangent, which a polynomial never has.

Family 5 · two roots

A cubic curve

Trigger: \(f\) is a cubic, so \(f'(x)=m\) is a quadratic equation.

The classic lost mark. One root gets found, the stem said “values”, and the second is never looked for. Say the count out loud: “\(f'\) is a quadratic, the discriminant is positive, so there are exactly two.”

Family 6 · no solution

\(m_{\text{req}}\) outside the range of \(f'\)

Trigger: a required slope the slope function never reaches.

Do not leave it blank. “No such \(x\) exists, because \(f'(x)=3x^2+1\ge 1\) while the required slope is \(-\frac12\)” is a complete answer and earns the marks.

Family 7 · not a polynomial

Roots, reciprocals, exponentials, trig

Trigger: \(f\) is \(\sqrt x\), \(1/x\), \(e^x\), \(\ln x\), \(\sin x\).

Two new obligations: a domain check, since a root outside the domain is not an answer; and a root count that is not read off a degree. \(-1/x^2=-\frac14\) gives two answers, and \(\cos x=\frac12\) gives infinitely many unless an interval is stated.

Family 8 · a line

The answer is an equation, not an \(x\)

Trigger: “find the equation of the tangent line”.

Point-slope, with \(y_0=f(x_0)\) from the original function. Three failure modes in order: stopping at \(x_0\); using \(f'(x_0)\) as the \(y\)-coordinate; writing the line with \(m_{\text{given}}\) instead of \(m_{\text{req}}\).

Family 9 · the normal

Normal line to the curve

Trigger: “normal line”, or “perpendicular to the curve” rather than to a line.

The flip runs the other way: \(m_{\text{normal}}=-1/f'(a)\), and \(a\) was given to you. The word that decides is “to the line” versus “to the curve”.

Family 10 · external point

A tangent through a point off the curve

Trigger: a point is given rather than a line, and it is not on the curve.

No perpendicularity at all. Unknown tangency point \(a\), then force \(f'(a)(p-a)+f(a)=q\). Usually two answers. Most likely family to be mistaken for Q9.1 and solved wrong.

Family 11 · disguised line

The slope is hidden

Trigger: \(Ax+By=C\), two points, a segment, a description.

Convert to a slope before anything else. The standard misfire is reading \(A\) as the slope of \(2x-3y=6\), giving \(2\) instead of \(\frac23\), after which the whole answer follows the error silently.

Family 12 · parameter

Solve for a constant, not for \(x\)

Trigger: the curve or the line carries an unknown constant.

Same equation, different unknown. “For which \(a\) does \(f=ax^2+4x-3\) have slope \(-\frac16\) at \(x=-1\)?” gives \(-2a+4=-\frac16\), so \(a=\frac{25}{12}\).

06 · The checks

Verify before you commit

One calculator operation tests all four stations at once. This is the centred-difference check established in the Q8.1 tutorial, applied at your answer rather than at a convenient point.

THE EIGHT-SECOND CHECK

  it must equal m_req     not merely "something plausible"

In Q8.1 this check verified that you had differentiated correctly. Here it verifies the whole chain. Flip the slope the wrong way, read the given line wrong, or slip in the algebra, and the number that comes out is not \(m_{\text{req}}\).

A perpendicular pair is always one steep and one shallow, and always one up and one down. If both your slopes are positive, you are wrong before you have done any calculus.

07 · The traps

Ranked by what it actually costs

  1. Only one root found when there are two.A cubic curve makes \(f'(x)=m\) a quadratic, and the stem's word “values” was literal. Fix: after solving, name the degree of \(f'\) and state the count.
  2. Answering with the wrong object.An \(x\) when a line was demanded, a line when an \(x\) was demanded, or the slope \(m_{\text{req}}\) when either was. Fix: re-read the stem's final clause before typing.
  3. The perpendicular flip done half-way.\(-6\) is negated but not reciprocated; \(\frac16\) is reciprocated but not negated. Fix: multiply the two slopes and demand exactly \(-1\).
  4. Flipping when the stem said parallel, or not flipping when it said perpendicular.Fix: underline the relationship word before station one.
  5. Reading the slope wrong off \(Ax+By=C\).\(2x-3y=6\) has slope \(\frac23\), not \(2\) and not \(-\frac23\). Fix: solve for \(y\); never eyeball it.
  6. Using \(f'(x_0)\) as the \(y\)-coordinate of the tangent point.The point is \((x_0,f(x_0))\), always from the original function. Fix: evaluate line and curve at \(x_0\) and demand the same number.
  7. Rounding the answer.\(-0.69\) is wrong, \(-0.694\) fails at \(10^{-4}\), \(-\frac{25}{36}\) is right. Fix: enter the exact fraction.
  8. Missing a domain restriction on a non-polynomial.A root of \(f'(x)=m\) outside the domain of \(f\) is not a tangent point. Fix: state the domain before solving.
  9. Using \(-1/m\) on a vertical or horizontal given line.\(-1/0\) is not an answer. Fix: switch to the geometric statement.
  10. Manufacturing a second answer because the stem said “values”.On a quadratic there is exactly one, and inventing a partner loses the mark you had. The count comes from the degree of \(f'\), not the grammar of the stem.
  11. A silent sign error in \(-\frac16-4\).It is \(-\frac{25}{6}\), not \(-\frac{23}{6}\) and not \(\frac{23}{6}\). Fix: the eight-second check catches this immediately.

The two most expensive keystrokes

The bracket. -1/6x is one missing bracket, renders as something plausible, and is \(-\frac{1}{6x}\) rather than \(-\frac{x}{6}\). Write -x/6 and the ambiguity disappears.

The wrong object. A box that wants an \(x\) will not accept a line equation, and a box that wants a line will not accept -25/36. It is the stem's last four words that decide.

09 · Drill key

Score sign separately from magnitude

A wrong sign almost always means station two, the flip, went wrong. A wrong magnitude almost always means station three, the algebra. Those need different remediation, so a single “wrong” tells you nothing.

A quiet gate and an absent gate look identical

Fifteen of the assertions are seeded controls: each one is written to fail if the thing it guards were broken, so the gates are proven live rather than merely quiet. Three of them confirm that \(6\), \(-6\) and \(+\frac16\) all fail the perpendicularity product test, and two confirm that the eight-second check rejects the two most plausible wrong answers.

The derivative, two independent ways

\(f'(x)=6x+4\) was checked at five points by complex-step differentiation at \(h=10^{-30}\), which has no subtractive cancellation, and by a bounded Richardson centred-difference ladder. The ladder machinery was separately proven second order on a curved control function, \(\sin x + x^3\), with measured error ratios \(4.00,\ 4.00,\ 4.00\). That control is necessary because a centred quotient differentiates a quadratic exactly, so the quadratic itself cannot demonstrate the ladder's convergence order.

The root count, two independent ways

A numpy companion-matrix census and a 400,001-sample sign-change sweep over \([-50,50]\) with 80 bisection refinements. Both report exactly one real root. The census machinery was itself controlled by showing it returns two roots on \(3x^2-4x-2\) and zero on \(x^2+1\), so its answer of one here is a measurement and not a default.

Tangency, by evaluation rather than algebra

The line and the curve were each evaluated at \(x=-\frac{25}{36}\) and agreed to \(10^{-14}\); \(f-T\) was shown to have a double root there and to be positive on both sides, which distinguishes touching from crossing. A seeded control confirmed that a line with the right slope through the wrong point does change sign.

Perpendicularity, numerically

The slope of \(y=6x+2\) was recovered by complex-step as \(6.000000\) and the tangent's as \(-0.1666667\); their product is \(-1.000000\) to \(10^{-12}\), and the angle between the direction vectors is \(90.000000\) degrees. The given line's slope was read out of the exam paper by regular expression rather than assumed, so the number \(6\) on this page traces to the printed stem.

Point value

Midterm 1 prints its point values inline at the end of each stem. Item 6 is worth 4 points, confirmed by locating the marker immediately after the verbatim stem in the paper file. MT1 carries exactly one printed “no credit will be given” clause, on item 3, and it does not apply here. No paper on file sums to 100, so this question's weight is 4 of MT1's 64, not a percentage.

MATH 265 · Midterm 2 · Q7 · Q3.2 in the bank

Six Limits, Justified

Twenty-four points, a quarter of the paper, and the largest single question on it. It looks like six computations. It is really one classification test with six different answers, and the examiner picked exactly one limit of each verdict type.

The question, verbatim 24 points · 4 each

Evaluate each of the limits given below. If a limit does not exist, explain why.

  • a \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1}\frac{x^2 - 1}{x^3 - 1}\)

  • e \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\pi/3}\frac{\tan(2x)}{3x + \pi}\)
  • f \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2}\frac{\cos(\pi x)}{(x-2)^2}\)

SECTION 00

The 30-second version

You do not "do" a limit. You classify it, and the classification names the tool.

lim f(x) as x -> a
  -> 1. SUBSTITUTE a.  Do not simplify first.  Just look at what you get.
  -> 2. READ THE SYMBOL you got:
           a number      -> that IS the answer                  (done)
           0/0           -> indeterminate: factor / conjugate / trig identity
           nonzero / 0   -> INFINITE: do a one-sided SIGN check
           x -> infinity -> compare the leading terms only
  -> 3. Run the one tool that symbol named.
  -> 4. State the verdict AND the reason, because unjustified = zero.

Step 1 is the step everybody skips, and it is the step that decides everything. Part (c) of this very question is worth 4 points and is over the instant you substitute.

SECTION 01

What this question is really testing

Midterm 2 is 94 points across 11 questions. This one question is 24 of them.

Q3.224 points, 25.5% of the paper
Next largest question16 points
Six sub-parts4 points each

That is a quarter of the paper in one question, and it is the highest-density marks-per-minute item on the exam. There is no long computation anywhere in it.

The observation that matters more than any single answer

Look at the six cards at the top of this page again. All six reasons are different. The examiner did not choose six limits, they chose one representative of each verdict type.

PartThe type it represents
a\(0/0\) resolved by algebra
b\(0/0\) resolved by a trig identity
cNot indeterminate at all. The decoy.
dBehaviour as \(x \to \infty\)
enonzero\(/0\) where the sign flips, so DNE
fnonzero\(/0\) where the sign holds, so a signed infinity

This is a classification exam wearing the costume of a computation exam. The skill being marked is not algebra, it is triage. If you can sort a limit into the right bin in five seconds, all six parts take about eight minutes. If you cannot, you will apply cancelling to (c), get \(-1/2\), and lose 4 points on the easiest item on the page.

The "no credit for unjustified answers" clause

This appears verbatim on the paper, and it also appears on Q3.3. It means a bare correct number can score zero on a hand-marked sitting. Every answer needs its reason named, and Section 5 gives you the exact sentences.

Format warning

The printed paper says "explain why", which is hand-marked language. The live delivery for this course is Möbius, answer-only and machine-marked, where no reasoning is collected at all. Prepare for both: get the six verdicts fast, which is all Möbius wants, and be able to write one justification line each in case the paper you sit does ask for method.

"Indeterminate" and "undefined" are not the same word

\(\frac00\) is indeterminate: it tells you nothing yet, so you keep working. \(\frac{5}{0}\) is undefined, and that is informative: the limit is infinite, so you stop doing algebra and start checking signs.

Cancelling is only legal in the \(\frac00\) row. The difference is 4 marks.

2.2   The three trig limits, and the only form worth memorising

$$\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1 \qquad \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin(kx)}{x}=k \qquad \lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos(kx)}{x^2}=\frac{k^2}{2}$$

The third one is the one students do not have, and it is exactly what part (b) is. Learn it in that form and (b) is a five-second question: \(\frac{x^2}{1-\cos2x}\) is the reciprocal of \(\frac{1-\cos2x}{x^2}\to\frac{2^2}{2}=2\), so the answer is \(\frac12\).

Two supporting identities, both verified as identities over an interval, not just at a point:

$$1-\cos(kx) = 2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{kx}{2}\right) \qquad \sin(2x) = 2\sin x\cos x$$

2.3   The degree rule for \(x\to\pm\infty\)

For a ratio of polynomials, only the leading terms matter.

DegreesLimit
\(\deg(\text{num}) < \deg(\text{den})\)0
\(\deg(\text{num}) = \deg(\text{den})\)ratio of the leading coefficients
\(\deg(\text{num}) > \deg(\text{den})\)±∞, so DNE

Part (d) is row three: degree 3 over degree 2. The constants \(5\) and \(2\) are decoration and do not enter the answer.

2.4   The sign rule for nonzero\(/0\)

This is the half of the question most marks are lost in, and it is the whole of (e) and (f). Once you know the answer is infinite, one question remains: does the denominator's sign change as you cross the point?

Denominator near \(a\)Sign behaviourVerdict
an even power, e.g. \((x-2)^2\)positive on both sidesone signed infinity
an odd power or a linear factor, e.g. \(3x+\pi\)flipsleft \(\neq\) right, so DNE

Then the numerator's sign at the point decides which infinity. That is the entire method, and it is two lines of work.

SECTION 03

The universal procedure

Seven steps, and four of them are free

1. Substitute \(a\) into the unsimplified expression. Write down the symbol you get.
2. Classify it using the table in 2.1.
3. If \(0/0\): pick the algebra by looking at what is in the expression, whether polynomial, root, stacked fraction, or trig.
4. If nonzero\(/0\): do not do algebra. Do a sign check from each side.
5. If \(x\to\pm\infty\): compare leading terms only.
6. Recheck by substituting a number close to \(a\) into the original. This catches sign errors.
7. State the verdict and name the reason. Unjustified means unmarked.

SECTION 04

Worked, part by part, with the routes compared

a \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}\) = 2/3

Substituting gives \(\frac00\), so this is indeterminate and cancelling is legal.

Route A · factor both · use this

Both are differences of powers, so both carry a factor of \((x-1)\).

$$\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}=\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)(x^2+x+1)}=\frac{x+1}{x^2+x+1} \;\xrightarrow[x\to1]{}\;\frac{2}{3}$$

The cancelled form is continuous at \(x=1\), which is why you may now substitute. The two factorisations to memorise as a pair: \(x^2-a^2=(x-a)(x+a)\) and \(x^3-a^3=(x-a)(x^2+ax+a^2)\).

Route B · the difference-of-powers shortcut

$$\lim_{x\to a}\frac{x^n-a^n}{x^m-a^m}=\frac{n}{m}\,a^{\,n-m}$$

Here \(n=2\), \(m=3\), \(a=1\), so the limit is \(\frac23\cdot1^{-1}=\frac23\) instantly. Safe whenever \(a\neq0\) and both really are differences of \(n\)th powers.

b \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^2}{1-\cos(2x)}\) = 1/2

Substituting gives \(\frac{0}{1-1}=\frac00\).

Route A · half-angle identity · use this

Use \(1-\cos(2x)=2\sin^2x\):

$$\frac{x^2}{1-\cos2x}=\frac{x^2}{2\sin^2x}=\frac12\left(\frac{x}{\sin x}\right)^2 \;\xrightarrow[x\to0]{}\;\frac12(1)^2=\frac12$$

Route C · the conjugate

Multiply above and below by \(1+\cos2x\):

$$\frac{x^2(1+\cos2x)}{\sin^2 2x} =\left(\frac{x}{\sin2x}\right)^{2}(1+\cos2x) \;\xrightarrow[x\to0]{}\;\left(\frac12\right)^2\cdot 2=\frac12$$

Correct, and three times the work.

The only trap here is a reciprocal

The answer is \(\frac12\), not \(2\). Read the fraction one more time before you commit.

c \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{6x-9}{x^3-12x+3}\) = −3

Substitute first.

$$\frac{6(0)-9}{0-0+3}=\frac{-9}{3}=-3$$

The denominator at \(x=0\) is \(3\), not \(0\). There is no indeterminate form, the function is continuous at \(0\), and the limit is the value. The answer is \(-3\) and the work is finished in about four seconds. The justification is one word: continuity.

Why this part is on the paper

It is placed among five hard limits to catch pattern-matching. It looks like the others, so a student in a hurry reaches for a tool. Watch what the two most tempting tools produce.

What you might doResultRight?

Try to factor and cancelnothing cancels, minutes wastedno
The most expensive four points on the paper

That \(-\frac12\) is not a near miss, it is a different number, and it is what you get for using a tool whose precondition you never checked. L'Hôpital's rule requires \(\frac00\) or \(\frac{\infty}{\infty}\). This is \(\frac{-9}{3}\).

This is the single most valuable habit in the whole question: substitute before you reach.

d \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}\) = −∞

Numerator degree 3, denominator degree 2, so \(3>2\) and the ratio grows without bound. The leading behaviour is \(\frac{-2x^3}{x^2}=-2x\), which tends to \(-\infty\).

The version that shows your work, dividing by the highest power in the denominator:

$$\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2} =\frac{\frac{5}{x^2}-2x}{1+\frac{2}{x^2}} \;\xrightarrow[x\to\infty]{}\;\frac{0-2x}{1+0}=-2x\to-\infty$$

Every term with an \(x\) in a denominator dies, and what survives is \(-2x\).

The sign is the only thing that can go wrong

The leading coefficient is \(-2\) and \(x\to+\infty\), so the answer is \(-\infty\). Numerical check: at \(x=1000\) the expression is about \(-2000\), which is large and negative. Verified.

How to write it. "The limit does not exist; the expression decreases without bound, so \(\lim = -\infty\)." Saying \(-\infty\) is the explanation of why it does not exist, so give both.

e \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\pi/3}\frac{\tan(2x)}{3x+\pi}\) = DNE

This is the hardest part of the question and the one to slow down on.

Step 1 · substitute

The denominator is \(3(-\frac\pi3)+\pi=0\). Now the numerator, which is where people go wrong. \(2x\to-\frac{2\pi}{3}\), and \(\tan\) has period \(\pi\), so

$$\tan\!\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\tan\!\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}+\pi\right)=\tan\frac{\pi}{3}=\sqrt3$$

\(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\) sits strictly between the tangent asymptotes at \(-\frac{\pi}{2}\) and \(-\frac{3\pi}{2}\), so \(\tan(2x)\) is perfectly well behaved and continuous there. The numerator tends to \(\sqrt3\approx1.732\), which is not zero.

Step 2 · classify

\(\frac{\sqrt3}{0}\) is nonzero over zero. This is not \(\frac00\), so there is no algebra to do and nothing to cancel. It is infinite. The only remaining question is the sign.

Step 3 · sign check

The numerator is positive and roughly constant at \(\sqrt3\) near the point, so the sign of the whole expression is the sign of \(3x+\pi\).

Side\(x\)\(3x+\pi\)Quotient
from the left, \(x\to-\frac\pi3^-\)slightly less than \(-\frac\pi3\)slightly negative\(\frac{+}{-}\to-\infty\)
from the right, \(x\to-\frac\pi3^+\)slightly more than \(-\frac\pi3\)slightly positive\(\frac{+}{+}\to+\infty\)

Step 4 · verdict

$$\lim_{x\to-\pi/3^-}\frac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}=-\infty \qquad \lim_{x\to-\pi/3^+}\frac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}=+\infty$$

The one-sided limits differ, so the two-sided limit does not exist. Here you must write DNE, and not an infinity: no single symbol describes the behaviour. This is the difference between (e) and (f), and it is the whole point of having both on the paper.

The two traps, in the order they bite

1. Assuming the numerator is zero. Many students see \(\tan\) of something near \(-2\pi/3\), panic, and guess \(0/0\). Evaluate it: it is \(\sqrt3\). Had you assumed \(\frac00\) you would have gone looking for a cancellation that does not exist, and lost the time as well as the marks.

2. Answering \(+\infty\) because the right side does. Check both sides. The denominator is linear, so the sign flips, so it cannot be a single infinity.

Alternative route · the shift substitution

Put \(u=3x+\pi\), so \(x=\frac{u-\pi}{3}\) and \(2x=\frac{2u}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\). The limit becomes \(\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{2u}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)}{u}\), whose numerator tends to \(\sqrt3\) while the denominator tends to \(0\) and changes sign. Same conclusion, and it makes the "nonzero over zero" structure impossible to misread. This substitution is the workhorse of Family 8 below.

f \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\cos(\pi x)}{(x-2)^2}\) = +∞

Step 1, substitute. The numerator is \(\cos(2\pi)=1\), and the denominator is \(0\). Again \(\frac{1}{0}\): nonzero over zero, so infinite, so the only question is the sign.

Step 2, sign check. The numerator is positive near \(x=2\). The denominator is a square, so it is positive on both sides. The sign never flips.

$$\lim_{x\to2^-}=+\infty,\qquad \lim_{x\to2^+}=+\infty,\qquad \text{so}\quad \lim_{x\to2}\frac{\cos(\pi x)}{(x-2)^2}=+\infty$$

Step 3, how to say it. Both sides agree, so you are entitled to write \(+\infty\). Strictly the limit still fails to exist as a real number, so the complete sentence is: "The limit does not exist. The expression increases without bound from both sides, so \(\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=+\infty\)." Write both halves and you cannot be marked down on either convention.

Why (e) and (f) are both here

They are the same classification, \(\frac{\text{nonzero}}{0}\), with opposite outcomes, and the only thing separating them is the parity of the denominator's power. Even power, no flip, one signed infinity. Odd or linear, flip, DNE.

Nail this pair and you have Family 5, the most common limit family on the whole course.

SECTION 05

The exam-ready write-up

If the sitting is hand-marked, "no credit for unjustified answers" is enforced literally. Each part needs one line naming the reason. These are the sentences, short on purpose.

PartThe sentence that earns the method marks
aSubstituting gives \(\frac00\), an indeterminate form. Factoring, \(\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}=\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)(x^2+x+1)}=\frac{x+1}{x^2+x+1}\) for \(x\neq1\). This is continuous at \(x=1\), so the limit is \(\frac{2}{3}\).
bSubstituting gives \(\frac00\). Using \(1-\cos2x=2\sin^2x\), the expression is \(\frac12\left(\frac{x}{\sin x}\right)^2\). Since \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\to1\), the limit is \(\frac12\).
cThe denominator at \(x=0\) is \(3\neq0\), so the form is not indeterminate. The function is continuous at \(0\), so the limit is the value there, \(\frac{-9}{3}=-3\).
dDividing numerator and denominator by \(x^2\) gives \(\frac{5/x^2-2x}{1+2/x^2}\). As \(x\to\infty\) the terms \(5/x^2\) and \(2/x^2\) tend to \(0\) and the numerator behaves like \(-2x\), which decreases without bound. The limit does not exist; \(\lim=-\infty\).
eAs \(x\to-\frac\pi3\), \(\tan(2x)\to\tan(-\frac{2\pi}{3})=\sqrt3\neq0\) while \(3x+\pi\to0\). Since \(3x+\pi<0\) for \(x<-\frac\pi3\) and \(3x+\pi>0\) for \(x>-\frac\pi3\), the left limit is \(-\infty\) and the right limit is \(+\infty\). The one-sided limits differ, so the limit does not exist.
fAs \(x\to2\), \(\cos(\pi x)\to\cos2\pi=1\neq0\) while \((x-2)^2\to0^+\). The denominator is positive on both sides, so the expression increases without bound from both sides. The limit does not exist as a real number; \(\lim=+\infty\).
Four features make these earn the marks

Each one names the form found on substitution. Each one names the theorem or identity used. Each one that cancels says "for \(x\neq a\)", which is the step being tested. Each infinite one states the sign from each side before drawing a conclusion.

This one habit catches nearly every sign error, and a sign error is the most common way to lose marks on (d), (e) and (f).

Check 2 · the cheap invariants

  • A cancelled fraction must agree with the original at some third point. If \(\frac{x+1}{x^2+x+1}\) is right, then at \(x=2\) it should equal \(\frac{4-1}{8-1}=\frac37\), and it does.
  • A trig limit answer should be a simple rational or a multiple of \(\pi\). Anything stranger and you probably lost a factor of \(k\).
  • For \(x\to\infty\) on a rational function the answer is \(0\), a ratio of coefficients, or \(\pm\infty\). Never anything else. A different-looking number means recount the degrees.
  • Nonzero\(/0\) can never come out finite. If you have "simplified" such a limit to a number, you did algebra in the wrong row of the classification table.

Check 3 · the numbers worth memorising

\(\frac23\approx0.6667\), \(\frac12=0.5\), \(\sqrt3\approx1.7321\), \(\cos2\pi=1\), \(\tan\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\sqrt3\). Knowing that last one by sight removes the only genuinely hard step in the paper.

Read each family as a structure, not a list of facts

5C · the two-stage trap

This one looks like \(\frac00\), cancels once, and then turns out to be nonzero\(/0\).

$$\lim_{x\to-2}\frac{3x^2-2x-16}{(x+2)^2}$$

At \(x=-2\) the numerator is \(12+4-16=0\), so it really is \(\frac00\) and you really may factor: \(3x^2-2x-16=(x+2)(3x-8)\), giving \(\frac{3x-8}{x+2}\). Now substitute again: \(\frac{-14}{0}\). The lesson generalises

After every cancellation, substitute again and re-classify. One round of algebra does not mean one round of classification.

The boundary case is the one worth understanding

SECTION 09

The trap list

Ranked by how often each one actually costs marks.

  1. Reaching for a tool without substituting first. This is part (c), 4 points, and the wrong answer \(-\frac12\) is what a confident student produces. Substitute, always, before anything.
  2. Getting the sign wrong on an infinite limit. Parts (d), (e) and (f) are 12 of the 24 points and every one turns on a sign. Check the sign from each side, in writing.
  3. Saying \(+\infty\) when the answer is DNE, or DNE when a signed infinity is wanted. Even power, no flip, so a signed infinity. Odd or linear, flip, so DNE.
  4. Assuming a trig numerator is \(0\) because the point is not \(0\). In (e), \(\tan(-\frac{2\pi}{3})\) is \(\sqrt3\), not \(0\). Evaluate it, do not assume it.
  5. Not re-classifying after a cancellation. Family 5C looks like \(\frac00\), cancels once, and becomes nonzero\(/0\). One round of algebra, two rounds of classification.
  6. Inverting a trig limit the wrong way. Part (b) is \(\frac12\); the limit of the upside-down version is \(2\).
  7. Forgetting \(\sqrt{x^2}=\lvert x\rvert\) when \(x\to-\infty\). A missing minus sign, every time.
  8. Cancelling in a non-\(\frac00\) form. Illegal, and it produces a finite answer where the truth is infinite.
  9. Using L'Hôpital where the syllabus wants algebra. It may not be examinable at this point in the course, and on a hand-marked paper it may not earn the justification marks even when the number is right.
  10. Answering a bare number on a paper that says "no credit for unjustified answers". One line per part is all it takes.

# Q1.2 - Composite functions and their domains

**The question (MT1 Q2, 6 points).**
Let $f(x) = 2x^2 - 5$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}.$
Find the composite functions and their corresponding domains.

- a. $f\left(g(x)\right)$
- b. $g\left(f(x)\right)$

**The answers.**

$$\text{a.}\quad f(g(x)) = 2\left(\frac{x+5}{2x-9}\right)^{2}-5 = \frac{-18x^{2}+200x-355}{(2x-9)^{2}},
\qquad \text{domain } x\neq\frac92$$

$$\text{b.}\quad g(f(x)) = \frac{2x^{2}}{4x^{2}-19},
\qquad \text{domain } x\neq\pm\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\approx\pm 2.1794494718$$

**What to type into Möbius:**

| part | the function | the domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | `2*(x+5)^2/(2*x-9)^2-5` | `(-infinity,9/2)U(9/2,infinity)` |
| b | `2*x^2/(4*x^2-19)` | `(-infinity,-sqrt(19)/2)U(-sqrt(19)/2,sqrt(19)/2)U(sqrt(19)/2,infinity)` |

> **Erratum you need to know about.**
> The MATH 265 exam note sheets artifact, block A5, prints part b as $\dfrac{2x^{2}}{4x^{2}-59}$ with
> domain $x\neq\pm\frac{\sqrt{59}}{2}$.
> That is wrong, and it is wrong in exactly the way this question is designed to catch.
> $2(2x^{2}-5)-9 = 4x^{2}-10-9 = 4x^{2}-19$, not $4x^{2}-59$.
> The independent check is that the excluded inputs must be the $x$ with $f(x)=\frac92$, and
> $2x^{2}-5=\frac92$ gives $x^{2}=\frac{19}{4}$.
> Do not memorise the note sheet on this one line.

## 0. The 30-second version

Do the domain **before** the algebra, both times.
The algebra destroys the evidence you need for the domain, so collect the evidence first.

```
f(x) = 2x^2 - 5          g(x) = (x+5)/(2x-9)

a)  f(g(x))
  -> DOMAIN FIRST.  g needs 2x - 9 != 0             x != 9/2
  -> f is a polynomial, it swallows any real input  no second condition
  -> now substitute g into EVERY x of f             2((x+5)/(2x-9))^2 - 5
      ANSWER   2(x+5)^2/(2x-9)^2 - 5,   x != 9/2

b)  g(f(x))
  -> DOMAIN FIRST.  f is a polynomial, all reals    no first condition
  -> g's input must dodge 9/2, so f(x) != 9/2       2x^2 - 5 != 9/2
                                                    x^2 != 19/4  ->  x != +-sqrt(19)/2
  -> now substitute f into EVERY x of g             (2x^2-5+5) / (2(2x^2-5)-9)
      ANSWER   2x^2/(4x^2 - 19),   x != +-sqrt(19)/2
```

Four lines each, and neither line needs a calculator.
The only arithmetic in the whole question is $2(2x^{2}-5)-9=4x^{2}-19$, and that single line is where
the marks are lost.

## 1. What this question is really testing

Question 2 is worth 6 of 64 points, and it is two questions stapled together.
The composition itself is substitution and takes fifteen seconds.
The domain is the actual assessment, and it is worth roughly half the marks.

| Reflex | Where it comes back |
| :--- | :--- |
| Composition is substitution into *every* $x$, not multiplication | the chain rule, and every derivative on the paper |
| Reading restrictions off denominators, even roots and logs | Q1.3 "is this a function of $x$", Q1.4 the cone domain, Q4.1 asymptotes |
| The domain is **not** read off the simplified formula | limits with removable holes, continuity, Q3.x |
| Writing a set of reals in interval notation | every domain and every interval answer on both papers |

The instruction is "find the composite functions **and their corresponding domains**".
A perfect composite with no domain is half an answer.
On a hand-marked paper that is three of six points; in Möbius the domain is a separate answer box and
is simply scored zero.

**The one sentence that generates every correct answer.**

> The domain of $f\circ g$ is the set of all $x$ that are in the domain of $g$ **and** whose output
> $g(x)$ is in the domain of $f$.

Two conditions, always, in that order.
Most wrong answers on this question come from checking only one of them, or from checking neither and
instead reading the final formula.

**Format warning.**
The sample paper prints "(6 points)" and reads like a written exam.
The live delivery is Möbius, answer-only and machine-marked, and there the composite and the domain are
separate boxes with separate parsers.
Prepare for both: get the two expressions and the two domains fast, and be able to write the six
justification sentences in Section 5 if the paper you sit asks for method.

## 2. The four things you must have cold

You cannot do this question at speed by rebuilding these under pressure.

### 2.1 The definition, in the only form worth memorising

$$\operatorname{dom}(f\circ g)=\{\,x : x\in\operatorname{dom}(g)\ \textbf{ and }\ g(x)\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\,\}$$

Read it left to right as a pipeline.
The input $x$ has to survive $g$, and then the thing $g$ produced has to survive $f$.
Two gates in series, and a value must pass both.

The order is not symmetric, which is why part a and part b have completely different answers.
In part a the restriction lives in the *inner* function; in part b it lives in the *outer* one.
Nothing about the two functions changed between the parts.
Only the order changed, and the order is the whole question.

### 2.2 The restriction triggers

Four things, and only four things, remove points from a domain in this course.

| What you see | The condition it forces | Bracket at the boundary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a denominator $D(x)$ | $D(x)\neq 0$ | the point is **removed**, always |
| an even root $\sqrt[2n]{R(x)}$ | $R(x)\geq 0$ | **closed**, the boundary is included |
| a logarithm $\ln A(x)$ | $A(x)>0$ | **open**, the boundary is excluded |
| a physical quantity (radius, length, time) | usually $>0$ | open, and stated in words |

Two combinations are worth their own line because they change the bracket.

| Combination | Condition | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{R(x)}}$ | $R(x)>0$, strict | the root is legal at 0 but the denominator is not |
| $\sqrt{\dfrac{N(x)}{D(x)}}$ | $\dfrac{N}{D}\geq 0$ **and** $D\neq 0$ | a sign chart, with a closed bracket at a zero of $N$ and an open one at a zero of $D$ |

An odd root, an absolute value, a polynomial, $\sin$, $\cos$ and $e^{x}$ restrict nothing.
If the question hands you one of those, say "all real numbers" out loud rather than leaving a blank.

### 2.3 Interval notation, written the way Möbius wants it

| The set | Interval notation | Möbius |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| all reals | $(-\infty,\infty)$ | `(-infinity,infinity)` |
| all reals except $\frac92$ | $\left(-\infty,\frac92\right)\cup\left(\frac92,\infty\right)$ | `(-infinity,9/2)U(9/2,infinity)` |
| all reals except $\pm\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$ | $\left(-\infty,-\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right)\cup\left(-\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2},\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right)\cup\left(\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2},\infty\right)$ | three intervals joined by `U` |
| $x\geq 3$ | $[3,\infty)$ | `[3,infinity)` |
| $x>4$ | $(4,\infty)$ | `(4,infinity)` |

Removing $n$ isolated points always produces $n+1$ intervals.
That is a free check on part b: two removed points, so three intervals, and if you wrote two you have
dropped one of the $\pm$.

### 2.4 The three sets, kept separate on the page

Beginners collapse these into one and then cannot say which condition they have already used.

| Set | For Q1.2 | Comment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\operatorname{dom}(f)$ | all reals | $f$ is a polynomial, so it never restricts anything |
| $\operatorname{dom}(g)$ | $x\neq\frac92$ | one denominator, one excluded point |
| $\operatorname{dom}(f\circ g)$ | $x\neq\frac92$ | gate 1 bites, gate 2 does not |
| $\operatorname{dom}(g\circ f)$ | $x\neq\pm\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$ | gate 1 does not bite, gate 2 does |

Write those four lines down before you write any algebra and the question is already finished.

## 3. The universal procedure

This works for every composite-and-domain question on either paper, in any of the families in
Section 8.

> 1. **Name the outer and the inner.** In $f(g(x))$, $g$ runs first and $f$ runs second. Say it out loud, because half of all errors here are the two functions swapped.
> 2. **Gate 1: the inner function's own domain.** Look at $g$ alone and apply the trigger table from 2.2. Write the condition on $x$.
> 3. **Gate 2: the outer function's requirement, expressed as a condition on the inner's output.** Look at $f$ alone and find what its input is not allowed to be. Then set $g(x)$ equal to that forbidden value and solve for $x$. Every solution is removed.
> 4. **Intersect.** The domain is everything satisfying gate 1 and gate 2 at once. Now write it in interval notation and count the pieces.
> 5. **Only now, substitute.** Replace every $x$ in the outer function with the whole inner expression, in brackets. Simplify only as far as the answer form asks.
> 6. **Check by an independent evaluation.** Pick one easy $x$ inside your domain. Compute the composite the long way, $g$ first and then $f$. Compute it again from your simplified formula. They must agree.

The load-bearing word is **only** in step 5.
Simplification cancels factors, and a cancelled factor is a removed point that has vanished from the
page.
If you do the algebra first you will read the domain off a formula that no longer remembers the
restriction.

**Classify before you start.**
One glance at where the ugliness lives tells you which gate will bite.

| $f$ (outer) | $g$ (inner) | Which gate bites | Family |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| polynomial | polynomial | neither, domain is all reals | 0 |
| polynomial | rational, root or log | gate 1 only | 1 |
| rational, root or log | polynomial | gate 2 only | 2 |
| rational | rational | both, and one exclusion hides after simplifying | 3 |
| polynomial | even root | gate 1, and the simplified formula loses it entirely | 4 |
| even root | polynomial | gate 2, and it is an inequality not an exclusion | 5 |
| log | anything | gate 2, strict inequality | 7 |

Q1.2a is row 2 of that table.
Q1.2b is row 3.
The examiner chose one function of each type precisely so that one part exercises each gate.

## 4. Q1.2 worked, several ways

### Part a - $f(g(x))$

**Gate 1.**
$g(x)=\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$ has one denominator, so $2x-9\neq 0$, so $x\neq\dfrac92$.

**Gate 2.**
$f(x)=2x^{2}-5$ is a polynomial.
A polynomial accepts every real number, so $\operatorname{dom}(f)=\mathbb{R}$ and there is nothing for
$g(x)$ to dodge.
Gate 2 is vacuous.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{dom}(f\circ g)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}: x\neq\tfrac92\right\}
=\left(-\infty,\tfrac92\right)\cup\left(\tfrac92,\infty\right)}$$

**Now the algebra.**
Substitute the whole of $g$ into the single $x$ that appears in $f$:

$$f(g(x))=2\big(g(x)\big)^{2}-5=2\left(\frac{x+5}{2x-9}\right)^{2}-5
=\frac{2(x+5)^{2}}{(2x-9)^{2}}-5$$

That is a complete and acceptable answer.
If the question, or the Möbius box, wants a single fraction, put it over the common denominator
$(2x-9)^{2}$:

$$f(g(x))=\frac{2(x+5)^{2}-5(2x-9)^{2}}{(2x-9)^{2}}$$

$$2(x+5)^{2}=2\left(x^{2}+10x+25\right)=2x^{2}+20x+50$$
$$5(2x-9)^{2}=5\left(4x^{2}-36x+81\right)=20x^{2}-180x+405$$
$$2x^{2}+20x+50-\left(20x^{2}-180x+405\right)=-18x^{2}+200x-355$$

$$f(g(x))=\frac{-18x^{2}+200x-355}{(2x-9)^{2}},\qquad x\neq\frac92$$

Both forms are the same function and Möbius grades by mathematical equivalence, so either is accepted.

### Part b - $g(f(x))$

**Gate 1.**
$f(x)=2x^{2}-5$ is a polynomial, defined for every real $x$.
Gate 1 is vacuous.

**Gate 2.**
$g$ has a denominator, so $g$ refuses the input $\frac92$.
Its input here is $f(x)$, so the condition is $f(x)\neq\frac92$:

$$2x^{2}-5\neq\frac92 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2x^{2}\neq\frac{19}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\;
x^{2}\neq\frac{19}{4} \;\Longrightarrow\; x\neq\pm\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$$

$$\boxed{\operatorname{dom}(g\circ f)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}: x\neq\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right\}
=\left(-\infty,-\tfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right)\cup\left(-\tfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right)
\cup\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2},\infty\right)}$$

**Now the algebra.**
Substitute $f$ into every $x$ of $g$.
There are two of them, one on top and one underneath, and both must be replaced:

$$g(f(x))=\frac{f(x)+5}{2f(x)-9}=\frac{\left(2x^{2}-5\right)+5}{2\left(2x^{2}-5\right)-9}
=\frac{2x^{2}}{4x^{2}-10-9}=\frac{2x^{2}}{4x^{2}-19},\qquad x\neq\pm\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$$

The numerator collapses beautifully, $-5+5=0$, which is a deliberate gift from the examiner and also a
distraction: the collapse happens on top while the mark is on the bottom.

**The second, independent route to the same domain.**
Because nothing cancelled here, you can also set the *composite's* denominator to zero:

$$4x^{2}-19=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{2}=\frac{19}{4} \;\Longrightarrow\; x=\pm\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$$

Same answer, and the agreement of two independent routes is a real check.
But understand why this route is only safe here: no factor cancelled between the substitution and the
final form, so the final form still remembers every restriction.
In Family 3 and Family 4 it does not, and this route silently gives the wrong domain.

### The route comparison

Two things can vary: how you get the domain, and how far you push the algebra.

**Getting the domain.**

| | Route A: two gates from the definition | Route B: read the final formula |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Works on part a | yes | yes |
| Works on part b | yes | yes, by luck |
| Works when a factor cancels | yes | **no**, silently |
| Works with roots inside | yes | **no**, the interval vanishes |
| Lines of work | 2 | 1 |
| Verdict | **use this one always** | fine as a cross-check, never as the method |

**Doing the algebra for part a.**

| | Route 1: leave it stacked | Route 2: single fraction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| What you write | $\dfrac{2(x+5)^{2}}{(2x-9)^{2}}-5$ | $\dfrac{-18x^{2}+200x-355}{(2x-9)^{2}}$ |
| Lines of work | 1 | 4 |
| Where errors hide | nowhere | expanding $5(2x-9)^{2}$, and the sign of the whole bracket when you subtract it |
| Accepted by Möbius | yes | yes |
| Verdict | **use this under pressure** | only if the question says "as a single fraction" |

Route 2 costs three extra lines and adds two places to drop a sign, for zero extra marks.
The instruction was "find the composite function", and the stacked form *is* the composite function.

**Under exam pressure: gates first, then substitute, then stop.**
Do not simplify past what was asked.

## 5. The exam-ready write-up

If the paper you sit asks for method, these are the sentences that earn the marks.
Copy the shape, not just the numbers.

> 1. The domain of $g$ requires $2x-9\neq 0$, so $x\neq\dfrac92$.
> 2. $f$ is a polynomial, so $\operatorname{dom}(f)=\mathbb{R}$ and $g(x)$ is always an acceptable input to $f$.
> 3. Therefore $f(g(x))=2\left(\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}\right)^{2}-5$ with domain $\left(-\infty,\dfrac92\right)\cup\left(\dfrac92,\infty\right)$.
> 4. For $g\circ f$: $f$ is a polynomial so it imposes no restriction, but $g$ is undefined when its input equals $\dfrac92$.
> 5. Solving $f(x)=\dfrac92$ gives $2x^{2}-5=\dfrac92$, hence $x^{2}=\dfrac{19}{4}$ and $x=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$; these are excluded.
> 6. Therefore $g(f(x))=\dfrac{2x^{2}}{4x^{2}-19}$ with domain $\left(-\infty,-\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right)\cup\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2},\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\right)\cup\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2},\infty\right)$.

Three things a marker looks for that students routinely skip.
**Naming** which function is inner and which is outer.
**Stating** explicitly that the polynomial imposes no restriction, rather than silently not mentioning
it, because silence reads as "did not check".
**Deriving** the part b exclusion from $f(x)\neq\frac92$ rather than from the final denominator, since
that is the step the question is actually assessing.

## 6. Verify before you commit

This takes about fifteen seconds and it catches every error this question can produce.

**The single-point check, which is the important one.**
Pick an easy $x$ inside your claimed domain and compute the composite twice, by two different roads.

Take $x=1$ for part a.
The long way: $g(1)=\dfrac{6}{-7}=-\dfrac67$, then $f\!\left(-\dfrac67\right)=2\cdot\dfrac{36}{49}-5
=\dfrac{72-245}{49}=-\dfrac{173}{49}\approx-3.5306$.
From the formula: $\dfrac{-18+200-355}{(2-9)^{2}}=\dfrac{-173}{49}$.
Agreement, so the substitution and the expansion are both right.

Take $x=3$ for part b.
The long way: $f(3)=13$, then $g(13)=\dfrac{18}{26-9}=\dfrac{18}{17}\approx1.0588$.
From the formula: $\dfrac{2\cdot 9}{36-19}=\dfrac{18}{17}$.
Agreement.

If you only do one check on this question, do this one.
It independently confirms the algebra, and it costs two substitutions.

**Confirm each excluded point actually breaks something.**
An exclusion you cannot justify is an exclusion you invented.

- Part a: at $x=\frac92$ the inner denominator $2x-9$ is $0$, so $g\!\left(\frac92\right)$ does not exist and neither can $f(g(\frac92))$.
- Part b: at $x=\frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$, $f(x)=2\cdot\frac{19}{4}-5=\frac{19}{2}-5=\frac92$, which is exactly the input $g$ refuses.

**Count the intervals.**
$n$ removed points gives $n+1$ intervals.
Part a removes one point and has two intervals.
Part b removes two points and has three intervals.
If you wrote two intervals for part b you dropped a $\pm$.

**The end behaviour check, which also answers the asymptote follow-up.**
As $x\to\infty$, $g(x)\to\frac12$, so $f(g(x))\to f\!\left(\frac12\right)=2\cdot\frac14-5=-\frac92$.
The formula agrees: the ratio of leading coefficients is $\frac{-18}{4}=-\frac92$.
As $x\to\infty$, $f(x)\to\infty$, so $g(f(x))\to\frac12$, and the formula gives $\frac{2}{4}=\frac12$.
In both cases the composite's horizontal asymptote is the outer function evaluated at the inner
function's limit, which is a check you can do in your head.

**The numbers worth memorising for this question.**

$$\frac92=4.5,\qquad \frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}\approx 2.1794,\qquad
\frac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\approx 1.5811,\qquad \frac{91}{16}=5.6875$$

The first two are Q1.2's own exclusions.
The third is the boundary in every root and log variation built on $2x^{2}-5$.
The fourth is the second exclusion of $g\circ g$, which is the most likely harder reworking of this
question.

## 7. Answer-entry rules

Möbius is answer-only and machine-marked, and this question has *four* answer boxes: two functions and
two domains.
Grading is by mathematical equivalence, so a correct answer is never rejected for its form, only for
being **parsed** differently than you intended.

| Rule | Do | Not |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Multiplication | `2*x` | `2x` |
| Powers | `(2*x-9)^2` | `2*x-9^2` |
| Whole denominator bracketed | `2*x^2/(4*x^2-19)` | `2*x^2/4*x^2-19` |
| Whole numerator bracketed | `(x+5)/(2*x-9)` | `x+5/2*x-9` |
| Exact endpoints | `sqrt(19)/2` | `2.179` |
| Infinity | `infinity` | `inf`, `oo`, `∞` |
| Union | `U` between intervals | a comma, or the word "or" |
| Natural log | `ln(x)` | `log(x)` |

**The single most expensive typo on this question is `2*x^2/4*x^2-19`.**
It parses left to right as $\dfrac{2x^{2}}{4}\cdot x^{2}-19=\dfrac{x^{4}}{2}-19$.
At $x=1$ that is $-18.5$ where the true value is $-\dfrac{2}{15}\approx-0.1333$; at $x=3$ it is $21.5$
where the true value is $\dfrac{18}{17}\approx1.0588$.
Nothing about the output looks like a typo, which is why it costs the whole mark.
Bracket the entire denominator, every time.

**Domain boxes.**
If the box wants interval notation, give it; if it wants an inequality, `x != 9/2` is usually accepted;
if it is a text box, write the words.
Read the box's own hint text before typing, because Möbius domain boxes differ between questions.

**Always click the preview icon.**
An empty preview modal means broken syntax, not wrong mathematics.
For part b these are all accepted as equivalent, so pick the one you can type without a typo:
`2*x^2/(4*x^2-19)`, `(2*x^2)/(4*x^2-19)`, `2*x^2/((2*x-sqrt(19))*(2*x+sqrt(19)))`.

## 8. The variation catalog

This is the part that makes the question repeatable.
The families are organised by **which gate bites and what kind of condition it produces**, not by what
the functions look like.
Everything below has been numerically verified against a direct evaluation of the composition.

Read the whole catalog as one idea seen eleven times: *gate 1 and gate 2, then substitute*.
Only three things ever vary between families.
Which gate is non-vacuous, whether the condition is an exclusion or an inequality, and whether the
simplified formula still shows the restriction.

### Family 0 - polynomial into polynomial: no restriction at all

**Trigger.** Both functions are polynomials, or both are $\sin$, $\cos$, $e^{x}$, $|x|$, odd roots.
**Tool.** None. Substitute and write "all real numbers".
**The trap.** Leaving the domain blank because there was nothing to exclude. A blank scores zero; "all real numbers" scores full.

| composite | simplified | domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $f(f(x))$ with $f=2x^{2}-5$ | $8x^{4}-40x^{2}+45$ | $(-\infty,\infty)$ |
| $F(G(x))$, $F=x^{2}+1$, $G=3x-2$ | $9x^{2}-12x+5$ | $(-\infty,\infty)$ |
| $G(F(x))$, same pair | $3x^{2}+1$ | $(-\infty,\infty)$ |

Note the second and third rows: same two functions, different order, genuinely different functions.
That is the cheapest possible demonstration that composition does not commute, and it is worth doing
once so the fact stops feeling like a rule you were told.

### Family 1 - polynomial outside, restricted inside: gate 1 only

**Trigger.** The outer function is a polynomial; the inner one has a denominator, a root or a log.
**Tool.** Gate 1 alone. The inner function's own domain **is** the composite's domain.
**This is Q1.2 part a.**

| $f$ (outer) | $g$ (inner) | $f(g(x))$ | domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $2x^{2}-5$ | $\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$ | $\dfrac{2(x+5)^{2}}{(2x-9)^{2}}-5$ | $x\neq\dfrac92$ |
| $x^{2}+3$ | $\dfrac{1}{x-4}$ | $\dfrac{1}{(x-4)^{2}}+3$ | $x\neq 4$ |
| $3x+1$ | $\dfrac{x}{x+2}$ | $\dfrac{4x+2}{x+2}$ | $x\neq -2$ |

Read the table as structure: the domain column is a copy of the inner function's denominator condition,
every time, with nothing added.
The outer polynomial contributes exactly nothing, and saying so explicitly is a mark.

### Family 2 - restricted outside, polynomial inside: gate 2 only

**Trigger.** The outer function has the denominator, root or log; the inner one is a polynomial.
**Tool.** Gate 2. Take the outer function's forbidden input value, set the *inner formula* equal to it, and solve for $x$.
**This is Q1.2 part b, and it is the harder half.**

The step students skip is that the condition is not a condition on $x$ directly.
It is a condition on $g(x)$ that you then have to *pull back* through the inner formula.

| $f$ (outer) | $g$ (inner) | composite | forbidden input to $f$ | solve | domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$ | $2x^{2}-5$ | $\dfrac{2x^{2}}{4x^{2}-19}$ | $\dfrac92$ | $2x^{2}-5=\dfrac92$ | $x\neq\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{x-3}$ | $x^{2}-1$ | $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}-4}$ | $3$ | $x^{2}-1=3$ | $x\neq\pm2$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{2x-5}$ | $x+1$ | $\dfrac{1}{2x-3}$ | $\dfrac52$ | $x+1=\dfrac52$ | $x\neq\dfrac32$ |

Notice the sign pattern.
When the inner function is **even** in $x$, as $2x^{2}-5$ and $x^{2}-1$ are, the pullback is a square
equation and you get a $\pm$ pair, so two points come out and three intervals go in.
When the inner function is linear you get exactly one point and two intervals.
Counting the intervals before you write them is therefore a check, not an afterthought.

### Family 3 - restricted into restricted: two gates, and one exclusion goes invisible

**Trigger.** Both functions have a denominator, a root or a log.
**Tool.** Both gates, and then you must *not* simplify your way out of the answer.
**This is the family that punishes Route B from Section 4.**

The exam's own two functions give the cleanest example.
Take $g\circ g$ with $g(x)=\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$.

Gate 1: $x\neq\dfrac92$.
Gate 2: $g(x)\neq\dfrac92$, so $\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}=\dfrac92$ gives $2(x+5)=9(2x-9)$, then
$2x+10=18x-81$, then $16x=91$, so $x\neq\dfrac{91}{16}=5.6875$.

Now the algebra:

$$g(g(x))=\frac{\frac{x+5}{2x-9}+5}{2\cdot\frac{x+5}{2x-9}-9}
=\frac{\frac{x+5+5(2x-9)}{2x-9}}{\frac{2(x+5)-9(2x-9)}{2x-9}}
=\frac{11x-40}{-16x+91}=\frac{11x-40}{91-16x}$$

$$\operatorname{dom}(g\circ g)=\left\{x: x\neq\tfrac92 \text{ and } x\neq\tfrac{91}{16}\right\}$$

Here is the trap in one line.
The simplified formula $\dfrac{11x-40}{91-16x}$ is **perfectly well behaved at $x=\frac92$**, where it
evaluates to $\dfrac{9.5}{19}=\dfrac12$.
There is no denominator zero there, no warning, nothing on the page.
Route B reads the formula, sees only $91-16x=0$, and reports one exclusion instead of two.
The $\frac92$ was removed by gate 1 and then erased by the cancellation of $(2x-9)$ from top and bottom.

Two more instances where the erasure is even more complete.

| $f$ | $g$ | simplified $f(g(x))$ | domain | what the formula shows |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\dfrac1x$ | $\dfrac{1}{x-2}$ | $x-2$ | $x\neq 2$ | nothing at all |
| $\dfrac{1}{x-1}$ | $\dfrac1x$ | $\dfrac{x}{1-x}$ | $x\neq 0$ and $x\neq 1$ | only $x\neq1$ |

Row 1 is worth staring at.
The composite of two rational functions simplifies to a *straight line*, and yet the point $x=2$ is
genuinely not in its domain, because $g(2)$ does not exist and there is nothing for $f$ to receive.
A graph of the composite is the line $y=x-2$ with a hole punched at $(2,0)$.

### Family 4 - even root inside: the domain becomes an interval and the formula loses it

**Trigger.** The inner function is an even root.
**Tool.** Gate 1, which is now an inequality rather than an exclusion.
**Why it is dangerous.** Squaring the root during simplification destroys every trace of the condition.

| $f$ | $g$ | simplified $f(g(x))$ | domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $2x^{2}-5$ | $\sqrt{x-3}$ | $2x-11$ | $[3,\infty)$ |
| $x^{2}$ | $\sqrt{x}$ | $x$ | $[0,\infty)$ |
| $x^{2}$ | $\sqrt{x-2}$ | $x-2$ | $[2,\infty)$ |

Row 1: $f(\sqrt{x-3})=2\left(\sqrt{x-3}\right)^{2}-5=2(x-3)-5=2x-11$.
The answer is a straight line whose domain is a half-line.
Nothing in "$2x-11$" hints at $x\geq 3$, and a student who simplifies first will confidently write "all
real numbers" and lose the domain mark on a part they got algebraically right.
Row 2 is the classic statement that $\left(\sqrt{x}\right)^{2}=x$ **only for $x\geq0$**, which is the
same fact wearing different clothes.

### Family 5 - even root outside: gate 2 as an inequality

**Trigger.** The outer function is an even root.
**Tool.** Gate 2, solved as an inequality: set the inner formula $\geq 0$.
**Bracket rule.** Closed at every boundary, because $\sqrt{0}=0$ is legal.

| $f$ | $g$ | composite | condition | domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\sqrt{x}$ | $2x^{2}-5$ | $\sqrt{2x^{2}-5}$ | $2x^{2}-5\geq 0$ | $\left(-\infty,-\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\right]\cup\left[\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2},\infty\right)$ |
| $\sqrt{x-1}$ | $x^{2}$ | $\sqrt{x^{2}-1}$ | $x^{2}\geq1$ | $(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)$ |
| $\sqrt{x}$ | $x-4$ | $\sqrt{x-4}$ | $x\geq 4$ | $[4,\infty)$ |

$\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\approx1.5811$, from $2x^{2}=5$, so $x^{2}=\dfrac52$ and $x=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac52}
=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$.

Compare rows 1 and 2 with Family 2: the same even inner function, the same $\pm$ structure, but the
answer is now two closed rays instead of three open intervals.
The shape of the answer is decided entirely by whether the outer function *forbids one value*
(a denominator) or *forbids a half-line* (an even root).

### Family 6 - a root and a rational together: you need a sign chart

**Trigger.** A quotient underneath an even root, or a root in a denominator.
**Tool.** A sign chart, plus a separate exclusion for the denominator's own zero.
**Bracket rule.** Closed at a zero of the numerator, open at a zero of the denominator, always.

Take $\sqrt{g(x)}=\sqrt{\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}}$ with the exam's own $g$.

The quotient changes sign only at $x=-5$ (numerator zero) and $x=\dfrac92$ (denominator zero).
Test one point in each of the three regions:

| region | test $x$ | $\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$ | sign |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $x<-5$ | $-9$ | $\dfrac{-4}{-27}$ | $+$ |
| $-5<x<\dfrac92$ | $0$ | $\dfrac{5}{-9}$ | $-$ |
| $x>\dfrac92$ | $5$ | $\dfrac{10}{1}$ | $+$ |

$$\operatorname{dom}\left(\sqrt{g(x)}\right)=(-\infty,-5]\cup\left(\tfrac92,\infty\right)$$

The bracket asymmetry is the entire content of this family.
At $x=-5$ the quotient is $0$, and $\sqrt{0}$ is fine, so $-5$ is **in**, closed bracket.
At $x=\frac92$ the quotient does not exist at all, so $\frac92$ is **out**, open bracket.
Writing $[-5,\ldots]$ and $[\frac92,\ldots]$ with the same bracket is a guaranteed lost mark.

| composite | condition | domain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\sqrt{\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}}$ | quotient $\geq 0$, denominator $\neq 0$ | $(-\infty,-5]\cup\left(\dfrac92,\infty\right)$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x-4}}$ | $x-4>0$, strict | $(4,\infty)$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x^{2}-5}}$ | $2x^{2}-5>0$, strict | $\left(-\infty,-\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\right)\cup\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2},\infty\right)$ |

Rows 2 and 3 are Family 5's expressions with the root moved into a denominator, and every bracket
flipped from closed to open as a result.
That is the cheapest way to test whether you understand the difference between $\geq$ and $>$ here.

### Family 7 - logarithm outside: gate 2 with a strict inequality

**Trigger.** $\ln$ or $\log$ as the outer function.
**Tool.** Gate 2 with $>0$ instead of $\geq 0$.
**The whole family in one sentence.** It is Family 5 with every bracket opened.

| composite | condition | domain | compare with Family 5 or 6 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\ln\left(2x^{2}-5\right)$ | $2x^{2}-5>0$ | $\left(-\infty,-\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\right)\cup\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2},\infty\right)$ | Family 5 row 1, brackets now open |
| $\ln\left(\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}\right)$ | quotient $>0$ | $(-\infty,-5)\cup\left(\dfrac92,\infty\right)$ | Family 6 row 1, $-5$ now excluded |
| $\ln(x-4)$ | $x>4$ | $(4,\infty)$ | Family 5 row 3, $4$ now excluded |

Row 2 is the sharpest test in the whole catalog.
The set is identical to Family 6 row 1 except for a single bracket at $x=-5$, and the reason is that
$\sqrt{0}$ exists while $\ln 0$ does not.
If you can produce both answers correctly from the same $g$, you understand the trigger table.

### Family 8 - a domain declared in the question

**Trigger.** The question states a domain, for example "let $f(x)=2x^{2}-5$ for $x\geq 0$".
**Tool.** Gate 2, but the forbidden set now comes from the *stated* domain rather than from the formula.

With $f$ restricted to $[0,\infty)$ and $g(x)=\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$, the composite $f(g(x))$ needs
$g(x)\geq 0$ as well as $x\neq\dfrac92$.
That is exactly the sign chart from Family 6:

$$\operatorname{dom}(f\circ g)=(-\infty,-5]\cup\left(\tfrac92,\infty\right)$$

Compare that with Q1.2 part a, where the same two functions gave $x\neq\frac92$ and nothing else.
One extra clause in the question turned a single removed point into two removed rays.
This is the cheapest way for the examiner to make part a genuinely hard without changing a symbol of
the algebra, so read the question's preamble before you start.

### Family 9 - three functions deep, and composition run backwards

**Trigger.** $f(g(h(x)))$, or an instruction to write a given $h$ as a composite.
**Tool.** The same two gates, applied one layer at a time from the inside out.

Forwards, with $h(x)=x-3$ and the exam's $f$ and $g$:

$$f(g(h(x)))=2\left(\frac{(x-3)+5}{2(x-3)-9}\right)^{2}-5=2\left(\frac{x+2}{2x-15}\right)^{2}-5$$

Gate on $h$: none, it is a polynomial.
Gate on $g$: its input $h(x)$ must dodge $\dfrac92$, so $x-3\neq\dfrac92$, so $x\neq\dfrac{15}{2}$.
Gate on $f$: none.
Domain: $x\neq\dfrac{15}{2}$, which the simplified denominator $2x-15$ confirms.

Backwards, the decomposition question.
Given $h$, produce $f$ and $g$ with $h=f\circ g$, neither of them the identity.

| given $h(x)$ | a valid $g$ (inner) | a valid $f$ (outer) | domain of $h$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3x+1}}$ | $3x+1$ | $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ | $\left(-\dfrac13,\infty\right)$ |
| $2\left(\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}\right)^{2}-5$ | $\dfrac{x+5}{2x-9}$ | $2x^{2}-5$ | $x\neq\dfrac92$ |
| $\sqrt{2x^{2}-5}$ | $2x^{2}-5$ | $\sqrt{x}$ | $\left(-\infty,-\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\right]\cup\left[\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2},\infty\right)$ |

The rule for decomposing: **the inner function is whatever is inside the outermost operation**.
Find the last thing you would do if you were evaluating $h$ by hand, and that operation is $f$.
Row 2 is just Q1.2 part a read right to left, which is worth noticing.

### Family 10 - questions stacked on top of the composite

**Trigger.** The composite is not the answer; it is the setup for a second question.
**Tool.** Build the composite and its domain first, then answer the real question.

| Question | Answer | How |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $(f\circ g)(0)$ | $-\dfrac{355}{81}\approx-4.3827$ | $g(0)=-\dfrac59$, then $f\!\left(-\dfrac59\right)$ |
| $(g\circ f)(0)$ | $0$ | $f(0)=-5$, then $g(-5)=\dfrac{0}{-19}$ |
| $(f\circ g)(1)$ | $-\dfrac{173}{49}\approx-3.5306$ | $g(1)=-\dfrac67$ |
| $(g\circ f)(3)$ | $\dfrac{18}{17}\approx1.0588$ | $f(3)=13$ |
| $(f\circ g)(2)$ | $-\dfrac{27}{25}=-1.08$ | $g(2)=-\dfrac75$ |
| $(g\circ f)(2)$ | $-\dfrac83\approx-2.6667$ | $f(2)=3$, and note it differs from the row above |
| Is $\dfrac92$ in $\operatorname{dom}(f\circ g)$? | no | $g$ is undefined there |
| Is $\dfrac92$ in $\operatorname{dom}(g\circ f)$? | **yes** | $f(4.5)=35.5$, which $g$ accepts happily |
| Zeros of $f(g(x))$ | $x=\dfrac{100\pm19\sqrt{10}}{18}\approx2.2176,\ 8.8935$ | set $-18x^{2}+200x-355=0$ |
| Zeros of $g(f(x))$ | $x=0$ | set $2x^{2}=0$ |
| Vertical asymptotes of $f\circ g$ | $x=\dfrac92$ | numerator there is $180.5\neq0$, so it is a true asymptote and not a hole |
| Horizontal asymptote of $f\circ g$ | $y=-\dfrac92$ | $=f\!\left(\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)\right)=f\!\left(\dfrac12\right)$ |
| Horizontal asymptote of $g\circ f$ | $y=\dfrac12$ | $=\lim_{u\to\infty}g(u)$ |
| Does $f\circ g=g\circ f$? | no | one counterexample is enough: $(f\circ g)(2)=-1.08$ but $(g\circ f)(2)=-\dfrac83$ |

The two rows about $\frac92$ deserve attention.
The same number is forbidden in one composite and perfectly fine in the other, which is the strongest
possible illustration that the order matters and that $\frac92$ is not some property of the pair of
functions.
It is a property of $g$'s *input*, and in $g\circ f$ nothing feeds $\frac92$ into $g$ when $x=\frac92$.

The asymptote rows connect this question to Q4.1.
Once you have the composite as a single rational function, every asymptote technique from that topic
applies unchanged, and the "outer function evaluated at the inner function's limit" shortcut lets you
predict the horizontal asymptote before you simplify.

## 9. The trap list

Ranked by how often they actually cost marks.

1. **Reading the domain off the simplified formula.** This is the number one error and the whole reason the question exists. Simplification cancels factors, and each cancelled factor is a removed point that no longer appears. Family 3 and Family 4 are built to punish it.
2. **Botching $2(2x^{2}-5)-9$.** The coefficient must distribute over both terms: $4x^{2}-10$, and then $-9$ gives $4x^{2}-19$. Multiplying only the $x^{2}$ term, or subtracting in the wrong order, produces $4x^{2}-59$ or $4x^{2}+1$ or $4x^{2}-1$. The published note sheet gets this wrong, so do not trust a remembered answer here; do the two-term distribution on paper.
3. **Composing in the wrong order.** $f(g(x))$ means $g$ runs first. Half of all lost marks on this question are the two parts swapped, which produces two correct functions attached to two wrong labels.
4. **Multiplying instead of substituting.** $f(g(x))$ is not $f(x)\cdot g(x)$. Substitution replaces *every* $x$, including the ones in a denominator, as in part b where there were two.
5. **Losing the $\pm$.** When the pullback in gate 2 is a square, two values come out. Part b has two exclusions and therefore three intervals; writing two intervals means you dropped one.
6. **Giving the composite but not the domain.** The instruction says "and their corresponding domains". In Möbius that is a separate box and an unanswered box is zero.
7. **A decimal endpoint.** $2.179$ is not $\dfrac{\sqrt{19}}{2}$. Give exact endpoints unless asked to round.
8. **Wrong bracket at a boundary.** Closed for an even root, open for a log, open for anything in a denominator. Family 6 row 1 and Family 7 row 2 differ *only* in this and are otherwise the same set.
9. **Assuming $f\circ g=g\circ f$.** They almost never agree, and this question is an explicit demonstration that they do not.
10. **Forgetting the inner function's own domain when the outer one looks harmless.** A polynomial outer function contributes nothing, which tempts you to conclude there is no restriction at all. Gate 1 still applies. This is exactly part a.
11. **Not bracketing the denominator on entry.** `2*x^2/4*x^2-19` parses as $\frac{x^{4}}{2}-19$ and looks like a plausible answer.
12. **Cancelling a factor and calling the result the same function.** $\dfrac{(x-2)(x+1)}{x-2}$ and $x+1$ are different functions with different domains, and this course cares about the difference.

Mark yourself on the **domain** separately from the **composite**.

If the composites are wrong, you are substituting carelessly, and the fix is to write brackets around

Mark the **brackets** as a third, separate score on questions 3, 4, 9, 11 and 12.
An answer that is right except for a square bracket is a specific, fixable error and it comes from the
trigger table in 2.2, not from anything about composition.

## 11. The one-page decision tree

```
Composite f(g(x)), and its domain?
|
+-- STEP 1. Name them. In f(g(x)), g runs FIRST, f runs SECOND.
|            Write the two formulas on separate lines before anything else.
|
+-- STEP 2. GATE 1 - the inner function's own domain. Look at g ALONE.
|            denominator D(x)   -> D(x) != 0
|            even root  sqrt(R) -> R(x) >= 0        [CLOSED bracket]
|            log        ln(A)   -> A(x) >  0        [OPEN bracket]
|            polynomial, |x|, sin, cos, e^x, odd root -> NO condition
|
+-- STEP 3. GATE 2 - the outer function's requirement, pulled back through g.
|            Ask: what input does f refuse?
|              f rational  -> its denominator's zero, call it c
|                             SOLVE g(x) = c, remove every solution
|              f even root -> SOLVE g(x) >= 0   (sign chart if g is a quotient)
|              f log       -> SOLVE g(x) >  0
|              f polynomial-> nothing, say so out loud
|            Even inner function -> expect a +- pair of solutions.
|
+-- STEP 4. INTERSECT gate 1 and gate 2. Write interval notation.
|            n removed points -> n+1 intervals. COUNT THEM.
|
+-- STEP 5. ONLY NOW substitute g into EVERY x of f, in brackets.
|            Simplify only as far as the question asked.
|            Do NOT go back and re-read the domain off this formula.
|
+-- STEP 6. CHECK. Pick one easy x inside the domain.
             Compute g(x), then f of that.  Compute your formula at the same x.
             They must agree.
             Then confirm each excluded x really breaks something.
             Then type it, bracket the whole denominator, and PREVIEW.
```

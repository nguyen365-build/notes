# Q3.2 - Six limits, with justification required

**The question (MT2 Q7, 24 points).**
Evaluate each of the limits given below.
If a limit does not exist, explain why.
**Note: No credit will be given for unjustified answers.**

- a. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1}\frac{x^2 - 1}{x^3 - 1}$
- b. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{x^2}{1 - \cos(2x)}$
- c. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{6x - 9}{x^3 - 12x + 3}$
- d. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty}\frac{5 - 2x^3}{x^2 + 2}$
- e. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\pi/3}\frac{\tan(2x)}{3x + \pi}$
- f. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2}\frac{\cos(\pi x)}{(x-2)^2}$

**The answers.**

| | limit | value | why, in one phrase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}$ at $1$ | $\dfrac23 \approx 0.6667$ | $0/0$, factor and cancel |
| b | $\dfrac{x^2}{1-\cos 2x}$ at $0$ | $\dfrac12 = 0.5$ | $0/0$, $1-\cos2x = 2\sin^2x$ |
| c | $\dfrac{6x-9}{x^3-12x+3}$ at $0$ | $-3$ | **not** indeterminate, substitute |
| d | $\dfrac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}$ at $\infty$ | $-\infty$ (DNE) | numerator degree is higher |
| e | $\dfrac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}$ at $-\pi/3$ | **DNE** | nonzero$/0$, and the sign flips |
| f | $\dfrac{\cos\pi x}{(x-2)^2}$ at $2$ | $+\infty$ (DNE) | nonzero$/0$, even power, no flip |

## 0. The 30-second version

You do not "do" a limit.
You **classify** it, and the classification names the tool.

```
lim f(x) as x -> a
  -> 1. SUBSTITUTE a.  Do not simplify first.  Just look at what you get.
  -> 2. READ THE SYMBOL you got:
           a number      -> that IS the answer                  (done)
           0/0           -> indeterminate: factor / conjugate / trig identity
           nonzero / 0   -> INFINITE: do a one-sided SIGN check
           x -> infinity -> compare the leading terms only
  -> 3. Run the one tool that symbol named.
  -> 4. State the verdict AND the reason, because unjustified = zero.
```

Step 1 is the step everybody skips, and it is the step that decides everything.
Part (c) of this very question is worth 4 points and is over the instant you substitute.

## 2. The four things you must have cold

### 2.1 The classification table

This is the whole question.
Substitute, then find your row.

| What substitution gives you | Name | What it means | Tool |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a finite number | determinate | the function is continuous there | **you are done** |
| $\dfrac{0}{\text{nonzero}}$ | determinate | $=0$ | **you are done, the answer is 0** |
| $\dfrac{\text{nonzero}}{0}$ | **infinite** | a vertical asymptote | one-sided sign check |
| $\dfrac{0}{0}$ | **indeterminate** | unknown until you do algebra | factor / conjugate / identity |
| $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$, $x\to\pm\infty$ | indeterminate | unknown until you compare growth | leading terms |
| bounded $\times$ something $\to 0$ | - | pinned to 0 | squeeze theorem |

**"Indeterminate" and "undefined" are not the same word, and the difference is 4 marks.**
$\frac00$ is *indeterminate*: it tells you nothing yet, so you keep working.
$\frac{5}{0}$ is *undefined*, and that is informative: the limit is infinite, so you stop doing
algebra and start checking signs.
Cancelling is only legal in the $\frac00$ row.

### 2.2 The three trig limits, and the only form worth memorising

$$\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1
\qquad
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin(kx)}{x}=k
\qquad
\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos(kx)}{x^2}=\frac{k^2}{2}$$

The third one is the one students do not have, and it is exactly what part (b) is.
Learn it in that form and (b) is a five-second question: $\frac{x^2}{1-\cos2x}$ is the reciprocal of
$\frac{1-\cos2x}{x^2}\to\frac{2^2}{2}=2$, so the answer is $\frac12$.

Two supporting identities, both verified as identities over an interval, not just at a point:

$$1-\cos(kx) = 2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{kx}{2}\right)
\qquad
\sin(2x) = 2\sin x\cos x$$

### 2.3 The degree rule for $x\to\pm\infty$

For a ratio of polynomials, only the **leading** terms matter.

| | limit |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\deg(\text{num}) < \deg(\text{den})$ | $0$ |
| $\deg(\text{num}) = \deg(\text{den})$ | ratio of the leading **coefficients** |
| $\deg(\text{num}) > \deg(\text{den})$ | $\pm\infty$, so DNE |

Part (d) is row three: degree 3 over degree 2.
The constants $5$ and $2$ are decoration and do not enter the answer.

### 2.4 The sign rule for nonzero$/0$

This is the half of the question most marks are lost in, and it is the whole of (e) and (f).

Once you know the answer is infinite, one question remains: **does the denominator's sign change as
you cross the point?**

| Denominator near $a$ | Sign behaviour | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| an **even** power, e.g. $(x-2)^2$ | positive on both sides | one signed infinity |
| an **odd** power or a linear factor, e.g. $3x+\pi$ | flips | left $\neq$ right, so **DNE** |

Then the numerator's sign at the point decides which infinity.
That is the entire method, and it is two lines of work.

## 3. The universal procedure

> 1. **Substitute $a$ into the unsimplified expression.** Write down the symbol you get.
> 2. **Classify** it using the table in 2.1.
> 3. If $0/0$: pick the algebra by looking at what is *in* the expression - polynomial, root,
>    stacked fraction, or trig.
> 4. If nonzero$/0$: do **not** do algebra. Do a sign check from each side.
> 5. If $x\to\pm\infty$: compare leading terms only.
> 6. **Recheck by substituting a number close to $a$** into the original. This catches sign errors.
> 7. **State the verdict and name the reason.** Unjustified means unmarked.

The classify step, applied to this question:

| part | substitute and you get | row | tool |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $\frac{1-1}{1-1}=\frac00$ | indeterminate | factor, both are differences of powers |
| b | $\frac{0}{1-\cos0}=\frac00$ | indeterminate | trig identity |
| c | $\frac{-9}{3}$ | **a number** | **stop, that is the answer** |
| d | $x\to\infty$ | leading terms | degree 3 over degree 2 |
| e | $\frac{\tan(-2\pi/3)}{0}=\frac{\sqrt3}{0}$ | nonzero$/0$ | sign check, linear denominator |
| f | $\frac{\cos2\pi}{0}=\frac{1}{0}$ | nonzero$/0$ | sign check, squared denominator |

Notice that four of the six are decided before you write a single line of algebra.

## 4. Q3.2 worked, part by part, with the routes compared

### (a) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}$

Substituting gives $\frac00$, so this is indeterminate and cancelling is legal.

**Route A - factor both (use this one).**
Both are differences of powers, and both therefore carry a factor of $(x-1)$.

$$\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}=\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)(x^2+x+1)}=\frac{x+1}{x^2+x+1}
\;\xrightarrow[x\to1]{}\;\frac{2}{3}$$

The cancelled form is continuous at $x=1$, which is *why* you may now substitute.
The two factorisations you need are worth memorising as a pair:

$$x^2-a^2=(x-a)(x+a)
\qquad
x^3-a^3=(x-a)(x^2+ax+a^2)$$

**Route B - the difference-of-powers shortcut.**

$$\lim_{x\to a}\frac{x^n-a^n}{x^m-a^m}=\frac{n}{m}\,a^{\,n-m}$$

Here $n=2$, $m=3$, $a=1$, so the limit is $\frac23\cdot1^{-1}=\frac23$ instantly.
This is worth knowing because it makes a whole family of exam variants a two-second question, and it
is safe whenever $a\neq0$ and both powers really are differences of $n$th powers.

**Route C - L'Hopital.**
$\frac{2x}{3x^2}$ at $x=1$ gives $\frac23$.
It agrees, but see the warning below before you rely on it.

| Route | Cost | Risk | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A, factor both | ~20 s | must recall the cubic factorisation | **use this under pressure** |
| B, shortcut | ~5 s | only applies to a pure difference of powers | use it as the check |
| C, L'Hopital | ~10 s | may be out of syllabus, and no justification | avoid as the primary |

### (b) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^2}{1-\cos(2x)}$

Substituting gives $\frac{0}{1-1}=\frac00$.

**Route A - the half-angle identity (use this one).**
Use $1-\cos(2x)=2\sin^2x$:

$$\frac{x^2}{1-\cos2x}=\frac{x^2}{2\sin^2x}=\frac12\left(\frac{x}{\sin x}\right)^2
\;\xrightarrow[x\to0]{}\;\frac12(1)^2=\frac12$$

**Route B - the memorised form.**
$\frac{1-\cos(kx)}{x^2}\to\frac{k^2}{2}$, so with $k=2$ the denominator-over-numerator is $2$, and
the answer is its reciprocal, $\frac12$.

**Route C - the conjugate.**
Multiply above and below by $1+\cos2x$:

$$\frac{x^2(1+\cos2x)}{1-\cos^2 2x}=\frac{x^2(1+\cos2x)}{\sin^2 2x}
=\left(\frac{x}{\sin2x}\right)^{2}(1+\cos2x)
\;\xrightarrow[x\to0]{}\;\left(\frac12\right)^2\cdot 2=\frac12$$

Correct, and three times the work.

**Route D - L'Hopital twice.**
$\frac{2x}{2\sin2x}$ is still $\frac00$, then $\frac{2}{4\cos2x}\to\frac12$.
Two applications, two chances to slip.

| Route | Cost | Risk | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A, half-angle identity | ~15 s | recalling $1-\cos2x=2\sin^2x$ | **use this under pressure** |
| B, memorised $k^2/2$ | ~5 s | inverting the fraction the wrong way | best if you trust the form |
| C, conjugate | ~45 s | $\sin^2 2x$ then needs a second $\sin u/u$ | fallback if the identity is gone |
| D, L'Hopital $\times2$ | ~30 s | must re-check it is still $0/0$ | avoid |

The one thing to get right: the answer is $\frac12$, not $2$.
Reciprocals are the whole trap here, so read the fraction one more time before you commit.

### (c) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{6x-9}{x^3-12x+3}$

**Substitute first.**

$$\frac{6(0)-9}{0-0+3}=\frac{-9}{3}=-3$$

The denominator at $x=0$ is $3$, not $0$.
There is no indeterminate form, the function is continuous at $0$, and the limit is the value.
**The answer is $-3$ and the work is finished in about four seconds.**

The justification is one word: continuity.

**Why this part is on the paper.**
It is placed among five hard limits to catch pattern-matching.
It *looks* like the others, so a student in a hurry reaches for a tool.
Watch what the two most tempting tools produce:

| What you might do | Result | Right? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| substitute (correct) | $-9/3=-3$ | **yes** |
| apply L'Hopital because it "looks like a limit problem" | $\frac{6}{3x^2-12}$ at $0=\frac{6}{-12}=-\frac12$ | **no** |
| try to factor and cancel | nothing cancels, wasted minutes | no |

That $-\frac12$ is not a near miss, it is a different number, and it is what you get for using a tool
whose precondition you never checked.
L'Hopital's rule requires $\frac00$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$.
This is $\frac{-9}{3}$.

This is the single most valuable habit in the whole question: **substitute before you reach.**

### (d) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}$

**Route A - the degree rule (use this one).**
Numerator degree 3, denominator degree 2, so $3>2$ and the ratio grows without bound.
The leading behaviour is $\frac{-2x^3}{x^2}=-2x$, which tends to $-\infty$.

$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}=-\infty$$

**Route B - divide by the highest power in the denominator.**
This is the version that shows your work:

$$\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}
=\frac{\frac{5}{x^2}-2x}{1+\frac{2}{x^2}}
\;\xrightarrow[x\to\infty]{}\;\frac{0-2x}{1+0}=-2x\to-\infty$$

Every term with an $x$ in a denominator dies, and what survives is $-2x$.

**Route C - factor out the leading power from each.**
$\frac{x^3(5/x^3-2)}{x^2(1+2/x^2)}=x\cdot\frac{-2}{1}=-2x\to-\infty$.

| Route | Cost | Risk | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A, degree rule | ~5 s | getting the *sign* wrong | **use this, then confirm the sign** |
| B, divide through | ~30 s | dropping a term | use if asked to justify in writing |
| C, factor out | ~20 s | index arithmetic | fine, equivalent to B |

**The sign is the only thing that can go wrong here, and it is worth its own line.**
The leading coefficient is $-2$, and $x\to+\infty$, so the answer is $-\infty$.
A quick numerical sanity check: at $x=1000$ the expression is about $-2000$, which is large and
negative. Verified.

**How to write it.**
"The limit does not exist; the expression decreases without bound, so $\lim = -\infty$."
Saying $-\infty$ *is* the explanation of why it does not exist, so give both.

### (e) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\pi/3}\frac{\tan(2x)}{3x+\pi}$

This is the hardest part of the question and it is the one to slow down on.

**Step 1, substitute.** The denominator is $3(-\frac\pi3)+\pi=0$.
Now the numerator, which is where people go wrong.
$2x\to-\frac{2\pi}{3}$, and $\tan$ has period $\pi$, so

$$\tan\!\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\tan\!\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}+\pi\right)=\tan\frac{\pi}{3}=\sqrt3$$

$-\frac{2\pi}{3}$ sits strictly between the tangent asymptotes at $-\frac{\pi}{2}$ and
$-\frac{3\pi}{2}$, so $\tan(2x)$ is perfectly well behaved and continuous there.
The numerator tends to $\sqrt3\approx1.732$, which is **not zero**.

**Step 2, classify.** $\frac{\sqrt3}{0}$ is nonzero over zero.
This is *not* $\frac00$, so there is no algebra to do and nothing to cancel.
It is infinite. The only remaining question is the sign.

**Step 3, sign check.** The numerator is positive and roughly constant at $\sqrt3$ near the point, so
the sign of the whole expression is the sign of $3x+\pi$.

| side | $x$ | $3x+\pi$ | quotient |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| from the left, $x\to-\frac\pi3^-$ | slightly less than $-\frac\pi3$ | slightly **negative** | $\dfrac{+}{-}\to-\infty$ |
| from the right, $x\to-\frac\pi3^+$ | slightly more than $-\frac\pi3$ | slightly **positive** | $\dfrac{+}{+}\to+\infty$ |

**Step 4, verdict.**

$$\lim_{x\to-\pi/3^-}\frac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}=-\infty\qquad
\lim_{x\to-\pi/3^+}\frac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}=+\infty$$

The one-sided limits differ, so the two-sided limit **does not exist**.

Here you must write **DNE**, and not an infinity: the one-sided limits disagree, so no single symbol
describes the behaviour. This is the difference between (e) and (f), and it is the whole point of
having both on the paper.

**The two traps in this part, in the order they bite.**

1. *Assuming the numerator is zero.* Many students see $\tan$ of something near $-2\pi/3$, panic, and
   guess $0/0$. Evaluate it: it is $\sqrt3$. If you had assumed $\frac00$ you would have gone looking
   for a cancellation that does not exist and lost the time as well as the marks.
2. *Answering $+\infty$ because the right side does.* Check both sides. The denominator is linear, so
   the sign flips, so it cannot be a single infinity.

**Alternative route, the shift substitution.**
Put $u=3x+\pi$, so $x=\frac{u-\pi}{3}$ and $2x=\frac{2u}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3}$.
The limit becomes $\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\tan\!\left(\frac{2u}{3}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)}{u}$, whose
numerator tends to $\tan(-\frac{2\pi}{3})=\sqrt3$ while the denominator tends to $0$ and changes sign.
Same conclusion, and it makes the "nonzero over zero" structure impossible to misread.
This substitution is the workhorse of Family 8 below, so it is worth having.

### (f) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\cos(\pi x)}{(x-2)^2}$

**Step 1, substitute.** The numerator is $\cos(2\pi)=1$, and the denominator is $0$.
Again $\frac{1}{0}$: nonzero over zero, so infinite, so the only question is the sign.

**Step 2, sign check.** The numerator is positive near $x=2$.
The denominator is a **square**, so it is positive on *both* sides.
The sign never flips.

$$\lim_{x\to2^-}=+\infty,\qquad
\lim_{x\to2^+}=+\infty,\qquad
\text{so}\quad \lim_{x\to2}\frac{\cos(\pi x)}{(x-2)^2}=+\infty$$

**Step 3, how to say it.**
Both sides agree, so you are entitled to write $+\infty$.
Strictly the limit still fails to exist as a real number, so the complete sentence is:
"The limit does not exist. The expression increases without bound from both sides, so
$\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=+\infty$."
Write both halves and you cannot be marked down on either convention.

**Why (e) and (f) are both here.**
They are the same classification, $\frac{\text{nonzero}}{0}$, with opposite outcomes, and the *only*
thing that separates them is the parity of the denominator's power.
Even power, no flip, one signed infinity.
Odd or linear, flip, DNE.
Nail this pair and you have Family 5, which is the most common limit family on the whole course.

## 5. The exam-ready write-up

If the sitting is hand-marked, "no credit for unjustified answers" is enforced literally.
Each part needs one line naming the reason.
These are the sentences, and they are short on purpose.

> **a.** Substituting gives $\frac00$, an indeterminate form. Factoring,
> $\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}=\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)(x^2+x+1)}=\frac{x+1}{x^2+x+1}$ for $x\neq1$. This is
> continuous at $x=1$, so the limit is $\frac{2}{3}$.
>
> **b.** Substituting gives $\frac00$. Using $1-\cos2x=2\sin^2x$, the expression is
> $\frac12\left(\frac{x}{\sin x}\right)^2$. Since $\frac{\sin x}{x}\to1$, the limit is $\frac12$.
>
> **c.** The denominator at $x=0$ is $3\neq0$, so the form is not indeterminate. The function is
> continuous at $0$, so the limit is the value there, $\frac{-9}{3}=-3$.
>
> **d.** Dividing numerator and denominator by $x^2$ gives $\frac{5/x^2-2x}{1+2/x^2}$. As
> $x\to\infty$ the terms $5/x^2$ and $2/x^2$ tend to $0$ and the numerator behaves like $-2x$, which
> decreases without bound. The limit does not exist; $\lim=-\infty$.
>
> **e.** As $x\to-\frac\pi3$, $\tan(2x)\to\tan(-\frac{2\pi}{3})=\sqrt3\neq0$ while $3x+\pi\to0$.
> Since $3x+\pi<0$ for $x<-\frac\pi3$ and $3x+\pi>0$ for $x>-\frac\pi3$, the left limit is $-\infty$
> and the right limit is $+\infty$. The one-sided limits differ, so the limit does not exist.
>
> **f.** As $x\to2$, $\cos(\pi x)\to\cos2\pi=1\neq0$ while $(x-2)^2\to0^+$. The denominator is
> positive on both sides, so the expression increases without bound from both sides. The limit does
> not exist as a real number; $\lim=+\infty$.

Four features make these earn the method marks.
Each one names the form found on substitution.
Each one names the theorem or identity used.
Each one that cancels says "for $x\neq a$", which is the step being tested.
Each infinite one states the sign from **each** side before drawing a conclusion.

## 6. Verify before you commit

Six answers, and about forty seconds to check all six.

**Check 1, the magnitude check.**
Substitute a number close to the point into the **original** expression, and see if it is near your
answer.

| part | try | original gives | claimed | agrees |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $x=1.001$ | $0.66667$ | $2/3$ | yes |
| b | $x=0.01$ | $0.50002$ | $1/2$ | yes |
| c | $x=0.001$ | $-2.999$ | $-3$ | yes |
| d | $x=1000$ | $-2000.0$ | $-\infty$ | yes, large and negative |
| e | $x=-\pi/3\pm10^{-6}$ | $-5.8\times10^5$ and $+5.8\times10^5$ | DNE | yes, opposite signs |
| f | $x=2\pm10^{-6}$ | $+10^{12}$ both sides | $+\infty$ | yes, same sign |

This one habit catches nearly every sign error, and a sign error is the most common way to lose
marks on (d), (e) and (f).

**Check 2, the cheap invariants.**

- A cancelled fraction must agree with the original at some third point. If $\frac{x+1}{x^2+x+1}$ is
  right, then at $x=2$ it should equal $\frac{4-1}{8-1}=\frac37$, and it does: $\frac{3}{7}$.
- A trig limit answer should be a simple rational or a multiple of $\pi$. If you get something
  strange, you probably lost a factor of $k$.
- For $x\to\infty$ on a rational function, the answer is $0$, a ratio of coefficients, or $\pm\infty$.
  It is never anything else. If you produce a different-looking number, recount the degrees.
- Nonzero$/0$ can never come out finite. If you have "simplified" such a limit to a number, you did
  algebra in the wrong row of the classification table.

**Check 3, the numbers worth memorising.**
$\frac23\approx0.6667$, $\frac12=0.5$, $\sqrt3\approx1.7321$, $\cos2\pi=1$,
$\tan\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\sqrt3$.
Knowing $\tan(-2\pi/3)=+\sqrt3$ by sight removes the only genuinely hard step in the paper.

## 7. Answer-entry rules

Möbius is answer-only and machine-marked, so a right method with a bad keystroke scores zero.
Grading is by mathematical equivalence, so a correct answer is never rejected for its *form*, only
for being **parsed** differently than you intended.

| Rule | Do | Not |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Multiplication | `2*x` | `2x` |
| Fractions | `2/3` | `0.667` when an exact value is wanted |
| Negative fractions | `-3` or `-1/2` | `- 1/2` with a stray space |
| Roots | `sqrt(3)` | a root glyph, or `3^.5` |
| Whole numerators | `(sqrt(6)+sqrt(2))/4` | `sqrt(6)+sqrt(2)/4` |
| Natural log | `ln(x)` | `log(x)` |

**The three limit-specific answers, and the honest state of what is known.**
The finite answers are unambiguous: type `2/3`, `1/2`, `-3`.

For the other three, the usual Möbius/Maple tokens are `infinity`, `-infinity` and `DNE`, and
`INF`/`-INF` are also accepted in some question types.
This is a **platform convention, not mathematics**, and it is set per question by whoever authored it:
some limit questions present a dropdown or a "does not exist" checkbox instead of a free-text box, in
which case you do not type anything.
Confirm it once on a practice question rather than discovering it in the exam, and if the box is free

## 8. The variation catalog

This is the part that makes the question repeatable.
Everything below has been recomputed independently and checked against the original function.

**Read each family as a structure, not as a list of facts.**
There are only thirteen families, and every limit on this course lands in one of them.
The trigger tells you which family you are in, and the family names the tool.
Notice how few *tools* there actually are: substitute, factor, conjugate, common denominator, trig
identity, sign check, leading terms, shift, squeeze.
Nine tools, thirteen triggers.

### Family 0 - not indeterminate, so substitute (this is 3.2c)

**Trigger:** you substituted and got a number, including $\frac{0}{\text{nonzero}}$.
**Tool:** none. That number is the answer.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{6x-9}{x^3-12x+3}$ | $-3$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}(3x^2-2x+1)$ | $9$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2+3}{x+4}$ | $\frac45$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\cos x}{x+1}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi}\frac{\sin x+2}{x^2}$ | $\frac{2}{\pi^2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^2-9}{x+3}$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^2-4}{x^2+4}$ | $-1$ |

Row six is the mirror trap.
$\frac{0}{6}$ *is* determinate and the answer is $0$.
Only $\frac00$ licenses algebra, so do not cancel here.

### Family 1 - $0/0$ polynomial, factor and cancel (this is 3.2a)

**Trigger:** $\frac00$, and everything in sight is a polynomial.
**Tool:** factor both, cancel the common $(x-a)$, then substitute.
**Shortcut:** $\lim_{x\to a}\frac{x^n-a^n}{x^m-a^m}=\frac{n}{m}a^{\,n-m}$.

| Limit | Answer | Shortcut check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}$ | $\frac23$ | $\frac23\cdot1^{-1}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^3-1}{x^2-1}$ | $\frac32$ | $\frac32\cdot1^{1}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^5-1}{x^3-1}$ | $\frac53$ | $\frac53\cdot1^{2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-1}{x^4-1}$ | $\frac12$ | $\frac24\cdot1^{-2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^5-32}{x^3-8}$ | $\frac{20}{3}$ | $\frac53\cdot2^{2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-x-2}$ | $\frac43$ | not a pure power pair |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^3-8}{x-2}$ | $12$ | $\frac31\cdot2^{2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^4-16}{x-2}$ | $32$ | $\frac41\cdot2^{3}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-1}\frac{x^3+1}{x+1}$ | $3$ | via $x^3-(-1)^3$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^2-x-6}{x-3}$ | $5$ | ordinary factoring |

**How the numbers are generated.** In the pure-power rows only three things move: $n$, $m$, and $a$.
There is one formula and no memorised answers.

### Family 2 - $0/0$ with a root, multiply by the conjugate

**Trigger:** $\frac00$ and a square root sits in the numerator or the denominator.
**Tool:** multiply above and below by the conjugate of the part containing the root. If both parts
have roots, do both.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\sqrt{6-x}-2}{\sqrt{3-x}-1}$ | $\frac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sqrt{x+4}-2}{x}$ | $\frac14$ |
| $\lim_{x\to9}\frac{\sqrt{x}-3}{x-9}$ | $\frac16$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sqrt{x+1}-1}{\sqrt{x+4}-2}$ | $2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to4}\frac{x-4}{\sqrt{x}-2}$ | $4$ |
| $\lim_{x\to8}\frac{\sqrt[3]{x}-2}{x-8}$ | $\frac{1}{12}$ |

The worked shape, verified as an identity rather than only at the point:

$$\frac{\sqrt{x+4}-2}{x}=\frac{(x+4)-4}{x\left(\sqrt{x+4}+2\right)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2}
\;\xrightarrow[x\to0]{}\;\frac14$$

The last row needs the cube-root version, $a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$, or the observation that it is
the derivative of $x^{1/3}$ at $8$.

### Family 3 - $0/0$ with a stacked fraction, one denominator first

**Trigger:** $\frac00$ and there is a fraction inside the fraction.
**Tool:** combine the small fractions over a common denominator, then cancel.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\frac{\frac1x-\frac13}{x-3}$ | $-\frac19$ |
| $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\frac{1}{2+h}-\frac12}{h}$ | $-\frac14$ |
| $\lim_{x\to4}\frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt x}-\frac12}{x-4}$ | $-\frac{1}{16}$ |

The shape, again verified as an identity:
$\frac{\frac1x-\frac13}{x-3}=\frac{3-x}{3x(x-3)}=-\frac{1}{3x}\to-\frac19$.
**Every answer in this family is negative**, because $\frac1x$ is decreasing.
That is a free sign check.

### Family 4 - $0/0$ trigonometric, identity then $\sin u / u$ (this is 3.2b)

**Trigger:** $\frac00$ with $\sin$, $\cos$ or $\tan$ in it, and $x\to0$.
**Tool:** rewrite until every trig piece is $\frac{\sin(\text{something})}{\text{that same thing}}$.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^2}{1-\cos2x}$ | $\frac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos2x}{x^2}$ | $2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos3x}{x^2}$ | $\frac92$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^2}$ | $\frac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\cos x-1}{x^2}$ | $-\frac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin3x}{x^2-x}$ | $-3$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin5x}{\sin3x}$ | $\frac53$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan2x}{3x}$ | $\frac23$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{\sin4x}$ | $\frac14$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin^2 3x}{x^2}$ | $9$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos4x}{x\sin x}$ | $8$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x-\tan x}{x^3}$ | $-\frac12$ |

**Read this as one fact, not twelve.**
Only two patterns appear: $\frac{\sin kx}{x}\to k$, and $\frac{1-\cos kx}{x^2}\to\frac{k^2}{2}$.
Every row is one of those, possibly inverted, possibly with an extra factor tagged on.
Rows five and six show where the signs come from: a flipped subtraction, and a leftover $\frac{1}{x-1}$
tending to $-1$.

### Family 5 - nonzero$/0$, decide by the sign from each side (this is 3.2e and 3.2f)

**Trigger:** you substituted and got a nonzero number over $0$.
**Tool:** no algebra. Sign of numerator, then sign of denominator from each side.

**Sub-family 5A, even power, so no flip, so one signed infinity.**

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\cos\pi x}{(x-2)^2}$ | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{1}{(x-1)^2}$ | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{-1}{(x-1)^2}$ | $-\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x+1}{(x-1)^2}$ | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x-3}{(x-1)^2}$ | $-\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{x^2}$ | $+\infty$ |

The sign of the answer is just the sign of the numerator at the point.
Rows four and five differ only in that: $x+1\to2>0$, and $x-3\to-2<0$.

**Sub-family 5B, sign flips, so DNE.**

| Limit | Left | Right | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to-\pi/3}\frac{\tan2x}{3x+\pi}$ | $-\infty$ | $+\infty$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\cos\pi x}{x-2}$ | $-\infty$ | $+\infty$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\frac{2x^2-x+1}{x-3}$ | $-\infty$ | $+\infty$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1}{x^3}$ | $-\infty$ | $+\infty$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2+1}{x^2-4}$ | $-\infty$ | $+\infty$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\sec x$ | $+\infty$ | $-\infty$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to5\pi/2}\tan x$ | $+\infty$ | $-\infty$ | DNE |

Compare row one of 5A with row two of 5B: identical numerator, and the only change is $(x-2)^2$ versus
$(x-2)$.
One is $+\infty$ and the other is DNE.
That is the entire distinction the examiner is testing.

**Sub-family 5C, the two-stage trap.**
This one looks like $\frac00$, cancels **once**, and then turns out to be nonzero$/0$.

$$\lim_{x\to-2}\frac{3x^2-2x-16}{(x+2)^2}$$

At $x=-2$ the numerator is $12+4-16=0$, so it really is $\frac00$ and you really may factor:
$3x^2-2x-16=(x+2)(3x-8)$, giving $\frac{3x-8}{x+2}$.
Now substitute again: $\frac{-14}{0}$.
The form **changed** after cancelling, so re-classify: nonzero over zero, denominator linear, sign
flips, so the left limit is $+\infty$, the right is $-\infty$, and the answer is **DNE**.

The lesson generalises: **after every cancellation, substitute again and re-classify.**
One round of algebra does not mean one round of classification.

### Family 6 - $x\to\pm\infty$ on a rational function, compare degrees (this is 3.2d)

**Trigger:** $x\to\infty$ or $x\to-\infty$.
**Tool:** leading terms only.

| Limit | Degrees | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}$ | $3>2$ | $-\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}$ | $3>2$ | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5-2x^3}{x^3+2}$ | $3=3$ | $-2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{5-2x^2}{x^3+2}$ | $2<3$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}$ | $2<4$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{4x^2-3}{2x^2+x}$ | $2=2$ | $2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}$ | - | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}$ | - | $-1$ |

Rows one and two are the *same expression* with opposite answers, which is why "as $x\to$ what" must
be read before anything else.
Rows seven and eight are the $\lvert x\rvert$ trap: $\sqrt{x^2}=\lvert x\rvert$, not $x$.
At $x=-1000$, $\sqrt{x^2}=+1000$, so the quotient is $-1$.
Any time a root of an even power meets $x\to-\infty$, a minus sign appears.

### Family 7 - $x\to\infty$ with a root, conjugate at infinity

**Trigger:** $x\to\infty$ and a difference of two things that both grow, or a root over a polynomial.
**Tool:** for a difference, multiply by the conjugate. For a ratio, divide by the highest power,
remembering that inside a square root the power halves.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2+3x}-x\right)$ | $\frac32$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2+x}-\sqrt{x^2-x}\right)$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(x-\sqrt{x^2-4x}\right)$ | $2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2+1}-x\right)$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{4x^2+1}}{x+3}$ | $2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{\sqrt{4x^2+1}}{x+3}$ | $-2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x+2}{\sqrt{4x^2+1}}$ | $\frac32$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{3x+2}{\sqrt{4x^2+1}}$ | $-\frac32$ |

$\infty-\infty$ is indeterminate, so rows one to four cannot be answered by inspection.
The pattern for $\sqrt{x^2+bx}-x$ is $\frac{b}{2}$, which covers rows one, three and four at a glance
($b=3$, $b=-4$, $b=0$).

### Family 8 - a trig limit at a point that is not $0$, shift $u=x-a$

**Trigger:** trig, $\frac00$, and $x\to a$ with $a\neq0$.
**Tool:** substitute $u=x-a$ so the limit moves to $u\to0$, then use an angle-addition identity to
turn the shifted trig function into a plain one.

| Limit | Identity used | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\sin\pi x}{x-2}$ | $\sin(\pi u+2\pi)=\sin\pi u$ | $\pi$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\frac{\cos x}{x-\pi/2}$ | $\cos(u+\frac\pi2)=-\sin u$ | $-1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\frac{1-\sin x}{(x-\pi/2)^2}$ | $1-\sin(u+\frac\pi2)=1-\cos u$ | $\frac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi}\frac{1+\cos x}{(x-\pi)^2}$ | $1+\cos(u+\pi)=1-\cos u$ | $\frac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/6}\frac{\sin x-\frac12}{x-\pi/6}$ | difference quotient | $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/4}\frac{\tan(x-\pi/4)}{x-\pi/4}$ | already shifted | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1/2}\frac{\cos\pi x}{x-1/2}$ | $\cos(\pi u+\frac\pi2)=-\sin\pi u$ | $-\pi$ |

**This is 3.2e's honest cousin, and telling them apart is a marks question.**
Family 8 is $\frac00$ and has a finite answer.
3.2e substitutes to $\frac{\sqrt3}{0}$ and has no limit at all.
The *only* way to know which one you are looking at is to evaluate the numerator.
Compare rows one and seven with 3.2e: same flavour, different form, completely different verdict.

### Family 9 - bounded times something that dies, squeeze theorem

**Trigger:** a factor that oscillates forever and never settles, such as $\sin x$ as $x\to\infty$ or
$\sin\frac1x$ as $x\to0$, multiplied by something tending to $0$.
**Tool:** bound the oscillating factor by $1$ and show the rest tends to $0$.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\frac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}\right)\sin x$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}x^2\sin\frac1x$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}x\sin\frac1x$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\sin\frac1x$ | **DNE** |

The write-up is always the same:
$\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert \le \left\lvert g(x)\right\rvert \to 0$, therefore $f(x)\to0$.

The last row is the boundary case and it is the one worth understanding.
There is nothing tending to $0$ multiplying it, so there is nothing to squeeze against.
$\sin\frac1x$ keeps taking every value in $[-1,1]$ arbitrarily close to $0$, so the limit does not
exist. Verified: on a sample of points approaching $0$, the values still span $-1.000$ to $1.000$.
**"Bounded" alone never gives a limit. Bounded times vanishing does.**

### Family 10 - one-sided by necessity, $\lvert x\rvert$ and piecewise

**Trigger:** an absolute value, or a piecewise definition, sitting at the joining point.
**Tool:** split into cases and take the two one-sided limits separately.
Recall $\lvert x\rvert = x$ for $x>0$ and $-x$ for $x<0$.

| Limit | Left | Right | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{x}$ | $-1$ | $1$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\frac{\lvert x-3\rvert}{x-3}$ | $-1$ | $1$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\lvert x\rvert-x}{x}$ | $-2$ | $0$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\lvert x^2-4\rvert}{x-2}$ | $-4$ | $4$ | DNE |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{x^2}$ | $+\infty$ | $+\infty$ | $+\infty$ |

The first four are DNE with **finite** one-sided values, which is a different reason for DNE than
anything in Family 5.
There are three distinct ways a limit can fail, and a written answer should say which:
the sides disagree finitely (Family 10), the sides disagree infinitely (Family 5B), or it oscillates
(Family 9, last row).

### Family 11 - continuity, solve for the constant

**Trigger:** "find $k$ so that $f$ is continuous", or a piecewise function with an unknown.
**Tool:** compute the limit, then set the function value equal to it.

| Problem | Requirement | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $f(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}$ for $x\neq1$, $f(1)=k$ | $k=\lim_{x\to1}f$ | $k=2$ |
| $f(x)=\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ for $x\neq3$, $f(3)=k$ | $k=\lim_{x\to3}f$ | $k=6$ |
| $f(x)=cx+1$ for $x\le2$, $x^2$ for $x>2$ | $2c+1=4$ | $c=\frac32$ |

Continuity at $a$ has three requirements, and a question can attack any of them: $f(a)$ is defined,
$\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists, and the two are equal.

### Family 12 - the difference quotient in disguise

**Trigger:** the shape $\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}$, or $\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ with $h\to0$.
**Tool:** recognise it as $f'(a)$ and differentiate, or grind it algebraically.

| Limit | Recognise as | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(2+h)^3-8}{h}$ | $\frac{d}{dx}x^3$ at $2$ | $12$ |
| $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sqrt{4+h}-2}{h}$ | $\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt x$ at $4$ | $\frac14$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^4-81}{x-3}$ | $\frac{d}{dx}x^4$ at $3$ | $108$ |
| $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\cot(\frac\pi4+h)-1}{h}$ | $\frac{d}{dx}\cot x$ at $\frac\pi4$ | $-2$ |

The last row is the link to Final Exam 1 Q3, "use the definition of the derivative to compute the
derivative of $\cot x$", where $-\csc^2\frac\pi4=-2$.
Family 12 also re-derives Family 1's shortcut: $\lim_{x\to a}\frac{x^n-a^n}{x-a}=na^{n-1}$ is just
the power rule.

## 9. The trap list

Ranked by how often each one actually costs marks.

1. **Reaching for a tool without substituting first.** This is part (c), 4 points, and the wrong
   answer $-\frac12$ is what a confident student produces. Substitute, always, before anything.
2. **Getting the sign wrong on an infinite limit.** Parts (d), (e) and (f) are 12 of the 24 points and
   every one of them turns on a sign. Check the sign from **each** side, in writing.
3. **Saying $+\infty$ when the answer is DNE, or DNE when a signed infinity is wanted.** Even power,
   no flip, so a signed infinity. Odd or linear, flip, so DNE.
4. **Assuming a trig numerator is $0$ because the point is not $0$.** In (e), $\tan(-\frac{2\pi}{3})$
   is $\sqrt3$, not $0$. Evaluate it, do not assume it.
5. **Not re-classifying after a cancellation.** Family 5C looks like $\frac00$, cancels once, and
   becomes nonzero$/0$. One round of algebra, two rounds of classification.
6. **Inverting a trig limit the wrong way.** Part (b) is $\frac12$; the limit of the upside-down
   version is $2$. Read the fraction once more before committing.
7. **Forgetting $\sqrt{x^2}=\lvert x\rvert$ when $x\to-\infty$.** A missing minus sign, every time.
8. **Cancelling in a non-$\frac00$ form.** Illegal, and it produces a finite answer where the truth is
   infinite.
9. **Using L'Hopital where the syllabus wants algebra.** It may not be examinable at this point in the
   course, and on a hand-marked paper it may not earn the justification marks even when the number is
   right.
10. **Answering a bare number on a paper that says "no credit for unjustified answers".** One line per
    part is all it takes.

## 10. Drill set

Twelve problems, mixed families, no order.
Do them in one sitting and aim for eight minutes.

1. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to5}\frac{x^2-25}{x-5}$
2. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos5x}{x^2}$
3. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{4-3x^2}{2x^2+x-1}$
4. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to4}\frac{x+4}{x^2-16}$
5. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sqrt{x+9}-3}{x}$
6. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan3x}{\sin5x}$
7. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-3}\frac{x^3+27}{x+3}$
8. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{2x^4+1}{x^3-x}$
9. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to5}\frac{\frac1x-\frac15}{x-5}$
10. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}\frac{\sin(2x-2)}{x-1}$
11. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{9x^2+2}}{1-3x}$
12. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-2x}{x^2-4x+4}$

### Answer key

| # | Exact | Decimal | Family and method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | $10$ | $10$ | F1, factor $(x-5)(x+5)$ and cancel |
| 2 | $\frac{25}{2}$ | $12.5$ | F4, $\frac{1-\cos kx}{x^2}\to\frac{k^2}{2}$ with $k=5$ |
| 3 | $-\frac32$ | $-1.5$ | F6, equal degrees, ratio of leading coefficients $\frac{-3}{2}$ |
| 4 | **DNE** | - | F5B, $\frac{8}{0}$, denominator $(x-4)(x+4)$ flips sign, $-\infty$ then $+\infty$ |
| 5 | $\frac16$ | $0.1\overline{6}$ | F2, conjugate $\sqrt{x+9}+3$ |
| 6 | $\frac35$ | $0.6$ | F4, $\frac{\tan3x}{x}\to3$ over $\frac{\sin5x}{x}\to5$ |
| 7 | $27$ | $27$ | F1, sum of cubes, or shortcut $\frac31(-3)^2$ |
| 8 | $+\infty$ (DNE) | - | F6, degree $4>3$, positive leading coefficient |
| 9 | $-\frac{1}{25}$ | $-0.04$ | F3, common denominator, result $-\frac{1}{5x}$ |
| 10 | $2$ | $2$ | F8, shift $u=x-1$, giving $\frac{\sin2u}{u}\to2$ |
| 11 | $-1$ | $-1$ | F7, $\sqrt{9x^2}=3\lvert x\rvert=3x$ for $x>0$, over $-3x$ |
| 12 | $+\infty$ **and** $-\infty$, so **DNE** | - | F5C, cancels once to $\frac{x}{x-2}$, then $\frac{2}{0}$ flips |

**Score sign and magnitude separately.**
They are different failures and they need different fixes.
A wrong magnitude means you picked the wrong tool, so reread the family.
A wrong sign means your method was right and your bookkeeping was not, so add the "substitute a nearby
number" check from Section 6 to every infinite limit.
Item 12 is the one to check twice: it is the only drill that needs two rounds of classification.

## 11. The one-page decision tree

```
                       lim f(x) as x -> a
                              |
                    SUBSTITUTE a, unsimplified
                              |
        +---------------------+---------------------+------------------+
        |                     |                     |                  |
   a NUMBER             0 / 0  (indeterminate)  NONZERO / 0        a -> +/- inf
        |                     |                     |                  |
   THAT IS THE               |              NO ALGEBRA.            leading
   ANSWER  [F0]              |              Sign check.            terms only
   (0/nonzero = 0)           |                     |                  |
                             |          +----------+---------+     deg< -> 0
              +--------------+-------+  |                    |     deg= -> ratio
              |       |       |      |  even power       odd/linear   deg> -> +/-inf
         polynomial  root  stacked  trig    |                 |          [F6]
            [F1]     [F2]   frac   [F4]   no flip          flips     root? -> [F7]
          factor  conjugate [F3]  identity   |                 |      sqrt(x^2)=|x|
              |       |    common    |    ONE SIGNED       sides differ
              +-------+----+---------+     INFINITY          -> DNE
                           |               [F5A]              [F5B]
                    CANCEL, then
                    SUBSTITUTE AGAIN
                    and RE-CLASSIFY  <-- [F5C] lives here
                           |
                  a != 0 and trig? -> shift u = x-a  [F8]
                  oscillating factor? -> squeeze     [F9]
                  |x| or piecewise? -> one-sided     [F10]
```

How a limit claim was checked, since a limit is not a single number:

| Check | What it asserts | Tolerance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| identity | the simplified form is the *same function* as the original near the point, sampled at 80 points | $10^{-11}$ relative |
| probe | the original really approaches the claimed value, and the error **shrinks** as $h$ shrinks | $3\times10^{-3}$ relative to the limit |
| verdict | for an infinite answer: growth by a factor $\ge3$ per decade over five decades, with a constant sign | - |
| DNE | the two one-sided values genuinely disagree | - |
| squeeze | $\lvert f\rvert$ stays under the claimed bound at 400 sampled points, and the bound dies | - |

Probe tolerances are **relative** to the size of the claimed limit, deliberately.
An absolute bar is meaningless: an error of $5\times10^{-3}$ refutes a claimed limit of $0.5$ and says
nothing whatever about a claimed limit of $108$.

Every assertion type was first pointed at a deliberately **wrong** claim to prove it can fail:
10 negative controls (a is $\frac32$, b is $1$, c is $-\frac12$, d is $+\infty$, e's sides agree, f's
left side is $-\infty$, and four more), and all 10 reported FAIL.
A check that cannot fail is not a check.

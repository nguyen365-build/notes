# Q1.1 - Exact value of a cosine

**The question (MT1 Q1, 5 points).**
Give the exact value of $\cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{12}\right).$

**The answer.**

$$\cos\left(-\frac{\pi}{12}\right)=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\approx 0.9659258263$$

**What to type into Möbius:** `(sqrt(6)+sqrt(2))/4`

**Browser version of this tutorial:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/d05135d1-3bb9-4b14-81cb-08a7df83f11a

Source of the question: `MATH265.md` Q1.1, and `MATH265/MATH265.md` "Midterm Exam 1" item 1.
Every exact value in this file was recomputed independently in Python and checked against a direct
trig evaluation to 1e-11.
Nothing here is copied from a solution key.

## 0. The 30-second version

If you only remember one thing, remember this chain.

```
cos(-pi/12)
  -> cos is EVEN, so drop the minus sign          cos(pi/12)
  -> pi/12 is 15 deg, and 15 = 45 - 30            cos(pi/4 - pi/6)
  -> cos(A-B) = cosA cosB + sinA sinB             (r2/2)(r3/2) + (r2/2)(1/2)
  -> combine over 4                               (r6 + r2)/4
```

That is four lines, about twenty seconds, and no calculator.

## 1. What this question is really testing

This is question 1 of the midterm and it is worth 5 of 64 points, but it is not a warm-up.
It is a gate.
The examiner is checking three separate reflexes at once, and each one of them reappears later in the paper.

| Reflex | Where it comes back |
| :--- | :--- |
| Parity of the trig functions (cos is even, sin is odd) | limits at negative arguments, symmetry in curve sketching |
| Decomposing a non-standard angle into standard ones | trig limits, the derivative-from-definition question |
| Producing an *exact* surd rather than a decimal | every "exact value" instruction on the paper |

The phrase "exact value" is the whole instruction.
It means no decimals, no rounding, no calculator output.
An answer of `0.9659` is wrong even though it is numerically close, because the question asked for a
number written in radicals.

**Format warning.**
The sample paper prints "(5 points)" and other questions on it say things like "Explain the procedure
you are using", which reads like a hand-marked written exam.
The live delivery for this course is Möbius, answer-only and machine-marked, where no work is shown.
Prepare for both: get the surd fast, which is all Möbius wants, and be able to write the four
justification lines in Section 5 in case the paper you sit does ask for method.

## 2. The four things you must have cold

You cannot do this question "fast" by deriving these.
They have to be recall.

### 2.1 The standard-angle table

| deg | rad | $\sin$ | $\cos$ | $\tan$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| 30 | $\pi/6$ | $1/2$ | $\sqrt3/2$ | $1/\sqrt3$ |
| 45 | $\pi/4$ | $\sqrt2/2$ | $\sqrt2/2$ | $1$ |
| 60 | $\pi/3$ | $\sqrt3/2$ | $1/2$ | $\sqrt3$ |
| 90 | $\pi/2$ | $1$ | $0$ | undefined |

Memory hook for the sine column: it goes $\tfrac12,\ \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\ \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ as the
angle grows, and cosine is the same list read backwards.

### 2.2 Parity

| Even (drop the minus) | Odd (pull the minus out front) |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\cos(-x)=\cos x$ | $\sin(-x)=-\sin x$ |
| $\sec(-x)=\sec x$ | $\tan(-x)=-\tan x$ |
| | $\csc(-x)=-\csc x$, $\cot(-x)=-\cot x$ |

Two even, four odd.
Cosine and its reciprocal are the even pair, everything else is odd.

### 2.3 Quadrant signs (ASTC)

Quadrant I: **A**ll positive.
Quadrant II: **S**ine only.
Quadrant III: **T**angent only.
Quadrant IV: **C**osine only.

Reduce any angle to its reference angle first, get the magnitude, then attach the sign from ASTC.
The reference angle is the acute angle to the nearest part of the $x$-axis, never to the $y$-axis.

### 2.4 The two formula families

**Sum and difference.**

$$\cos(A\mp B)=\cos A\cos B \pm \sin A\sin B$$

$$\sin(A\pm B)=\sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$$

$$\tan(A\pm B)=\frac{\tan A\pm\tan B}{1\mp\tan A\tan B}$$

The cosine one is the trap: its middle sign is the *opposite* of the sign in the bracket.
So $\cos(A-B)$ has a **plus** in the middle.
Say "cosine flips" out loud every time you write it.

**Half angle.**

$$\cos\frac{\theta}{2}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}},\qquad
\sin\frac{\theta}{2}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}}$$

The $\pm$ is not decoration.
You choose the sign from the quadrant of $\theta/2$, not of $\theta$.

## 3. The universal procedure

Every "exact value" question on this course is the same four moves.
Learn the moves, not the answers.

> **Step 1 - Strip.**
> Kill the minus sign with parity.
> Reduce anything past $2\pi$ by subtracting full turns.
> Note which quadrant the surviving angle lives in, and hold that sign in your head.
>
> **Step 2 - Classify.**
> Look at the denominator of the angle.
> That single number picks your tool.
>
> | Angle looks like | Tool |
> | :--- | :--- |
> | multiple of $\pi/6$, $\pi/4$, $\pi/3$, $\pi/2$ | straight recall, no formula |
> | odd multiple of $\pi/12$ (15, 75, 105, 165, ...) | **sum or difference formula** |
> | odd multiple of $\pi/8$ (22.5, 67.5, ...) | **half-angle formula** |
> | odd multiple of $\pi/24$ or worse | half-angle applied twice |
> | contains $\arcsin$, $\arccos$, $\arctan$ | **reference triangle**, no formula |
>
> **Step 3 - Substitute.**
> Write the formula out in full symbols first, then substitute.
> Never substitute into a half-remembered formula.
>
> **Step 4 - Simplify and sanity-check.**
> Put everything over one denominator.
> Then run the decimal check in Section 6 before you write the answer down.

For Q1.1: Step 1 gives $\cos(\pi/12)$, quadrant I so positive.
Step 2 sees $\pi/12$ and picks the difference formula.
Steps 3 and 4 are below.

## 4. Q1.1 worked three ways

All three routes are legitimate and all three give the same surd.
You should be able to run route A in your sleep, recognise route B, and keep route C in reserve.

### Route A - parity first, then the difference formula (use this one)

$$\cos\left(-\frac{\pi}{12}\right)=\cos\frac{\pi}{12}\qquad\text{(cosine is even)}$$

Now split $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$.
In degrees $15=45-30$, so

$$\frac{\pi}{12}=\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi}{6}$$

Check that split before you use it: $\dfrac{\pi}{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{6}=\dfrac{3\pi-2\pi}{12}=\dfrac{\pi}{12}$.
Good.

$$\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)=\cos\frac{\pi}{4}\cos\frac{\pi}{6}+\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\sin\frac{\pi}{6}$$

$$=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}
=\frac{\sqrt6}{4}+\frac{\sqrt2}{4}
=\boxed{\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}}$$

### Route B - difference formula without using parity

You do not have to use parity.
Keep the minus sign inside and split $-\dfrac{\pi}{12}=\dfrac{\pi}{6}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$:

$$\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)=\cos\frac{\pi}{6}\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\sin\frac{\pi}{4}
=\frac{\sqrt6}{4}+\frac{\sqrt2}{4}$$

Same answer, which is the point.
Cosine does not care about the order of the subtraction, because it is even.
This route is a useful self-check but it is one more thing to get right under pressure.

Another valid split is $\dfrac{\pi}{12}=\dfrac{\pi}{3}-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$:

$$\cos\frac{\pi}{3}\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\sin\frac{\pi}{4}
=\frac12\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2}
=\frac{\sqrt2}{4}+\frac{\sqrt6}{4}$$

Verified numerically: both splits give 0.9659258263.

### Route C - half angle from $\pi/6$

Since $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$ is half of $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, and $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$ sits in quadrant I so the
cosine is positive:

$$\cos\frac{\pi}{12}=+\sqrt{\frac{1+\cos(\pi/6)}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}}{2}}
=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt3}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}}{2}$$

That is a correct exact value, and it is numerically 0.9659258263, but it is an ugly *nested* radical.
It equals the clean form:

$$\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}}{2}=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$$

Both were verified numerically to 1e-11.
Denesting that by hand is real work, so if a marker or a machine wants the tidy form you have paid
extra for no reason.

### Which route, and why

| | Route A (parity + difference) | Route B (difference, no parity) | Route C (half angle) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lines of work | 4 | 4 | 5, plus denesting |
| Where errors hide | the middle sign of $\cos(A-B)$ | the middle sign, *and* the split direction | choosing the $\pm$, and the nested radical |
| Output form | clean surd | clean surd | nested radical |
| Verdict | **use this** | good cross-check | reserve for the $\pi/8$ family |

**Under exam pressure, use Route A.**
Half-angle is the right tool for $\pi/8$ angles, where no sum of standard angles will reach the target.
It is the wrong tool for $\pi/12$ angles, where a difference of standard angles lands exactly on it.

## 5. The exam-ready write-up

If the paper you sit asks for method, these are the four sentences that earn the marks.
Copy the shape, not just the numbers.

> 1. Since cosine is an even function, $\cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{12}\right)=\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{12}\right)$.
> 2. Writing $\dfrac{\pi}{12}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ expresses the angle as a difference of two standard angles.
> 3. By the cosine difference identity $\cos(A-B)=\cos A\cos B+\sin A\sin B$,
>    $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{12}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}+\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\dfrac12$.
> 4. Therefore $\cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{12}\right)=\dfrac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$.

Three things a marker looks for that students routinely skip: **naming** the parity property,
**naming** the identity used, and stating the decomposition as a deliberate choice rather than letting
$\dfrac{\pi}{4}-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ appear from nowhere.
Those are free marks.

## 6. Verify before you commit

This takes eight seconds and it catches nearly every sign slip.

**The magnitude check.**
$\sqrt6\approx 2.449$ and $\sqrt2\approx 1.414$, so $(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/4\approx 3.863/4\approx 0.966$.
Is that plausible?
$-\pi/12$ is $-15^\circ$, a tiny angle, and $\cos 0=1$, so the answer must be positive and a hair
under 1.
0.966 fits.

**The three cheap invariants.**

1. **Range.** Any $\sin$ or $\cos$ answer must sit in $[-1,1]$. If you produce $(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)/2\approx1.93$ you have dropped a factor of 2.
2. **Sign.** Get the quadrant from ASTC *independently* of the algebra, then check the algebra agrees. Two independent routes to the same sign is what makes it a check rather than a restatement.
3. **Neighbour sanity.** $\cos$ is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, so $\cos(\pi/12)$ must be bigger than $\cos(\pi/6)\approx0.866$ and smaller than $\cos 0 = 1$. It is.

**The four numbers worth memorising outright.**

$$\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\approx 0.9659,\qquad \frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}\approx 0.2588,\qquad
2-\sqrt3\approx 0.2679,\qquad \frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}\approx 0.9239$$

Every answer in the 15-degree family is one of the first three, up to a sign.
If your surd does not evaluate to 0.9659, 0.2588 or 0.2679 in absolute value, you have made an
arithmetic error rather than discovered a new value.

| Rule | Do | Not |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

The single most expensive mistake available on this question is `sqrt(6)+sqrt(2)/4`, which parses as

## 8. The variation catalog

This is the part that makes the question repeatable.
Everything below has been numerically verified.

### Family 0 - the freebies (multiples of $\pi/6$, $\pi/4$, $\pi/3$)

No formula.
Reference angle plus ASTC.
$\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}=-\frac12$, $\sin\frac{5\pi}{6}=\frac12$, $\tan\frac{7\pi}{4}=-1$,
$\cos\frac{5\pi}{2}=0$.
If the denominator is 2, 3, 4 or 6, you are in this family and you should not be writing a formula.

### Family 1 - the $\pi/12$ (15 degree) family: sum and difference

This is Q1.1's own family, and it is the single most likely variation to appear.
Every entry is built from $15=45-30$, $75=45+30$, or $105=60+45$.

| $k$ | angle | deg | $\cos\frac{k\pi}{12}$ | $\sin\frac{k\pi}{12}$ | $\tan\frac{k\pi}{12}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | $\pi/12$ | 15 | $\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}$ | $2-\sqrt3$ |
| 5 | $5\pi/12$ | 75 | $\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}$ | $\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $2+\sqrt3$ |
| 7 | $7\pi/12$ | 105 | $\frac{\sqrt2-\sqrt6}{4}$ | $\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $-(2+\sqrt3)$ |
| 11 | $11\pi/12$ | 165 | $-\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}$ | $\sqrt3-2$ |
| 13 | $13\pi/12$ | 195 | $-\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $-\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}$ | $2-\sqrt3$ |
| 17 | $17\pi/12$ | 255 | $\frac{\sqrt2-\sqrt6}{4}$ | $-\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $2+\sqrt3$ |
| 19 | $19\pi/12$ | 285 | $\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}$ | $-\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $-(2+\sqrt3)$ |
| 23 | $23\pi/12$ | 345 | $\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}$ | $-\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}$ | $\sqrt3-2$ |

Read the table as structure, not as 24 separate facts.
There are only two magnitudes here, 0.9659 and 0.2588, and cosine and sine swap which one they get as
you move between the 15 and 75 columns.
Negative angles fold in through parity: $\cos(-5\pi/12)=\cos(5\pi/12)$ and
$\sin(-\pi/12)=-\sin(\pi/12)$.

Decompositions that reach each one: $15=45-30$, $75=45+30$, $105=60+45$, $165=120+45$ or $135+30$,
$195=150+45$, $255=210+45$, $285=225+60$, $345=300+45$.
In practice, reduce to a reference angle of $15^\circ$ or $75^\circ$ first and let ASTC supply the sign.
That is fewer moves than hunting for a clever split.

### Family 2 - the $\pi/8$ (22.5 degree) family: half angle

No sum of standard angles hits $22.5^\circ$, so here half-angle is the *only* tool.

| angle | deg | $\cos$ | $\sin$ | $\tan$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\pi/8$ | 22.5 | $\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $\sqrt2-1$ |
| $3\pi/8$ | 67.5 | $\frac{\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $\sqrt2+1$ |
| $5\pi/8$ | 112.5 | $-\frac{\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $-(\sqrt2+1)$ |
| $7\pi/8$ | 157.5 | $-\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{2-\sqrt2}}{2}$ | $-(\sqrt2-1)$ |

Worked example, $\cos\frac{\pi}{8}$.
$\frac{\pi}{8}$ is half of $\frac{\pi}{4}$, and $\frac{\pi}{8}$ is in quadrant I so take the plus sign:

$$\cos\frac{\pi}{8}=+\sqrt{\frac{1+\cos(\pi/4)}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}}{2}}
=\sqrt{\frac{2+\sqrt2}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}}{2}\approx0.9239$$

The nested radical is the *expected* answer form here.
Do not try to denest it.
Möbius accepts `sqrt(2+sqrt(2))/2`.

### Family 3 - reciprocal functions

Do the cosine or sine first, then flip and rationalise.
Never look for a "secant difference formula"; there is not one.

$$\sec\left(-\frac{\pi}{12}\right)=\frac{1}{\cos(\pi/12)}=\frac{4}{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}
=\frac{4(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)}{(\sqrt6)^2-(\sqrt2)^2}=\frac{4(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)}{4}=\sqrt6-\sqrt2\approx1.0353$$

$$\csc\frac{\pi}{12}=\sqrt6+\sqrt2\approx3.8637,\qquad \cot\frac{\pi}{12}=2+\sqrt3\approx3.7321$$

The conjugate step is the whole content of this variation.
Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt6-\sqrt2$, and note that the difference of squares collapses the
denominator to $6-2=4$, which cancels the 4 on top.
That is why the secant and cosecant of $\pi/12$ come out so clean.

### Family 4 - tangent of a 15-degree angle

Two routes.
The tangent difference formula:

$$\tan\frac{\pi}{12}=\tan(45^\circ-30^\circ)=\frac{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt3}}{1+\frac{1}{\sqrt3}}
=\frac{\sqrt3-1}{\sqrt3+1}$$

That is exact but not simplified.
Rationalise by multiplying top and bottom by $\sqrt3-1$:

$$\frac{(\sqrt3-1)^2}{3-1}=\frac{3-2\sqrt3+1}{2}=\frac{4-2\sqrt3}{2}=2-\sqrt3\approx0.2679$$

Or just divide the two values you already have:
$\tan\frac{\pi}{12}=\frac{\sin}{\cos}=\frac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}$, then rationalise to the
same $2-\sqrt3$.
If you already computed both the sine and the cosine, dividing beats learning a third formula.

### Family 5 - inverse trig composition (reference triangle)

$\sin(\arccos\frac35)$ and its relatives.
There is no identity for these.
**Draw the triangle.**

$\arccos\frac35$ is "the angle whose cosine is $\frac35$", so label a right triangle with
adjacent $=3$ and hypotenuse $=5$.
Pythagoras gives opposite $=\sqrt{25-9}=4$.
Then read off whatever the outer function wants:

$$\sin\left(\arccos\frac35\right)=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}=\frac45$$

$$\cos\left(\arcsin\frac13\right)=\frac{\sqrt8}{3}=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\approx0.9428,\qquad
\tan\left(\arcsin\frac13\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt8}=\frac{\sqrt2}{4}$$

$$\tan\left(\arccos\left(-\frac23\right)\right)=-\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\approx-1.1180$$

That last one carries the trap.
The *range* of $\arccos$ is $[0,\pi]$, so a negative cosine puts the angle in quadrant II, where
tangent is negative.
Get the magnitude from the triangle, then get the sign from the inverse function's range.
The ranges you need: $\arcsin$ and $\arctan$ land in $[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]$, and $\arccos$
lands in $[0,\pi]$.

The algebraic version uses the same method: $\tan(\arcsin x)=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$, from a triangle
with opposite $x$ and hypotenuse 1.

### Family 6 - double-angle composition

$\cos(2\arcsin\frac13)$ looks worse than it is.
Use $\cos2\theta=1-2\sin^2\theta$ and let $\theta=\arcsin\frac13$, so $\sin\theta=\frac13$ by definition:

$$\cos\left(2\arcsin\frac13\right)=1-2\left(\frac13\right)^2=1-\frac29=\frac79\approx0.7778$$

The whole trick is picking the *form* of the double-angle identity whose input you already know.
You know $\sin\theta$, so choose the $1-2\sin^2\theta$ form and never compute $\cos\theta$ at all.

More of the same:
$\sin(2\arccos\frac35)=2\cdot\frac45\cdot\frac35=\frac{24}{25}$ via $\sin2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$
and the 3-4-5 triangle.
$\tan(2\arctan\frac12)=\frac{2\cdot\frac12}{1-\frac14}=\frac43$.
$\cos(2\arctan\frac34)=\frac{7}{25}=0.28$, from the 3-4-5 triangle and $2\cos^2\theta-1$.

### Family 7 - the identity read backwards

Sometimes the question hands you the *right-hand* side of an identity and the exact value is trivial
once you recognise it.
Do not grind these.

$$\sin75^\circ\cos15^\circ+\cos75^\circ\sin15^\circ=\sin(75^\circ+15^\circ)=\sin90^\circ=1$$

$$\cos^2\frac{\pi}{8}-\sin^2\frac{\pi}{8}=\cos\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$$

$$2\sin\frac{\pi}{12}\cos\frac{\pi}{12}=\sin\frac{\pi}{6}=\frac12$$

$$\cos\frac{\pi}{12}\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\sin\frac{\pi}{12}\sin\frac{\pi}{4}
=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi}{12}\right)=\cos\frac{\pi}{6}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$$

The recognition cue: two products added or subtracted, with the *same two angles* appearing in both
terms, is always a sum or difference formula collapsing.
A squared cosine minus a squared sine of the same angle is always a double angle collapsing.

### Family 8 - deeper nesting

$\pi/24$ is $7.5^\circ$, which is half of $15^\circ$.
Apply half-angle to the answer you already have:

$$\cos\frac{\pi}{24}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\cos(\pi/12)}{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}}}{2}\approx0.9914$$

Verified numerically.
Doubly nested radicals are the correct final form.
This is unlikely on the midterm, but knowing the pattern continues tells you the method is general
rather than a bag of memorised special cases.

## 9. The trap list

Ranked by how often they actually cost marks.

1. **The middle sign of $\cos(A\pm B)$.** The formula's sign is the *opposite* of the bracket's, so $\cos(A-B)$ has a plus in the middle. This is the number one error on this question.
2. **Bracketing the numerator on entry.** `sqrt(6)+sqrt(2)/4` is a different number from `(sqrt(6)+sqrt(2))/4`.
3. **Giving a decimal.** "Exact value" forbids it, whatever the precision.
4. **Applying odd-function parity to cosine.** $\cos(-x)=+\cos x$. Writing $-\cos x$ flips the sign of the answer for free.
5. **Choosing the half-angle $\pm$ from the wrong quadrant.** The sign comes from $\theta/2$, the angle you are computing, not from $\theta$.
6. **Degrees versus radians.** $\pi/12$ is $15^\circ$, not $12^\circ$. If the calculator is in degrees, every numerical check silently lies to you.
7. **Forgetting to reduce past $2\pi$.** $\frac{25\pi}{12}$ is coterminal with $\frac{\pi}{12}$.
8. **Not rationalising a reciprocal.** $\frac{4}{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}$ is exact but may be marked unsimplified on a written paper. Möbius accepts it; a human marker may not.
9. **Reaching for a formula in Family 0.** If the denominator is 2, 3, 4 or 6, recall is the method.
10. **Trying to denest a $\pi/8$ answer.** The nested radical *is* the answer there.

If magnitudes keep coming out right and signs keep coming out wrong, the problem is ASTC and reference
angles, not the identities, and drilling more identities will not fix it.

## 11. The one-page decision tree

```
Exact value of a trig function of THETA?
|
+-- Is there an inverse trig function inside?
|     YES -> draw a REFERENCE TRIANGLE. Magnitude from the sides,
|            sign from the inverse function's RANGE.
|            (arcsin, arctan -> [-pi/2, pi/2];  arccos -> [0, pi])
|            Outer function doubled? Use a DOUBLE-ANGLE identity
|            in the form whose input you already know.
|
+-- Is it two products added, or cos^2 - sin^2?
|     YES -> an identity is collapsing. Read it BACKWARDS. Usually one line.
|
+-- Otherwise: STRIP first.
      1. Negative angle?  cos/sec: drop the sign.  Others: pull it out front.
      2. Bigger than 2pi? Subtract full turns.
      3. Note the quadrant. Get the sign from ASTC and set it aside.
      4. Reduce to a reference angle in [0, pi/2].
      Now look at the denominator of the reference angle:
        2, 3, 4, 6      -> RECALL. No formula.
        12              -> SUM/DIFFERENCE formula (15 = 45-30, 75 = 45+30).
        8               -> HALF-ANGLE formula. A nested radical is the answer.
        24 or worse     -> HALF-ANGLE twice.
      5. Reattach the sign from step 3.
      6. Check: is it in [-1,1]? is the decimal 0.9659 / 0.2588 / 0.2679 / 0.9239 / 0.3827?
      7. Type it with every bracket, and PREVIEW.
```

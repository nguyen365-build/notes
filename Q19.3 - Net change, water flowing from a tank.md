# Q19.3 - Net change, water flowing from a tank

**The question (Final Exam 1, item 12, 3 points).** Water flows from the bottom of a storage tank at a rate of $r(t) = 180 - 6t$ liters per minute, where $0 \le t \le 50.$
Find the amount of water that flows from the tank during the first 15 minutes.

**The answer.** $\displaystyle\int_0^{15}(180-6t)\,dt = \Big[180t - 3t^2\Big]_0^{15} = 2700 - 675 = 2025$ litres.

**What to type into Möbius:** `2025`

## 0. The 30-second version

```
r(t) is a RATE (litres per minute).  You are asked for an AMOUNT (litres).
Rate -> amount is one operation: integrate.

  amount out over [0,15]  =  int_0^15 r(t) dt

  antiderivative  R(t) = 180t - 3t^2        (check: R' = 180 - 6t  OK)
  R(15) - R(0)    = 2700 - 675 - 0          =  2025 litres

  8-second check: r is LINEAR, so average rate = (180+90)/2 = 135,
                  and 135 x 15 = 2025.  Same number, no calculus.
```

That is the whole question, and the calculus takes one line.
The rest of this page is about the four things that can go wrong on the way, three of which produce a number you cannot distinguish from the right one.

## 1. What this question is really testing

| The reflex | Where it comes back |
| :--- | :--- |
| A rate integrates to a **change** in the quantity | Q19.6 and Q19.7 (average value), Q19.8 (displacement), Q19.9 (a sprinter) |
| The Fundamental Theorem is what licenses that | Q18.1, which is the same theorem read the other way |
| A change is not an amount until an initial condition is added | any "how much is left" wording, and every differential-equation question later |
| A signed rate integrates to a signed change | Q19.8, where displacement and distance differ |
| Units of the answer come from the integrand times the variable | Q6.1 section 2.2, and every applied question in the bank |

**The single sentence this question exists to install is the Net Change Theorem.**

> If $Q$ is a quantity and $Q' = r$ is its rate of change, then $\displaystyle\int_a^b r(t)\,dt = Q(b) - Q(a)$.
> The integral of a rate of change is the **net change** in the quantity, over that interval.

That is not a new theorem.
It is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2, with the letters renamed: `Q18.1 - Differentiate an integral with variable limits.md` section 2a states both halves side by side, and it is Part 2 (given $F' = f$, the definite integral is $F(b) - F(a)$) that this question uses.
Read that table rather than re-deriving it.
The only thing Q19.3 adds is a physical reading of $F$: when $f$ is a flow rate, $F$ is a volume.

### Why the marks are cheap and the trap is not

3 points, out of 124 on the paper, is 2.42 percent, and it is the lightest item on Final Exam 1.
An examiner does not put a hard integral in a 3-point slot.
**The difficulty is deliberately not in the antiderivative; it is in knowing what you were asked for.**
That is inference, since no marking scheme exists in any course file, but the point value is the evidence for it.

## 2. The four things you must have cold

### 2a. Rate in, quantity out, and how the units prove it

A rate is a quotient: some quantity per one unit of the input variable.
`Q6.1 - Average and instantaneous rate of population change.md` section 2.2 states the rule that never has an exception, and its own table already carries this exact row: *litres in a tank at minute $t$* gives a rate in **litres per minute**.

The integral multiplies the integrand by the variable, so it multiplies the units too:

$$\underbrace{\left[\frac{\text{L}}{\text{min}}\right]}_{r(t)}\times\underbrace{\big[\text{min}\big]}_{dt} \;=\; \big[\text{L}\big]$$

The minutes cancel and a volume comes out.
**This is a check you can run in three seconds and it catches an entire class of error that no arithmetic check can see.**

Do it as a cancellation, not as a sentence.
Writing "the answer is in litres because it is water" is not a check, because it is true whatever you computed.
Writing $\frac{\text{L}}{\text{min}}\times\text{min} = \text{L}$ is a check, because a wrong operation gives a wrong unit.

| what you wrote | its units | verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\int_0^{15} r\,dt$ | L | a volume, which is what was asked |
| $r(15)$ | L/min | a rate, not a volume |
| $r'(t)$ | L/min$^2$ | an acceleration of the flow |
| $\dfrac{1}{15}\int_0^{15} r\,dt$ | L/min | the average rate, which is Q19.6's question |
| $\int_0^{15} r\,dt \;+\; r(15)$ | inhomogeneous | not a quantity at all |

The last row is the one worth staring at.
**You cannot add a volume to a rate**, and a units gate rejects that sum before any number is computed.
The verification section implements exactly this as exponent vectors over (litre, minute) and proves it rejects all four wrong units above plus the inhomogeneous sum.

Note also that the constants carry units.
The $180$ in $180 - 6t$ is $180$ L/min, and the $6$ is $6$ L/min$^2$, so that $6t$ is again L/min.
A student who treats $180$ and $6$ as pure numbers has no way to notice when a conversion is needed.

### 2b. Net change is a change, and the classic lost mark

$$\int_a^b Q'(t)\,dt = Q(b) - Q(a) \qquad\text{so}\qquad Q(b) = Q(a) + \int_a^b Q'(t)\,dt$$

The integral by itself is the **second** of those, not the first.
If the question asks how much water is **left in the tank**, the integral is only half the answer and the initial volume is the other half.

Q19.3 does not ask that.
It asks for the amount that **flowed out**, which is exactly the integral, so no initial condition is needed here.
**That is precisely why the mark is so easy to lose on the next question of this type**: you learn the shape on a question where the initial condition is absent, and then meet one where it is not.

| the wording | what to compute | needs $V(0)$? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "the amount that flows from the tank" | $\int_0^{15} r$ | no |
| "the net change in the volume in the tank" | $-\int_0^{15} r$ | no |
| "how much water remains in the tank" | $V(0) - \int_0^{15} r$ | **yes** |
| "the total volume of water moved" | $\int_0^{15}\lvert r\rvert$ | no |

Three of those four are different numbers on a general question.
On **this** question the first and the fourth are the same, and that is the subject of section 6.

### 2c. Signed flow, and what the wording fixes

$r(t)$ here is defined as the rate at which water flows **from** the tank.
So $r > 0$ means water leaving.
Two consistent bookkeepings exist and you must pick one and stay in it:

| convention | the rate | the integral over $[0,15]$ | reads as |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **outflow positive** (the stem's) | $r(t) = 180-6t$ | $+2025$ | 2025 L left the tank |
| **tank contents** | $V'(t) = -r(t)$ | $-2025$ | the tank lost 2025 L |

Both are correct statements about the same physical event.
**The exam wants the first**, because it asks for "the amount of water that flows from the tank", and an amount of water is a positive quantity.
Answering $-2025$ is answering a different, unasked question correctly.

> **Decide which quantity you are accumulating before you integrate, not after you see the sign.**

That rule is unfalsifiable on this question, because the sign error here is repaired by the single most natural instinct a student has, which is that an amount of water cannot be negative.
It is fatal one question later.
See section 9.

### 2d. Where this rate stops making sense

$r(t) = 180 - 6t$ is zero at $t = 30$ and **negative** for $t > 30$.
The stem states the domain as $0 \le t \le 50$, so on the last 20 minutes, which is 40 percent of the stated domain, the model says water flows out of the tank at a negative rate.
A negative outflow is an inflow, which contradicts the stem's own sentence about water flowing from the bottom of a tank.

**This is a defect in the question's numbers, not in your reading of them.**
It is recorded here because it is where the interesting mathematics of this category actually lives, and because the asked interval $[0,15]$ sits entirely inside the physically sensible part, so the defect costs nothing on the exam.
The natural repair is a domain of $0 \le t \le 30$, at which point the tank has delivered $2700$ litres and the flow stops.

## 3. The universal procedure

> 1. **Name the quantity and the rate.** Write "$V$ = litres delivered, $r = V'$ in L/min". If you cannot name the quantity, you do not yet know what the integral means.
> 2. **Check the units of the rate**, as a quotient. Output unit over input unit.
> 3. **Decide what is being asked**: a change, a final amount, or a total moved. Section 2b's four-row table is the whole decision.
> 4. **Find where the rate changes sign inside the interval.** If it does not, change and total agree and you may integrate straight through. If it does, they differ and you must split.
> 5. **Antidifferentiate, then check by differentiating back.** The check is faster than the antidifferentiation.
> 6. **Evaluate $F(b) - F(a)$, upper minus lower**, and add the initial condition only if step 3 said to.
> 7. **State the units**, from step 2, and sanity-check the magnitude against rate times time.

### The classify table

| The stem says | The tool | Watch for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "at a rate of ..." and asks for an amount | integrate the rate | the units |
| "how much remains / how many are there at time $b$" | $Q(a) + \int_a^b r$ | the initial condition |
| "total distance / total volume moved" | $\int \lvert r\rvert$, split at the zeros | the split |
| "net change / displacement" | $\int r$, no split | do not take absolute values |
| "average rate" | $\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b r$ | dividing, and the units change back |
| a rate given as a table or graph | trapezoid or Riemann sums | you have no antiderivative |
| "at what time does ... " | solve $F(T) = $ target | a spurious second root |

## 4. Q19.3 worked eight ways

Every route below was evaluated independently, and all eight agree to $6.594\times10^{-12}$, which is roundoff.

### Route A - the antiderivative, the one to write down

$$\int_0^{15}(180-6t)\,dt = \Big[180t - 3t^2\Big]_0^{15} = (2700 - 675) - 0 = 2025$$

### Route B - the trapezoid, which is exact here

$r$ is linear, so the region under it is a trapezoid with parallel sides $r(0) = 180$ and $r(15) = 90$ and width 15.

$$A = \frac{180+90}{2}\times 15 = 135 \times 15 = 2025$$

### Route C - the midpoint rate

Again because $r$ is linear, the average rate is the rate at the midpoint: $r(7.5) = 135$, and $135 \times 15 = 2025$.

### Route D - rectangle plus triangle

Split the trapezoid horizontally: a $90 \times 15$ rectangle underneath, and a triangle of base 15 and height $180-90=90$ on top.

$$1350 + \tfrac12(15)(90) = 1350 + 675 = 2025$$

### Route E - substitution

Let $u = 180-6t$, so $du = -6\,dt$, and the limits run from $u=180$ down to $u=90$.

$$\int_{180}^{90} u\cdot\left(-\tfrac16\right)du = \frac{1}{6}\int_{90}^{180}u\,du = \frac{180^2-90^2}{12} = \frac{32400-8100}{12} = 2025$$

### Route F - tanh-sinh quadrature

A double-exponential quadrature rule that never sees the closed form returns $2025$ to $10^{-10}$.

### Route G - composite Simpson

At $n = 17, 23, 37, 51, 101, 199, 501$ and $1003$, all deliberately chosen odd and off multiples of 8, Simpson returns $2025$ to $10^{-10}$.

### Route H - average rate times time

$\overline{r} = 135$ L/min over 15 min is $2025$ L.
This is Route C stated in the language of Q19.6.

### Route comparison

| Route | Cost | Generalises? | Catches a wrong interval? | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A antiderivative | one line | **to every rate** | yes | **use this one** |
| B trapezoid | one line | only to linear rates | yes | the check, not the method |
| C midpoint | one line | only to linear rates | yes | fastest check |
| D geometry | two lines | only to linear rates | yes | good for a graph-given rate |
| E substitution | three lines | to composite rates | yes | overkill here |
| F tanh-sinh | not by hand | to anything | yes | verification only |
| G Simpson | not by hand | to anything | yes | verification only |
| H average rate | one line | to every rate | yes | the Q19.6 phrasing |

**Under exam pressure use Route A and check with Route C.**
Route A is the only one that survives a non-linear rate, and Route C costs one subtraction and one multiplication.
The pairing matters more than either half: A and C fail in different ways, so agreement between them is real evidence.

**But note carefully what Route C is not.** Routes B, C and D are exact **only because $r$ is linear**.
On the exponential rate in drill D7 the midpoint estimate is not the answer, and a student who learned "average the endpoints" from this question will carry a broken tool into the next one.
That is measured, not asserted: see the trap list, item 5.

## 5. The exam-ready write-up

These are the sentences that earn method marks if part marks exist, which is inference since no marking scheme is in any course file.

> The rate $r(t)$ is the derivative of the volume $V$ that has flowed out, so by the Net Change Theorem the amount that flows out between $t=0$ and $t=15$ is $\int_0^{15} r(t)\,dt$.
> $r$ is continuous on $[0,15]$ and $R(t)=180t-3t^2$ satisfies $R'(t)=180-6t=r(t)$, so by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus,
> $$\int_0^{15}(180-6t)\,dt = R(15)-R(0) = (2700-675)-0 = 2025.$$
> $r(t) > 0$ throughout $[0,15]$, so this is both the net change and the total volume delivered.
> The units are $\text{(L/min)}\times\text{min} = \text{L}$.
> **2025 litres of water flow from the tank during the first 15 minutes.**

Five sentences.
The third one is the one nobody writes and it is the one that shows you knew there was a question to answer.

## 6. Verify before you commit

### The eight-second checks, in the order they catch things

| Check | On this question | What it catches |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Units** | (L/min)(min) = L | answering with a rate, differentiating instead |
| **Magnitude bracket** | between $r(15)\cdot 15 = 1350$ and $r(0)\cdot 15 = 2700$ | a wrong interval, a dropped coefficient |
| **Midpoint** | $135\times15 = 2025$ | the antiderivative's arithmetic |
| **Sign** | $r>0$ on $[0,15]$, so the answer is positive | an orientation slip |
| **Lower limit** | $R(0)=0$, so it happens not to matter | nothing, here - see below |

The magnitude bracket is worth its own line.
For any **decreasing** positive rate, the true amount is strictly between (final rate)$\times$(time) and (initial rate)$\times$(time).
Here that is $1350 < A < 2700$, and $2025$ sits neatly in the middle because the rate is linear.
**This bracket rejects twelve of the sixteen wrong answers in the census**, measured rather than estimated, and that is more than the units check (4) and more than a positivity check (5).
It is the strongest single check available on this question.

### The check that cannot fire here, and why you should know that

$R(0) = 180(0) - 3(0)^2 = 0$.
So on this question, writing $R(15)$ and forgetting $-R(0)$ entirely gives the same answer, $2025$.
**A student who never learned to subtract the lower limit gets full marks on Q19.3 and loses them on any interval not starting at zero.**
Drill D2, which runs from $t=10$ to $t=20$, is the same question with that hiding point removed.

### Numbers worth memorising

| Quantity | Value |
| ---: | :--- |
| $r(0)$ | 180 L/min |
| $r(15)$ | 90 L/min |
| $r(30)$ | 0, the shut-off |
| $r(50)$ | $-120$, which the model should not permit |
| $\int_0^{15} r$ | **2025 L** |
| $\int_0^{30} r$ | 2700 L, everything the model delivers |

## 8. The variation catalog

Read each family as a **structure**, not as a list of facts.
Every family below is the same theorem; what changes is which of the four questions in section 2b's table is being asked, and whether the rate changes sign.
Thirteen distinct families are exercised by the verified rows in section 10 and below.

### Family 0 - the plain net change

**Trigger.** A rate is given, an interval is given, and the word is "how much" or "the amount".
**Tool.** $\int_a^b r$, no initial condition, no absolute value.
**Shape.** Antidifferentiate, evaluate upper minus lower.

| Instance | Answer |
| :--- | ---: |
| $r=180-6t$ on $[0,15]$ (the question) | 2025 L |
| $r=180-6t$ on $[0,25]$ | 2625 L |
| $r=180-6t$ on $[10,20]$ | 900 L |
| $r=200-4t$ on $[0,10]$ | 1800 L |

The third row is the useful one: it starts away from zero, so the lower limit is live.

### Family 1 - the final amount, which needs the initial condition

**Trigger.** "How much is left", "how many are there at time $b$", "what is the population in year $b$".
**Tool.** $Q(b) = Q(a) + \int_a^b r$, with attention to whether $r$ adds or removes.
**Shape.** Compute the integral, then do one addition or subtraction that the integral cannot tell you about.

| Instance | Answer |
| :--- | ---: |
| Tank holds 4000 L at $t=0$; how much at $t=15$? | 1975 L |
| Tank holds 2025 L at $t=0$; how much at $t=15$? | 0 L, exactly empty |
| Tank holds 3000 L; does it ever empty under this model? | **no**, the model delivers only 2700 L |

That last row is the family's best question, because the answer is not a number.
The tank cannot empty, because the flow shuts off at $t=30$ having delivered 2700 litres.

### Family 2 - the rate changes sign, so net and total differ

**Trigger.** The rate is zero somewhere strictly inside the interval.
**Tool.** Find the zeros. Net change is $\int r$ straight through; total moved is $\int \lvert r\rvert$, split at each zero and added with all pieces positive.
**Shape.** Two computations from the same pieces, differing only in the signs you attach.

| Instance | Net change | Total moved |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| $r = 180-6t$ on $[0,15]$ | 2025 L | **2025 L, the same** |
| $r = 180-6t$ on $[0,50]$ | 1500 L | 3900 L |
| $r = 180-6t$ on $[0,30]$ | 2700 L | 2700 L, the same |
| $v = t^2-4$ on $[0,3]$ | $-3$ m | $23/3 \approx 7.6667$ m |

Read the column pair, not the rows.
**They agree exactly when the rate does not change sign inside the interval**, and that is the only condition.
The first row is the exam question and the columns agree, which is why this family is invisible on Q19.3 and decides the mark on Q19.8.

### Family 3 - solve for the time

**Trigger.** "At what time has 1000 litres flowed out?", "when is the tank empty?", "how long until ...".
**Tool.** Set $F(T) = $ target and solve, then **discard roots outside the model's domain**.
**Shape.** $180T - 3T^2 = 1000$ gives $3T^2 - 180T + 1000 = 0$, so $T = \dfrac{180 \pm \sqrt{20400}}{6}$.

The two roots are $T = 6.1952385715$ and $T = 53.8047614285$.
Only the first is admissible.
The second is past the $t=30$ shut-off and past the stated domain $t \le 50$ as well, so it is doubly spurious.

**A quadratic in the time variable has two roots and a physical question usually has one answer.**
Say which one you rejected and why; that sentence is the difference between a solved equation and a solved problem.

### Family 4 - a rate that is not linear

**Trigger.** The rate contains an exponential, a trig function, a root, or a composite.
**Tool.** The same theorem, plus whatever antidifferentiation the rate needs.
**Shape.** Nothing changes except step 5.

| Instance | Answer |
| :--- | ---: |
| $r = 60e^{-t/10}$ on $[0,20]$ | $600(1-e^{-2}) = 518.7988300580$ |
| $r = t\sqrt{t^2+9}$ on $[0,4]$ | $\dfrac{98}{3} \approx 32.6667$ |
| $r = 100\sin(\pi t/12)$ on $[0,12]$ | $\dfrac{2400}{\pi} = 763.9437268411$ |

**The endpoint-average shortcut is wrong on every one of these**, and that is what makes this family the antidote to Route C.
For the exponential, the endpoint average is $\tfrac12(60 + 60e^{-2})\times 20 = 681.2$ against the true $518.8$, an error of 31 percent.

### Family 5 - two rates, in and out

**Trigger.** Water enters at one rate and leaves at another.
**Tool.** The net rate is the difference, and you integrate that.
**Shape.** $\int (r_{\text{in}} - r_{\text{out}})$, and everything else is Family 2.

| Instance | Net change | Total moved |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| in $5t$, out $100$, on $[0,40]$ | **0 L** | 2000 L |
| in $3t^2$, out $36t$, on $[0,12]$ | $-864$ | - |

The first row is the family's teaching instance, and the verification section confirms both numbers.
**The net change is exactly zero and two thousand litres of water actually moved.**
"Nothing changed" and "nothing happened" are different statements, and only the second is refuted by the total.

### Family 6 - the average value of the rate

**Trigger.** "Average rate of flow", "average velocity", "mean value".
**Tool.** $\dfrac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b r$, which is Q19.6's whole question.
**Shape.** Compute the integral, then divide, and note the units go back to L/min.

| Instance | Answer |
| :--- | ---: |
| average of $180-6t$ on $[0,15]$ | 135 L/min |
| average of $180-6t$ on $[0,30]$ | 90 L/min |

For a **linear** rate the average equals the midpoint value and the endpoint mean, and all three are 135 here.
For a non-linear rate they are three different numbers, which the verification section confirms on $t^2$.

### Family 7 - units and conversions

**Trigger.** The answer is wanted in different units from the ones the rate is stated in.
**Tool.** Convert once, at the end, or convert the rate and the variable together and check the integral is unchanged.
**Shape.** 2025 L is $2.025$ m$^3$; the same flow measured in L/s over 900 s integrates to the identical 2025 L, which the verification section checks by re-integrating in seconds.

**Convert at the end.** Converting mid-problem gives two chances to make the same error and no way to detect it.

### Family 8 - the rate is a table or a graph

**Trigger.** No formula is given.
**Tool.** Trapezoid or a Riemann sum; the FTC is unavailable because you have no antiderivative.
**Shape.** $\int \approx h\left(\tfrac12 r_0 + r_1 + \cdots + r_{n-1} + \tfrac12 r_n\right)$.

Sampled at $t = 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15$ the rates are $180, 162, 144, 126, 108, 90$, and the trapezoid rule with $h=3$ gives exactly **2025**.
It is exact because the rate is linear, and it will not be exact on Family 4.

### Family 9 - the model's own domain

**Trigger.** The rate goes negative, or undefined, inside the stated domain.
**Tool.** Find where, and say what it means physically before integrating past it.
**Shape.** Here $r < 0$ for $t > 30$, on 40 percent of the stated domain, which the stem's own sentence forbids.

### Family 10 - the question run backwards

**Trigger.** The answer is given and a parameter of the rate is not.
**Tool.** Integrate symbolically in the parameter, set equal to the target, solve.
**Shape.** $\int_0^{15}(180 - kt)\,dt = 2700 - \tfrac{225k}{2} = 1800$ gives $k = 8$.

### Family 11 - velocity, where this becomes Q19.8

**Trigger.** The rate is a velocity and the quantity is a position.
**Tool.** Displacement is $\int v$; distance travelled is $\int \lvert v\rvert$.
**Shape.** Exactly Family 2 with different nouns.

For $v = t^2-4$ on $[0,3]$: the velocity is zero at $t=2$, the displacement is $-3$ m and the distance is $23/3 \approx 7.6667$ m.
`Q19.1 - Area between a line and a parabola.md` section 3.1 already carries the absolute-value machinery; this is that machinery in physics clothing.

### Family 12 - the quantity is not a volume

**Trigger.** Marginal cost, population growth rate, current, power, rate of consumption.
**Tool.** The identical theorem. Nothing changes but the units.
**Shape.** If $C'(x) = 3 + 0.02x$ dollars per unit, the cost of going from 100 units to 300 is $\int_{100}^{300}C'(x)\,dx = 1400$ dollars.

Note that the input variable here is **units**, not time.
Q6.1 section 2.2 makes this point about rates and it is exactly as true about their integrals.

## 9. The trap list

Ranked by how often each actually costs a mark, which is inference, since no course file contains a rubric.

**1. Answering with a rate instead of a volume.**
$r(15) = 90$ is a plausible-looking number and it is the wrong kind of object.
Caught only by the units check.

**2. Forgetting that the integral is a change, not an amount.**
Invisible on Q19.3 and fatal the moment the wording is "how much remains".
The integral does not know the tank's initial contents and cannot tell you that you needed them.

**3. Reporting the total volume moved when the net change was asked, or the reverse.**
On this question they are the same number, so nothing you compute can tell you which one you found.
That makes Q19.3 the worst possible place to learn the distinction and the best possible place to be told about it.

**4. The lower limit.**
$R(0) = 0$ here, so omitting $-R(0)$ costs nothing.
It costs everything on an interval starting anywhere else, and drill D2 is that question.

**5. Averaging the endpoints on a curved rate.**
Routes B, C and D are exact here **because $r$ is linear**, and a student can easily leave this question believing the shortcut is general.
On $r = 60e^{-t/10}$ over $[0,20]$ the endpoint average is 31 percent high.

**6. The sign.**
$\int(-r)$ or $F(a)-F(b)$ gives $-2025$.
See below, because this one is more interesting than it looks.

**7. Integrating over the stated domain instead of the asked interval.**
$\int_0^{50} r = 1500$, which is smaller than the right answer despite covering more than three times as much time, because the model runs backwards after $t=30$.
That is a genuinely counter-intuitive number and it is worth knowing it exists.

### The census, and which errors hide

Seventeen candidate answers were enumerated and every one was computed, not typed.
No point was excluded as degenerate.

| The mistake | Value | Detected by |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| **right answer** | **2025** | - |
| midpoint rate $r(7.5)\times15$ | **2025** | **nothing - hides completely** |
| omitted the lower limit, $R(15)$ alone | **2025** | **nothing - hides completely** |
| sign flip, $F(0)-F(15)$ | $-2025$ | sign, and the student repairs it |
| initial rate $r(0)\times 15$ | 2700 | magnitude bracket |
| integrated over $[0,30]$ | 2700 | reading the interval |
| final rate $r(15)\times 15$ | 1350 | magnitude bracket |
| antiderivative $180t-6t^2$ | 1350 | magnitude bracket |
| dropped the 6, $\int(180-t)$ | 2587.5 | magnitude bracket |
| integrated $[0,50]$ | 1500 | reading the interval |
| total moved over $[0,50]$ | 3900 | magnitude bracket |
| integrated $[15,50]$ | $-525$ | sign and magnitude |
| answered $r(15)$ | 90 | units |
| answered $r(0)$ | 180 | units |
| differentiated, $r'(15)$ | $-6$ | units |
| $\int(-6)\,dt$ | $-90$ | units |
| antiderivative $180t-3t^3$ | $-7425$ | sign and magnitude |

**Two of the sixteen wrong routes return exactly 2025 and are undetectable by any check on this page.**
The midpoint shortcut hides because $r$ is linear, so the shortcut is genuinely valid here and is not really an error at all until Family 4.
The omitted lower limit hides because $R(0) = 0$ exactly.
Both are properties of *this question's numbers*, and both disappear the moment the interval does not start at zero or the rate is not linear.

Three values are each produced by two different routes: 1350, 2025 and 2700.
**Eleven of the sixteen wrong answers are positive**, so a positivity check rejects fewer than a third of them and is nearly useless here.
Seven of the sixteen lie between half and double the correct answer, so a rough plausibility check is not much better.

### Which pattern this is: Q19.1, not Q19.2

Q19.1 found three mistakes that all evaluated to $-4.5$ where the answer was $4.5$: the magnitude was right, only the sign was wrong, and the instinct "an area is positive" repaired all three into the correct answer.
Q19.2 inverted it: three errors collided at $0.221$ against a true $0.828$, so the same repair turned a visibly broken answer into a confident wrong one.

**Q19.3 is the Q19.1 pattern, and more forgiving still.**
Exactly one census entry has the right magnitude and the wrong sign, $-2025$, and "an amount of water is positive" repairs it into the correct answer.
Nothing on this question punishes the repair.

That is the danger, and it is worth stating as a sentence:

> **Q19.3 rewards the habit that Q19.2 punishes.**
> A student who learns on this question that a negative answer can simply be made positive will lose the mark on Q19.2, on Q19.8, and on any question where the flow reverses.

Both pages are on the same paper family and both traps are live in this course.
Learn the rule from Q19.3, where it is cheap; do not learn the repair.

## 11. The one-page decision tree

```
A RATE is given.  What is asked?
|
+- "how much flowed / how far / how many were added"   -> a CHANGE
|     |
|     +- does r change sign strictly INSIDE the interval?
|          |
|          +- NO  -> int_a^b r        (net = total, one computation)
|          |
|          +- YES -> which word?
|                     "net change / displacement"  -> int_a^b r
|                     "total moved / distance"     -> split at the zeros,
|                                                     add |each piece|
|
+- "how much is there AT time b"                       -> a FINAL AMOUNT
|     Q(b) = Q(a) + int_a^b r        <- the initial condition is half the answer
|
+- "average rate"                                      -> divide by (b - a)
|
+- "at what time ..."                                  -> solve F(T) = target,
                                                          then DISCARD roots
                                                          outside the domain

Then, always:
  check  units:      (units of r) x (units of t)  must be the answer's units
  check  magnitude:  for a decreasing positive rate,
                     r(b)(b-a)  <  answer  <  r(a)(b-a)
  check  sign:       decided BEFORE integrating, never repaired after
  preview the Möbius box
```

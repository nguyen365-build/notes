# Q3.1 - Five limits, algebraic and trigonometric

**The question (MT1 Q4, 16 points).**
Evaluate each of the limits below.
If a limit does not exist explain why.

- a. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2}\left(3x^2 - 2x + 1\right)$
- b. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -2}\frac{3x^2 - 2x - 16}{(x+2)^2}$
- c. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 3}\frac{2x^2 - x + 1}{x - 3}$
- d. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 2}\frac{\sqrt{6-x} - 2}{\sqrt{3-x} - 1}$
- e. $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sin(3x)}{x^2 - x}$

**The answers.**

| Part | Answer | Why, in one line | Möbius entry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $9$ | polynomial, so substitute | `9` |
| b | **DNE** ($+\infty$ from the left, $-\infty$ from the right) | $0/0$ collapses to $-14/0$ with an *odd* power left | `DNE` |
| c | **DNE** ($-\infty$ from the left, $+\infty$ from the right) | $16/0$, never was indeterminate | `DNE` |
| d | $\dfrac12$ | conjugate both roots, the $(2-x)$ cancels | `1/2` |
| e | $-3$ | $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x}\cdot\dfrac{3}{x-1}\to 1\cdot(-3)$ | `-3` |

## 0. The 30-second version

This question is not five calculations.
It is one **triage** repeated five times.
You substitute, you read what comes out, and the thing that comes out names the tool.

```
SUBSTITUTE x = a into the expression, and look at what you get.

  a finite number      -> that IS the answer, stop                    [F0]

  0 / 0                -> indeterminate: kill the common factor
                            polynomial       -> factor and cancel      [F1]
                            square root      -> multiply by conjugate  [F2]
                            sin / tan / cos  -> build sin(u)/u         [F5,F6]
                          then SUBSTITUTE AGAIN
                            (it can land on nonzero/0, see below)      [F4]

  nonzero / 0          -> NOT indeterminate. Never a number.
                          Do a SIGN CHART on the denominator:          [F3]
                            leftover power EVEN -> both sides agree -> +inf or -inf
                            leftover power ODD  -> sides disagree   -> DNE

  x -> infinity        -> divide top and bottom by the highest
                          power in the DENOMINATOR                     [F8]
```

Four lines, no calculator, and it covers every limit on both midterms.

## 1. What this question is really testing

Look at the five parts side by side and notice that they are five *different diagnoses*, not five instances of one method.
That is deliberate.

| Part | Substituting gives | Reflex the examiner is checking |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $9$ | Do you know when a limit is just an evaluation? |
| b | $0/0$ | Do you keep going after the cancellation, or stop too early? |
| c | $16/0$ | Do you know that this was never indeterminate? |
| d | $0/0$ with roots | Do you own the conjugate, on *both* the top and the bottom? |
| e | $0/0$ with a sine | Do you manufacture the matching denominator? |

The skill being tested is **triage**, not algebra.
A student who reaches for factoring on part c has already lost those marks, and a student who reaches for a sign chart on part d has too.

| Reflex | Where it comes back later in the paper |
| :--- | :--- |
| Recognising $0/0$ and killing the factor | the derivative from first principles, question 8 |
| The sign chart on a vanishing denominator | vertical asymptotes, question 6 |
| $\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1$ | the derivative of $\sin x$, and every trig limit on midterm 2 |
| One-sided limits disagreeing | curve sketching, and "the limit does not exist" conditions |

**The instruction "If a limit does not exist explain why" is worth marks.**
Two of the five parts here do not exist.
Writing "DNE" alone answers half the question.
The explanation is one sentence: *the one-sided limits disagree*, and you say what each one is.

**Format warning.**
The sample paper prints "(16 points)" and asks you to "explain why", which reads like a hand-marked written exam.

## 2. The five things you must have cold

You cannot triage at speed if you are deriving these.
They have to be recall.

### 2.1 The three signatures, and what each one means

This is the entire diagnostic.
Substitute, and one of exactly three things happens.

| What substitution gives | Name | What it means | What to do |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a finite number | determinate | the function is continuous there | you are done |
| $\dfrac00$ | **indeterminate** | top and bottom share a factor of $(x-a)$ | cancel it, then substitute again |
| $\dfrac{c}{0}$, $c \neq 0$ | **not indeterminate** | there is a vertical asymptote at $a$ | sign chart, answer is $\pm\infty$ or DNE |

The single most common error in this whole topic is treating $c/0$ as if it were $0/0$ and trying to factor.
$16/0$ is not an unknown quantity.
It is a known blow-up.
The only open question is which direction.

### 2.2 The factoring identities

| Pattern | Factors as |
| :--- | :--- |
| $a^2 - b^2$ | $(a-b)(a+b)$ |
| $a^3 - b^3$ | $(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$ |
| $a^3 + b^3$ | $(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2)$ |
| $x^n - a^n$ | $(x-a)(x^{n-1} + ax^{n-2} + \cdots + a^{n-1})$ |

And the one that actually solves part b: if substituting $x=a$ makes a polynomial zero, then $(x-a)$ **is** a factor of it.
That is the factor theorem, and it means you never have to guess.
Divide it out.

### 2.3 The conjugate

To kill a root that is causing a $0/0$, multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the part that contains the root.

$$(\sqrt{A} - B)(\sqrt{A} + B) = A - B^2$$

The root disappears and a linear factor appears in its place.
If there is a root on the top *and* a root on the bottom, you do it twice, once for each, in the same step.

### 2.4 The special trig limits

| Limit | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\displaystyle\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}$ | $1$ |
| $\displaystyle\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\tan u}{u}$ | $1$ |
| $\displaystyle\lim_{u\to0}\frac{1-\cos u}{u}$ | $0$ |
| $\displaystyle\lim_{u\to0}\frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}$ | $\dfrac12$ |

The variable $u$ must be **the same expression** on the top and the bottom.
$\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x}$ is not the special limit.
$\dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x}$ is.
You manufacture the $3x$ by multiplying and dividing by $3$, which costs you nothing and is the whole trick.

### 2.5 The sign chart

When you are at $c/0$, you need only two facts.

1. The **sign of the numerator** at $x=a$.
2. The **sign of the denominator** just to the left of $a$, and just to the right.

Then divide the signs.
The denominator is where all the work is, and its behaviour is decided by one integer: the power of $(x-a)$ that survives.

| Surviving power of $(x-a)$ | Left of $a$ | Right of $a$ | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| even (e.g. $(x-a)^2$) | positive | positive | both sides agree, so $+\infty$ or $-\infty$ |
| odd (e.g. $(x-a)^1$) | negative | positive | sides disagree, so **DNE** |

That table is the answer to parts b and c, and to the whole of Families 3 and 4 below.

## 3. The universal procedure

> 1. **Substitute** $x = a$. Write down what you get before you do anything else.
> 2. If it is a **finite number**, that is the limit. Stop.
> 3. If it is $\mathbf{c/0}$ with $c \neq 0$, go to the **sign chart** (step 7). Do not factor.
> 4. If it is $\mathbf{0/0}$, identify what kind of expression it is and kill the common factor:
>    polynomial, factor; radical, conjugate; trigonometric, build $\sin u / u$.
> 5. **Substitute again** into the simplified expression.
> 6. If you now get a finite number you are done. If you now get $c/0$, go to step 7.
> 7. **Sign chart.** Sign of the numerator at $a$, then sign of the denominator on each side.
>    Sides agree, answer $+\infty$ or $-\infty$. Sides disagree, answer DNE and say why.
> 8. **Sanity check** by substituting $x = a \pm 0.001$ and seeing whether the number you got shows up.

Step 5 is the one people skip.
Part b exists on this paper specifically to punish skipping it.

### Classify the five parts

| Part | Step 1 gives | Route from step | Step 5 gives | Final |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $9$ | 2 | - | $9$ |
| b | $0/0$ | 4, factor | $-14/0$ | 7, odd power, DNE |
| c | $16/0$ | 3 | - | 7, odd power, DNE |
| d | $0/0$ | 4, conjugate twice | $2/4$ | $1/2$ |
| e | $0/0$ | 4, build $\sin u/u$ | $1 \cdot (-3)$ | $-3$ |

## 4. Q3.1 worked, part by part

### Part a. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\left(3x^2-2x+1\right)$

Polynomials are continuous everywhere, so the limit is the value.

$$3(2)^2 - 2(2) + 1 = 12 - 4 + 1 = 9$$

**Route comparison.**
There is no second route worth having.
The only thing that can go wrong here is arithmetic, so do the three terms separately and add them, rather than in your head in one pass.
This part is 3 or 4 free marks and it takes eight seconds.
Take them and move on.

### Part b. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-2}\frac{3x^2-2x-16}{(x+2)^2}$

**Substitute first.**
Numerator: $3(4) - 2(-2) - 16 = 12 + 4 - 16 = 0$.
Denominator: $0$.
So this really is $0/0$, and the squared denominator is a decoy that makes it look like a plain asymptote.

#### Route A - factor, cancel, then sign chart (use this one)

The numerator vanishes at $x=-2$, so by the factor theorem $(x+2)$ divides it.

$$3x^2 - 2x - 16 = (x+2)(3x-8)$$

Cancel one factor of $(x+2)$:

$$\frac{(x+2)(3x-8)}{(x+2)^2} = \frac{3x-8}{x+2}$$

Substitute again: $\dfrac{3(-2)-8}{0} = \dfrac{-14}{0}$.
Now it is $c/0$ with $c = -14 < 0$, and the surviving power of $(x+2)$ is **one**, which is odd.

| Side | $x+2$ | $\dfrac{-14}{x+2}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $x \to -2^-$ (e.g. $x = -2.001$) | negative | $+\infty$ |
| $x \to -2^+$ (e.g. $x = -1.999$) | positive | $-\infty$ |

The two one-sided limits disagree, so the limit **does not exist**.

#### Route B - sign chart on the original, without cancelling

This is the route that gets the wrong answer, and it is worth seeing why.
If you look at $(x+2)^2$ and say "a square is always positive, so both sides agree", you conclude $\pm\infty$ on both sides and answer $-\infty$.
That is wrong.
The square is positive, but the numerator is *also* going to zero, and one power of $(x+2)$ cancels against it.
You cannot read the parity off the denominator you were handed.
You can only read it off the denominator **after cancelling**.

#### Route C - divide out numerically

Evaluate at $x = -2.001$ and $x = -1.999$ and watch the sign flip.
This is not a proof and earns no method marks, but it is a five-second check that Route A did not slip a sign, and on Möbius it is enough on its own.

$$f(-2.001) \approx +14000, \qquad f(-1.999) \approx -14000$$

#### Route comparison for part b

| Route | Time | Risk | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A, factor then sign chart | 40 s | low, one factorisation | **use this one under pressure** |
| B, sign chart on the original | 10 s | fails, gives $-\infty$ instead of DNE | never |
| C, numeric | 5 s | no method marks | use as the check on A |

**Use Route A.**
The moment you see $0/0$, cancel *before* you reason about signs.

### Part c. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to3}\frac{2x^2-x+1}{x-3}$

**Substitute first.**
Numerator: $2(9) - 3 + 1 = 16$.
Denominator: $0$.

This is $16/0$.
It was never indeterminate, so there is nothing to factor and nothing to cancel.
The numerator is a positive $16$, and the surviving power of $(x-3)$ is one, which is odd.

| Side | $x-3$ | $\dfrac{16}{x-3}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $x \to 3^-$ (e.g. $x = 2.999$) | negative | $-\infty$ |
| $x \to 3^+$ (e.g. $x = 3.001$) | positive | $+\infty$ |

The one-sided limits disagree, so the limit **does not exist**.

**Route comparison.**
There is only one route, and the trap is not a wrong route but a wasted minute.
Part c sits immediately after part b, which *did* need factoring, and the temptation is to carry the factoring reflex across.
Substituting first, every time, is what stops that.

### Part d. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\sqrt{6-x}-2}{\sqrt{3-x}-1}$

**Substitute first.**
Numerator: $\sqrt{4} - 2 = 0$.
Denominator: $\sqrt{1} - 1 = 0$.
So $0/0$, with a root on both the top and the bottom.

#### Route A - conjugate both, in one step (use this one)

Multiply by both conjugates at once:

$$\frac{\sqrt{6-x}-2}{\sqrt{3-x}-1}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{6-x}+2}{\sqrt{6-x}+2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3-x}+1}{\sqrt{3-x}+1}$$

The top conjugate gives $(6-x) - 4 = 2-x$.
The bottom conjugate gives $(3-x) - 1 = 2-x$.
So:

$$\frac{(2-x)\left(\sqrt{3-x}+1\right)}{\left(\sqrt{6-x}+2\right)(2-x)} = \frac{\sqrt{3-x}+1}{\sqrt{6-x}+2}$$

The $(2-x)$ cancels, which is the whole point, and now substitute:

$$\frac{\sqrt{1}+1}{\sqrt{4}+2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac12$$

Notice how clean that was: both conjugates produced *the same* factor $2-x$, so it cancelled exactly with nothing left over.
That is not luck, it is how the question was built.

#### Route B - conjugate the numerator only

Multiply by $\dfrac{\sqrt{6-x}+2}{\sqrt{6-x}+2}$ alone:

$$\frac{2-x}{\left(\sqrt{6-x}+2\right)\left(\sqrt{3-x}-1\right)}$$

Substituting now gives $\dfrac{0}{4 \cdot 0}$, still $0/0$.
You have to conjugate the bottom anyway.
This costs an extra step and an extra chance to drop a sign, but it does arrive.

#### Route C - substitute $u = 2-x$

Let $u = 2-x$, so $6-x = 4+u$ and $3-x = 1+u$, and $u \to 0$:

$$\frac{\sqrt{4+u}-2}{\sqrt{1+u}-1}$$

Each piece is now a standard "root minus its value" form, and each conjugates to $\dfrac{u}{\sqrt{4+u}+2}$ and $\dfrac{u}{\sqrt{1+u}+1}$.
Dividing gives $\dfrac{\sqrt{1+u}+1}{\sqrt{4+u}+2} \to \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac12$.
Same answer, more machinery.

#### Route comparison for part d

| Route | Time | Risk | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A, both conjugates at once | 50 s | low | **use this one under pressure** |
| B, numerator conjugate only | 80 s | medium, you must notice you are still at $0/0$ | fallback |
| C, substitute $u = 2-x$ | 90 s | low but slow | only if the algebra is uglier than this |

**Use Route A.**
Two roots means two conjugates, and doing them in the same line is faster than doing them in two.

### Part e. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin(3x)}{x^2-x}$

**Substitute first.**
Numerator: $\sin 0 = 0$.
Denominator: $0 - 0 = 0$.
So $0/0$, with a sine, which means the special limit.

#### Route A - factor the denominator, then build $\sin u / u$ (use this one)

First factor the denominator, because it hides an $x$:

$$x^2 - x = x(x-1)$$

Now the expression is $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x(x-1)}$.
The special limit needs $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x}$, so multiply and divide by $3$:

$$\frac{\sin 3x}{x(x-1)} = \frac{\sin 3x}{3x}\cdot\frac{3x}{x(x-1)} = \frac{\sin 3x}{3x}\cdot\frac{3}{x-1}$$

The split is now clean, because each factor has its own finite limit:

$$\underbrace{\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin 3x}{3x}}_{=\,1} \cdot \underbrace{\lim_{x\to0}\frac{3}{x-1}}_{=\,-3} = 1 \cdot (-3) = -3$$

The minus sign comes from $x-1 \to -1$, and it is where marks get lost.

#### Route B - substitute $u = 3x$

Let $u = 3x$, so $x = u/3$ and $u \to 0$:

$$\frac{\sin u}{\left(\frac{u}{3}\right)^2 - \frac{u}{3}} = \frac{\sin u}{\frac{u^2}{9} - \frac{u}{3}} = \frac{\sin u}{\frac{u}{3}\left(\frac{u}{3}-1\right)} = \frac{\sin u}{u}\cdot\frac{3}{\frac{u}{3}-1}$$

As $u \to 0$ this is $1 \cdot \dfrac{3}{-1} = -3$.
Correct, but the fractions inside fractions are an unforced error waiting to happen.

#### Route C - L'Hopital's rule

$\dfrac{3\cos 3x}{2x-1} \to \dfrac{3}{-1} = -3$.
This is by far the fastest, and on Möbius, where only the number is graded, it is a legitimate way to check yourself.
But **L'Hopital is not in scope for midterm 1**: it belongs to a later unit, and on a written paper that asks you to show method it can earn nothing.
Use it as a check, never as the answer.

#### Route comparison for part e

| Route | Time | Risk | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A, factor then build $\sin u/u$ | 45 s | low, watch the sign of $x-1$ | **use this one under pressure** |
| B, substitute $u = 3x$ | 70 s | medium, nested fractions | avoid |
| C, L'Hopital | 15 s | out of scope on midterm 1 | check only |

**Use Route A.**
Factor the denominator *before* you go looking for the special limit, because the factoring is what reveals the $x$ that pairs with the $\sin 3x$.

## 5. The exam-ready write-up

If the paper you sit asks you to justify, these are the sentences that earn the method marks.
Each is one line.

**(a)** $3x^2-2x+1$ is a polynomial and therefore continuous at $x=2$, so the limit is $3(2)^2-2(2)+1 = 9$.

**(b)** Substituting gives $0/0$.
Since the numerator vanishes at $x=-2$, $(x+2)$ is a factor: $3x^2-2x-16 = (x+2)(3x-8)$.
Cancelling one factor leaves $\dfrac{3x-8}{x+2}$, which at $x=-2$ is $\dfrac{-14}{0}$.
As $x\to-2^-$ the denominator is negative so the quotient tends to $+\infty$, and as $x\to-2^+$ it is positive so the quotient tends to $-\infty$.
The one-sided limits differ, so the limit does not exist.

**(c)** Substituting gives $\dfrac{16}{0}$, which is not indeterminate.
As $x\to3^-$ the denominator $x-3$ is negative, giving $-\infty$, and as $x\to3^+$ it is positive, giving $+\infty$.
The one-sided limits differ, so the limit does not exist.

**(d)** Substituting gives $0/0$.
Multiplying by the conjugate of the numerator and the conjugate of the denominator turns the expression into $\dfrac{(2-x)\left(\sqrt{3-x}+1\right)}{(2-x)\left(\sqrt{6-x}+2\right)}$.
Cancelling $(2-x)$ and substituting $x=2$ gives $\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac12$.

**(e)** Substituting gives $0/0$.
Writing $x^2-x = x(x-1)$ and rearranging gives $\dfrac{\sin 3x}{3x}\cdot\dfrac{3}{x-1}$.
Since $\lim_{u\to0}\dfrac{\sin u}{u}=1$, the limit is $1\cdot\dfrac{3}{-1} = -3$.

Note what the DNE sentences have in common.
They name **both** one-sided limits and then say they differ.
"The limit does not exist because the denominator is zero" is not an explanation, because part d also has a zero denominator and its limit is $1/2$.

## 6. Verify before you commit

### The eight-second numeric check

Substitute $x = a \pm 0.001$ on the calculator.
Your answer has to show up.

| Part | Try | You should see | Reading |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| a | $x = 2.001$ | $9.010$ | settling on $9$ |
| b | $x=-2.001$ then $x=-1.999$ | about $+14000$ then $-14000$ | huge, **opposite signs**, so DNE |
| c | $x=2.999$ then $x=3.001$ | about $-16000$ then $+16000$ | huge, **opposite signs**, so DNE |
| d | $x=1.999$ | $0.50009$ | settling on $1/2$ |
| e | $x=0.001$ | $-3.003$ | settling on $-3$ |

Two rules for reading that column.

- If both sides are **huge with the same sign**, the answer is $+\infty$ or $-\infty$, not DNE.
- If both sides are **huge with opposite signs**, the answer is DNE.

That distinction is the single most valuable thing on this page, because it separates part b from a question like $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{\cos \pi x}{(x-2)^2}$, which looks identical and answers $+\infty$.

### Cheap invariants

- A $0/0$ limit that comes out as a **clean small fraction** is usually right, because these questions are constructed to.
  $1/2$, $2/3$, $3/8$, $-3$ are the shape of the answers.
  If you get $\dfrac{47}{113}$, you slipped.
- A conjugate step must make the root **disappear from that side**.
  If you still have a root where you were trying to remove one, you multiplied by the wrong thing.
- In $\dfrac{\sin(ax)}{bx}$ the answer is always $\dfrac{a}{b}$.
  If your answer does not look like a ratio of the two coefficients, check the manufacturing step.

### Numbers to memorise

$$\lim_{u\to0}\frac{\sin u}{u}=1 \qquad \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin ax}{bx}=\frac ab \qquad \lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos kx}{x^2}=\frac{k^2}{2}$$

## 7. Answer-entry rules

The exam is machine-marked by Möbius, which hides two different parsers behind one answer box, and nothing in the interface tells you which one you are in.
Grading is by mathematical equivalence, so a correct answer is never rejected for its *form*, only for being *parsed* differently than you intended.

| Rule | Do | Not |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Multiplication | `3*x` | `3x` |
| Exact fractions | `1/2` | `0.5` |
| Negatives | `-3` | `- 3` with a stray space in a list box |
| Roots | `sqrt(6)` | a root glyph, or `6^.5` |
| Whole denominators | `(a+b)/4` | `a+b/4` |
| Does not exist | `DNE` | `undefined`, `none`, an empty box |
| Infinite | `infinity`, `-infinity` | the $\infty$ glyph |

**The two entry decisions that are specific to a limits question.**

1. **DNE versus infinity.**
   These are different answers and Möbius marks them differently.
   Part b and part c here are DNE, because the sides disagree.
   A question like $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{\cos\pi x}{(x-2)^2}$ is $+\infty$, because they agree.
   Never type DNE just because the value is not finite.
2. **Read the question's own instruction.**
   Some Möbius limit items ask for a number and reject any word, and some accept `DNE`.
   Which one you are in is visible only from the prompt and the preview, so check both before you commit.
   This is the one rule on this page I cannot settle from the paper files, so settle it yourself on a practice item.

**The single most expensive typo on this question** is answering `-infinity` for part b.
That is what you get if you read the parity off $(x+2)^2$ without cancelling first, and it is a plausible-looking wrong answer, which is exactly why the examiner built the part that way.

**Always click the preview icon.**
An empty preview modal means broken syntax, not wrong mathematics.

## 8. The variation catalog

This section is the point of the document.
Read each table as **structure**, not as a list of facts to memorise.
In almost every family below there are only two or three distinct answer shapes, and the whole table is generated by changing one number.

### Family 0 - direct substitution

**Trigger:** you substitute and get a finite number.
**Tool:** none, you are done.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to2}(3x^2-2x+1)$ **(this is part a)** | $9$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-1}(x^3+4x^2-3)$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{2x^2-x+1}{x+3}$ | $\dfrac83$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\cos x}{1+\sin x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to4}\sqrt{x^2+9}$ | $5$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/6}\sin 2x$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{6x-9}{x^3-12x+3}$ **(decoy)** | $-3$ |

The last row is a real exam part, midterm 2 question 7c, and it is in this family on purpose.
It looks like a hard rational limit and it is a substitution: the denominator at $x=0$ is $3$, not $0$.
An answer of $0$ here, from a student who assumed the denominator vanished, is the most common way to lose a free mark on that paper.

**The structure:** notice that a limit is "hard" only when the denominator vanishes.
Nothing else makes it hard.
Check the denominator first and half the question disappears.

### Family 1 - $0/0$ rational, factor and cancel

**Trigger:** $0/0$, polynomial over polynomial.
**Tool:** factor both, cancel $(x-a)$, substitute again.

$$\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = \frac{(x-a)P_1(x)}{(x-a)Q_1(x)} \longrightarrow \frac{P_1(a)}{Q_1(a)}$$

| Limit | Factors as | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^3-1}$ | $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-1)(x^2+x+1)}$ | $\dfrac23$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ | $\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}$ | $6$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-2}\dfrac{x^2+x-2}{x+2}$ | $\dfrac{(x+2)(x-1)}{x+2}$ | $-3$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{x^3-8}{x-2}$ | $\dfrac{(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}{x-2}$ | $12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to4}\dfrac{x^2-5x+4}{x^2-16}$ | $\dfrac{(x-4)(x-1)}{(x-4)(x+4)}$ | $\dfrac38$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{(2+x)^3-8}{x}$ | expand, $\dfrac{x(12+6x+x^2)}{x}$ | $12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{x^4-1}{x-1}$ | $\dfrac{(x-1)(x^3+x^2+x+1)}{x-1}$ | $4$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-3}\dfrac{x^2+2x-3}{x^2-9}$ | $\dfrac{(x+3)(x-1)}{(x+3)(x-3)}$ | $\dfrac23$ |

The first row is midterm 2, question 7a.
Row 6 is worth a second look: $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{(2+x)^3-8}{x} = 12$ is exactly the derivative of $x^3$ at $x=2$, written as a limit.
When a limit has the shape $\frac{f(a+x)-f(a)}{x}$, differentiating $f$ is faster than expanding, and midterm 1 question 8 asks for precisely that.

### Family 2 - $0/0$ with radicals, conjugate

**Trigger:** $0/0$ and there is a square root.
**Tool:** multiply by the conjugate of whichever side holds the root, both sides if both hold one.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{\sqrt{6-x}-2}{\sqrt{3-x}-1}$ **(this is part d)** | $\dfrac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sqrt{x+9}-3}{x}$ | $\dfrac16$ |
| $\lim_{x\to4}\dfrac{\sqrt x-2}{x-4}$ | $\dfrac14$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{\sqrt x-1}{x-1}$ | $\dfrac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sqrt{1+x}-\sqrt{1-x}}{x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{x-3}{\sqrt{x+1}-2}$ | $4$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sqrt{4+x}-2}{\sqrt{9+x}-3}$ | $\dfrac32$ |

**The structure:** every one of these answers is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{A}}$ or a ratio of two such things, where $A$ is what is under the root at the limit point.
Row 2, under the root is $9$, answer $\frac{1}{2\cdot3}=\frac16$.
Row 3, under the root is $4$, answer $\frac{1}{2\cdot2}=\frac14$.
Row 7 divides the two: $\dfrac{1/(2\cdot2)}{1/(2\cdot3)} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
Row 6 is row 3 upside down, so it is $4$.
That is one rule generating seven answers, and it is also why these are secretly derivative questions: $\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt x = \frac{1}{2\sqrt x}$.

### Family 3 - nonzero over zero, sign analysis

**Trigger:** you substitute and get $c/0$ with $c \neq 0$.
**Tool:** sign chart. Never factoring.

| Limit | Numerator at $a$ | Power left | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{2x^2-x+1}{x-3}$ **(this is part c)** | $+16$ | odd | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{\cos\pi x}{(x-2)^2}$ | $+1$ | even | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ | $+1$ | even | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{x+2}{(x-1)^2}$ | $+3$ | even | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x-3}{x^2}$ | $-3$ | even | $-\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-1}\dfrac{x^2+1}{x+1}$ | $+2$ | odd | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to-\pi/3}\dfrac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}$ | $+\sqrt3$ | odd | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |

Row 2 is midterm 2 question 7f, and row 7 is midterm 2 question 7e.
Row 7 deserves its own warning: it *looks* like a trigonometric limit needing a substitution, and it is not.
Substitute first and you find $\tan(-2\pi/3) = \sqrt3 \neq 0$, so the numerator never vanishes and there is no $0/0$ to resolve.
It is this family, in disguise.

**The structure:** there are only three possible answers in this whole family, $+\infty$, $-\infty$, and DNE, and they are chosen by exactly two bits.
Odd power, always DNE, regardless of the numerator.
Even power, the sign of the numerator decides between $+\infty$ and $-\infty$.
Seven rows, two bits.

### Family 4 - the hybrid: $0/0$ that collapses to nonzero over zero

**Trigger:** it was $0/0$, you cancelled, and the denominator *still* vanishes.
**Tool:** Family 1 followed immediately by Family 3.

This is the family part b belongs to and it is the one that separates grades.

| Limit | Cancels to | Power left | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to-2}\dfrac{3x^2-2x-16}{(x+2)^2}$ **(this is part b)** | $\dfrac{3x-8}{x+2}$ | odd | **DNE** ($+\infty$, $-\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{x^2-1}{(x-1)^3}$ | $\dfrac{x+1}{(x-1)^2}$ | even | $+\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{x^2-4}{(x-2)^2}$ | $\dfrac{x+2}{x-2}$ | odd | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x^2-3x}{x^3}$ | $\dfrac{x-3}{x^2}$ | even | $-\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{x^2-9}{(x-3)^2}$ | $\dfrac{x+3}{x-3}$ | odd | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |

**The structure, and the reason this family exists:**
the power of $(x-a)$ you were *handed* in the denominator is not the power that decides the answer.
Only the power **left after cancelling** decides.
Row 1 was handed a square and answers DNE.
Row 2 was handed a cube and answers $+\infty$.
Every intuition that reads the parity off the printed expression gets both of those backwards.

Compute it as one subtraction.
If the numerator carries $(x-a)^m$ and the denominator carries $(x-a)^n$, the leftover power is $n-m$, and that is the only integer you need.

### Family 5 - the $\sin(kx)/x$ special limit

**Trigger:** $0/0$ and there is a sine or a tangent going to zero.
**Tool:** manufacture the matching denominator so that $\frac{\sin u}{u}$ appears with the *same* $u$ top and bottom.

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x^2-x}$ **(this is part e)** | $-3$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 5x}{x}$ | $5$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 3x}{\sin 7x}$ | $\dfrac37$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\tan 4x}{x}$ | $4$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 2x}{3x^2+5x}$ | $\dfrac25$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x}{\sin x}$ | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin(x^2)}{x}$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 4x}{x\cos x}$ | $4$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x^2}$ | **DNE** |

**The structure:** every finite answer here is the ratio of two coefficients.
$\frac{\sin ax}{bx} \to \frac ab$, and $\frac{\sin ax}{\sin bx} \to \frac ab$, and $\tan$ behaves exactly like $\sin$.
For row 5, factor the denominator to $x(3x+5)$ and the $5$ is the coefficient that survives, giving $\frac25$.
For row 1, the factoring gives $x(x-1)$ and the surviving coefficient is $-1$, giving $\frac{3}{-1}=-3$.
Nine rows, one ratio rule.

The two rows that break the pattern are the two you should study.
Row 7: $\sin(x^2)$ pairs with $x^2$, and there is only one $x$ downstairs, so one $x$ survives on top and pushes the limit to $0$.
Row 9 is the same imbalance the other way: one $x$ survives *downstairs*, so it becomes $\frac{3}{x}$ and blows up in opposite directions, giving DNE.
Count the powers of $x$ before you compute anything.

### Family 6 - the $1-\cos$ family

**Trigger:** $0/0$ and a $1-\cos$ appears.
**Tool:** either the conjugate $(1+\cos u)$, or the standard result directly.

$$\lim_{u\to0}\frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}=\frac12$$

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{x^2}{1-\cos 2x}$ | $\dfrac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1-\cos x}{x}$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1-\cos x}{x^2}$ | $\dfrac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1-\cos 3x}{x^2}$ | $\dfrac92$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\cos x-1}{x\sin x}$ | $-\dfrac12$ |

Row 1 is midterm 2, question 7b, and it is the reciprocal case: $\frac{1-\cos 2x}{x^2}\to\frac{2^2}{2}=2$, so its reciprocal is $\frac12$.

**The structure:** one formula generates the whole table.

$$\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos kx}{x^2}=\frac{k^2}{2}$$

Row 3 is $k=1$, row 4 is $k=3$ giving $\frac92$, row 1 is $k=2$ inverted giving $\frac12$.
Row 2 has only one power of $x$ downstairs, so a factor of $x$ survives on top and it goes to $0$.
Row 5 uses $x\sin x \approx x^2$ and carries the minus sign from the reversed order.

If you would rather derive than memorise, the conjugate does it:

$$\frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}\cdot\frac{1+\cos u}{1+\cos u} = \frac{\sin^2 u}{u^2(1+\cos u)} = \left(\frac{\sin u}{u}\right)^2\cdot\frac{1}{1+\cos u} \to 1\cdot\frac12$$

### Family 7 - trigonometric limit away from zero, shift the variable

**Trigger:** $0/0$ with a trig function, but $x \to a$ where $a \neq 0$.
**Tool:** substitute $u = x - a$ so that $u \to 0$, then use the identity to re-express the trig function.

| Limit | Shift | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\dfrac{\cos x}{x-\pi/2}$ | $u=x-\frac\pi2$, $\cos(u+\frac\pi2)=-\sin u$ | $-1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi}\dfrac{\sin x}{x-\pi}$ | $u=x-\pi$, $\sin(u+\pi)=-\sin u$ | $-1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{\sin(x-1)}{x^2-1}$ | $u=x-1$, denominator $u(u+2)$ | $\dfrac12$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/4}\dfrac{1-\tan x}{\cos x-\sin x}$ | write $1-\tan x = \frac{\cos x - \sin x}{\cos x}$ | $\sqrt2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{\sin(x-2)}{x^2-4}$ | $u=x-2$, denominator $u(u+4)$ | $\dfrac14$ |

Rows 3 and 5 are the easy shape, where the argument of the sine is already $x-a$: the sine contributes $1$ and the answer is whatever is left in the denominator, $\frac{1}{a+a}$.
Rows 1 and 2 need a shift identity, and both produce $-1$ because both shifts introduce the same sign flip.
Row 4 is not really a shift at all: it is an algebraic identity that cancels the whole thing, and it is worth knowing because it costs one line.

**The trap this family sets** is that a limit at a non-zero point with a trig function does not have to be $0/0$.
Always substitute first.
The midterm 2 part $\lim_{x\to-\pi/3}\frac{\tan 2x}{3x+\pi}$ looks exactly like this family and belongs to Family 3.

### Family 8 - limits at infinity

**Trigger:** $x \to \infty$ or $x \to -\infty$.
**Tool:** divide top and bottom by the highest power of $x$ in the **denominator**, then use $\frac{1}{x^n}\to0$.

| Limit | Degrees | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{5-2x^3}{x^2+2}$ | top bigger | $-\infty$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{3x^2+1}{2x^2-x}$ | equal | $\dfrac32$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{x+1}{x^3-2}$ | bottom bigger | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\dfrac{4x^3-x}{2x^3+5}$ | equal | $2$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}$ | equal | $1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to-\infty}\dfrac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}$ | equal | $-1$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2+3x}-x\right)$ | $\infty-\infty$ | $\dfrac32$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ | bounded over $\infty$ | $0$ |

Row 1 is midterm 2, question 7d.

**The structure:** for a ratio of polynomials there are only three answers, decided by comparing degrees.
Bottom wins, the answer is $0$.
Degrees are equal, the answer is the ratio of the leading coefficients.
Top wins, the answer is $\pm\infty$.
You never need to do the division, you only need to compare two integers and then read two coefficients.

Rows 5 and 6 are the exception you must know, and they differ only in the direction of approach.
$\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$, which is $x$ when $x\to+\infty$ and $-x$ when $x\to-\infty$.
Forgetting the absolute value is how a correct method gives the wrong sign.
Row 7 is the $\infty-\infty$ form, and it is handled by the conjugate: multiply by $\frac{\sqrt{x^2+3x}+x}{\sqrt{x^2+3x}+x}$ to get $\frac{3x}{\sqrt{x^2+3x}+x}$, then divide by $x$ to get $\frac{3}{2}$.

### Family 9 - the squeeze theorem

**Trigger:** something **bounded** (a sine or cosine of anything) multiplied by something going to zero.
**Tool:** bound the oscillating factor between $-1$ and $1$, then squeeze.

$$-\lvert g(x)\rvert \le g(x)\sin(\text{anything}) \le \lvert g(x)\rvert, \quad\text{and if } g\to0 \text{ then the middle} \to 0$$

| Limit | Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\dfrac{3x^2-4}{4+2x+2x^4}\right)\sin x$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}x^2\sin\dfrac1x$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}x\cos\dfrac1x$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\cos x}{x}$ | $0$ |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\sin\dfrac1x$ | **DNE** |

Row 1 is final exam 2, question 1a.
Its rational factor is degree $2$ over degree $4$, so it tends to $0$ by Family 8, and $\sin x$ never leaves $[-1,1]$, so the product is squeezed to $0$.

**The structure:** every squeeze answer in this course is $0$.
That is not a coincidence, it is the only thing the squeeze theorem is used for at this level.
The real work is spotting *which* factor is bounded and *which* one dies.
Row 5 is the counterexample that makes the point: there is no dying factor, only the oscillation, so nothing is squeezed and the limit does not exist.
"Oscillating" alone does not give $0$, you need something multiplying it that goes to zero.

### Family 10 - tangent, cotangent, secant, cosecant at an asymptote

**Trigger:** a limit of $\tan$, $\cot$, $\sec$ or $\csc$ at a point where it is undefined.
**Tool:** rewrite as a fraction and do a Family 3 sign chart on the denominator.

| Limit | Rewrite | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to5\pi/2}\tan x$ | $\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$, $\cos\to0$, $\sin\to1$ | **DNE** ($+\infty$, $-\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\tan x$ | same | **DNE** ($+\infty$, $-\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\cot x$ | $\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$ | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\csc x$ | $\dfrac{1}{\sin x}$ | **DNE** ($-\infty$, $+\infty$) |
| $\lim_{x\to\pi/2}\sec x$ | $\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$ | **DNE** ($+\infty$, $-\infty$) |

Row 1 is final exam 2, question 1b.
$\frac{5\pi}{2}$ is $\frac{\pi}{2} + 2\pi$, so it is the same picture as row 2 shifted by a full period, which is the entire content of that question.

**The structure:** all five answers are DNE, and for the same reason every time.
$\sin$ and $\cos$ each cross zero *transversally*, meaning they change sign as they pass through, so the denominator is an odd-power zero and the sides always disagree.
There is no way to get $+\infty$ from a bare tangent or cotangent at an asymptote.
If a question asks for a one-sided version, such as $\lim_{x\to(\pi/2)^-}\tan x$, then and only then do you answer $+\infty$.

### Family 11 - one-sided, piecewise, and absolute value

**Trigger:** an absolute value, a piecewise definition, or a domain that stops at the limit point.
**Tool:** compute the two one-sided limits separately and compare.

| Limit | Left | Right | Answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\lvert x\rvert}{x}$ | $-1$ | $+1$ | **DNE** |
| $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{\lvert x-3\rvert}{x-3}$ | $-1$ | $+1$ | **DNE** |
| $\lim_{x\to2}f(x)$, $f = x^2$ for $x<2$, $3x-2$ for $x\ge2$ | $4$ | $4$ | $4$ |
| $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{\lvert x-2\rvert(x+1)}{x-2}$ | $-3$ | $+3$ | **DNE** |
| $\lim_{x\to0}\sqrt x$ | undefined | $0$ | DNE two-sided, $0$ from the right |

**The structure:** an absolute value divided by its own inside is always a $\pm1$ switch, so any limit of that shape at the switching point is DNE, and any limit of that shape *away* from the switching point is trivial.
Row 3 is the case that exists to show you the answer can be a plain number: piecewise does not mean DNE, it means *check*.
Row 5 is the domain case, and it matters for the curve-sketching question, where a function can have a perfectly good one-sided limit at the edge of its domain.

## 9. The trap list

Ranked by how often each one actually costs marks on this question.

1. **Reading the parity off the printed denominator instead of the cancelled one.**
   This is part b, and it turns DNE into $-\infty$.
   Cancel first, then count.
2. **Trying to factor a $c/0$.**
   This is part c, and it costs a minute rather than a mark, which on a 16-point question with five parts is still expensive.
   Substitute first, always.
3. **Answering DNE for every infinite limit.**
   $+\infty$ and DNE are different answers.
   Sides agree, name the infinity.
   Sides disagree, DNE.
4. **Losing the minus sign in part e.**
   $x-1 \to -1$, not $1$.
   The answer is $-3$, and $+3$ is the most common wrong number on that part.
5. **Not manufacturing the matching argument.**
   $\frac{\sin 3x}{x}$ is not $1$, it is $3$.
   The $u$ on the top and the $u$ on the bottom must be the same expression.
6. **Conjugating only one side when both sides have roots.**
   You end up at $0/0$ again and think you made an error.
   You did not, you are half done.
7. **Writing "DNE because the denominator is zero".**
   Part d also has a zero denominator and its limit is $1/2$.
   The explanation has to name both one-sided limits.
8. **Forgetting $\sqrt{x^2}=\lvert x\rvert$ as $x\to-\infty$.**
   Family 8, rows 5 and 6, where the same expression has two different answers.
9. **Using L'Hopital on midterm 1.**
   It gets the right number and it is out of scope, so on a written paper it earns nothing.
   Use it only as a private check.
10. **Assuming an oscillating factor forces the limit to zero.**
    It only does if something multiplying it goes to zero.
    $\sin(1/x)$ on its own is DNE.
